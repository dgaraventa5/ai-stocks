# Democrats Win Subpoenas; States Control the Power

Democrats won both chambers on 2026-11-03, but under a Trump veto their federal agenda for AI comes down to investigations, blocking, and a few bipartisan bills. The rules that actually bind AI infrastructure through 2028 will be set by state utility commissions, governors and local ballots, plus the executive agencies Democrats do not run (FERC, Commerce/BIS). The first Democratic targets are already named. They are Nvidia's China export approvals, now entangled with Jensen Huang's resignation. Next come OpenAI's "circular" financing and acqui-hire deals, and making data centers pay the full cost of the grid upgrades they need. A federal data-center moratorium and federal AI preemption are both effectively dead (high confidence). Three things can still become law: chip-export mandates riding the must-pass FY2028 NDAA, data-center cost allocation riding a bipartisan permitting bill, and child-safety chatbot rules (moderate confidence). Demand sits against all of this. Calendar-Q3 2026 capex at the five largest buyers was about **$230B, up 118% YoY**, and every 2027 statement points higher. Suppliers of memory, turbines and transformers say they are sold out into 2029–30. Demand's weak point is financing, not politics: the buyers are running negative free cash flow and borrowing at rising coupons. The sharpest Democratic oversight, from Warren at Senate Banking, is aimed at exactly that financing chain. So the core thesis holds: federal power yields headlines, binding constraints move to the states, and demand remains strong. It needs three qualifications. Chip exports are a bipartisan exception with a real path to law. The federal executive, not Congress, still holds the strongest federal levers. And political noise matters most where it could tighten the financing that demand depends on.

## Nvidia and chip exports: the one bipartisan front with a path to law

**Nvidia is the first and most certain oversight target (high confidence), and this is not a party-line fight.** On 2026-11-12, Wyden and Neal, the incoming Finance and Ways & Means chairs, opened an investigation into the H200 "scheme to approve and tax exports." They called the 25% Section 232 levy an unconstitutional export tax. They also alleged Trump approved the exports after Nvidia directed at least $26 million to his priorities, such as the White House ballroom, and they ordered Commerce to preserve records ([Wyden–Neal letter](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/11122026wydenneallettertolutnickonnvidia.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/democrats-launch-nvidia-investigation-write-scathing-letter-on-huangs-scheme-with-trump/)).

The Republicans are just as active. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott (R) requested testimony from Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick **by 2026-12-03**. Select Committee Chair Moolenaar has written repeatedly attacking the H200 decision. Majority Leader Thune said oversight continues even after Huang's exit ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/democrats-react-nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-china-spy-scandal-12708310); [Select Committee letter](https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/selectcommitteeontheccp.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/select-committee-h200-letter-to-lutnick-december-2025.pdf)). Treasury Secretary Bessent said agencies back adding Nvidia customer Megaspeed to the entity list ([The Standard/Reuters](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/348472/Bessent-blames-Commerce-Dept-over-lack-of-export-controls-for-Nvidia-partner-Megaspeed)). Megaspeed is a Singapore data-center firm under US criminal investigation for alleged smuggling, and a Megaspeed executive is reported to have had an affair with Huang ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns/)).

**The Huang date conflict (Nov 11 vs Nov 12) resolves cleanly: both dates are correct.** Nvidia's 8-K under Item 5.02, filed 2026-11-12, states the resignation was **effective 2026-11-11**. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones is chair (repo `per-stock/NVDA/news-log.md`, citing [CNBC live](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html)). The board announced it, and the press reported it, on Thursday 11-12. Use 11-11 for governance records and 11-12 for market-reaction windows.

A related figure needs the same care. The "about −25%" NVDA decline in one note measures from $217.94 on 10-27 to about $163 on 11-12. That window spans the pre-election AI selloff, the Nov 5 crash and the resignation. The resignation week by itself was about **−12.75% to −13%** ([MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/wall-street-closes-mixed-as-nvidia-and-amd-sink-nasdaq-still-posts-its-best-week-since-august/)).

**The financial exposure is smaller than the headlines.** Nvidia's China revenue, including Hong Kong, ran about **5.6–8.2% of total** in fiscal-2027 quarters. China Hopper data-center shipments were under 1% of data-center revenue, and the Q3 guide of **$108B ±2%** already assumes zero China data-center compute ([NVDA 10-Q Q2 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm); [NVDA Q2 FY27 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm)). About 600,000 H200s were reportedly stuck in Hong Kong awaiting **Beijing's** approval, not Washington's ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-h200-chips-hong-kong-china-import-approval)). AMD's licensed China AI GPU revenue has already run off to "not material" ([AMD 10-Q Q1 FY26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000002488/000000248826000076/amd-20260328.htm)).

Nvidia's real risks are therefore three. It could lose optional H200 China upside. It faces compliance and penalty exposure if diversion through Megaspeed is proven. And it faces a governance discount during a CEO search, with discovery of internal compliance documents as the tail risk (moderate confidence).

**The binding path for Congress is the NDAA, not standalone bills.** The GAIN AI Act passed the Senate as part of the FY2026 NDAA, 77–20, *under Republican control*, then died in conference ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2025/10/ai-export-control-bill-passes-senate-ndaa-amendment/408762/)). The FY2027 NDAA conference text reportedly drops GAIN AI, the Chip Security Act and the AI OVERWATCH Act again. That report is low-to-moderate confidence: one secondary source, not checked against the text ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/nvidia-chip-restrictions-unlikely-final-ndaa/)).

In calendar 2027, Democrats will control both chambers' conferees on the **FY2028 NDAA**, and NDAAs are rarely vetoed over a single provision. That makes chip-location verification, a GAIN-style domestic-priority rule, or a congressional review window on licenses (the Overwatch model) the most plausible *enacted* AI constraint of the 120th Congress (moderate confidence). Law firms add the **MATCH Act** (H.R.8170/S.4281), which targets semiconductor manufacturing equipment and seeks equivalent restrictions from allies ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-states/government-public-sector/a-political-shakeup-key-takeaways-from-the-2026-midterms)).

That bill is where the dollars are. AMAT's China share was about 25% of Q4 FY26 revenue. Lam's is reported as under 30% and KLAC's around 33%. All of these are low-to-moderate-confidence secondary figures that need checking against the 10-Ks ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/26/applied-materials-china-problem-is-getting-worse/); [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4507247-lam-research-anticipates-china-revenue-below-30-percent-in-2026-as-ai-drives-record-5b)). In April 2026 the administration itself ordered equipment makers to stop shipping to China's second-largest chipmaker ([InvestingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/us-orders-chip-equipment-companies-to-stop-shipments-to-chinas-second-largest-chipmaker-20260428/)). That is a reminder that the executive branch, not Congress, delivers the binding export shocks.

**Tariffs will see oversight more than repeal.** The Supreme Court ended the IEEPA tariffs 6–3 on 2026-02-20, but it left Section 232 untouched ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/02/the-supreme-court-ends-ieepa-tariffs)). That includes Proclamation 11002's 25% tariff on certain advanced chips. Schumer's S. 5390 repeals Sections 122 and 338 and keeps 232 ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/11/what-every-multinational-should-know-about-the-impact-of-the-2026-u-s-midterm-elections-on-u-s-trade-policy/)). Jeffries' promised 100-day tariff vote starts with Canada and lacks veto-proof support ([mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/democrats-plan-a-first-100-hours-vote-to-curb-trumps-tariffs-starting-with-canada-but-lack-the-votes-to-beat-a-veto/)).

The chip 232 tariff mostly works as the toll on China-bound H200 and MI-series exports. So the Wyden–Neal "export tax" theory threatens the pay-to-export model, not domestic AI demand. The CHIPS-equity conversions are criticized as state capitalism but face no named Democratic bill. These include Intel's 9.9% stake and stakes reportedly held in 41 companies ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/19/lutnick-intel-stock-chips-trump.html); [mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/the-us-government-now-holds-stakes-in-41-companies-what-a-democratic-congress-can-do-about-it/)). For INTC, expect governance noise, not cash impact (low-to-moderate confidence).

## AI regulation and Big Tech: hearings now, laws only where Republicans co-sign

**Federal preemption of state AI laws is dead for this Congress (high confidence).** It failed twice under unified Republican control, including the 99–1 Senate vote stripping the reconciliation moratorium in July 2025 ([Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/?p=15667)). Pallone and Cantwell will not advance an industry-terms version, and Cantwell's stated direction is guardrails and independent model testing ([Senate Commerce](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/dem/release/ranking-member-cantwell-floor-speech-on-ai-risks-and-the-need-for-strong-federal-guardrails/)). State law therefore remains the binding regulatory layer for model developers: California SB 53, the Colorado AI Act, New York's RAISE Act. The fight over those laws moves to the courts and to funding fights over Trump's December 2025 executive order and its DOJ AI Litigation Task Force ([Axios](https://axios.com/2025/12/11/trump-signs-executive-order-state-ai-laws)).

One caveat cuts the other way. Jeffries' AI commission co-chair Ted Lieu says the goal is "a federal AI standard," so a Democratic-written floor with limited preemption is not impossible ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/08/democrats-jeffries-ai-trump)).

**The bills that can become law are the bipartisan ones.** The **GUARD Act** (Hawley R / Blumenthal D) bans AI companions for minors and mandates age verification. It cleared Senate Judiciary unanimously on 2026-04-30 and is the furthest-advanced AI bill ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/ban-on-kids-companion-chatbots-advanced-by-senate-committee/)). Two disclosure-style bills sit in the same tier:

- the Grassley–Coons AI Whistleblower Protection Act ([Coons](https://www.coons.senate.gov/news/press-releases/senators-coons-grassley-introduce-ai-whistleblower-protection-act))
- the Hawley–Warner AI layoff-disclosure bill ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/05/hawley-warner-ai-jobs-bill))

All three are cheap to sign and politically hard to veto (moderate confidence).

The economically dangerous bills face a veto or filibuster and work mainly as hearing vehicles and templates for states (moderate-to-high confidence):

- the Hawley–Blumenthal **copyright/personal-data tort**, which would effectively negate fair use for training ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/07/22/hawley-blumenthal-bill-aims-rein-ai-companies-use-copyrighted-works/))
- the Durbin–Hawley **AI LEAD Act** product-liability regime ([Durbin](https://www.durbin.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/durbin-hawley-introduce-bill-allowing-victims-to-sue-ai-companies))
- the Hawley–Blumenthal compute-threshold **AI Risk Evaluation Act** ([Hawley](https://www.hawley.senate.gov/hawley-blumenthal-introduce-bipartisan-ai-evaluation-legislation-to-put-americans-first))
- Warner's frontier pre-release access regime ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/from-voluntary-engagement-to-mandatory-access-senator-warner-proposes-new-rules-for-frontier-ai-models-and-data-centers))

The Warner regime has an unresolved conflict: a 21-day pre-release access window in S. 5061 versus 45 days in an October version. Evercore's pre-election view fits this picture: little federal progress unless a major AI-safety incident changes the politics ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/midterm-sweep-democrats-could-turn-104900248.html)).

**The antitrust and financing probes matter more for markets than any AI bill.** Several investigations can escalate from letters to subpoenas once Warren chairs Banking and Democrats chair Judiciary:

- Warren, Wyden and Blumenthal pressed FTC and DOJ on acqui-hires worth more than $36.7B, including Nvidia–Groq ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/04/sen-warren-others-urge-ftc-doj-to-scrutinize-tech-acquihire-deals.html)). DOJ reportedly opened a formal Groq probe in September; the deal is reported at both $17B and $20B, and the probe report is low-to-moderate confidence ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327154/20260910/doj-opens-formal-probe-nvidia-groq-deal-reverse-acquihire-playbook-faces-first-legal-test.htm)).
- Warren pressed OpenAI on its roughly **$1.4T of commitments** made through "complex and often circular partnerships," and on any federal backstop ([Warren](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-presses-openai-ceo-on-spending-commitments-and-bailout-requests-after-cfo-suggests-government-backstop)).
- Markey promised that Big Tech executives would "need to plant new forests for all of the subpoenas," and Casar wants AI CEOs under oath ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/casar-markey-put-ai-executives-174945782.html)).

The 2019–20 precedent says to expect headlines, not law. House Judiciary's 16-month probe produced a 450-page report recommending structural separations, and none of it was enacted ([House Judiciary Democrats](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/judiciary-antitrust-subcommittee-investigation-reveals-digital-economy-highly-concentrated-impacted-by-monopoly-power)). The stocks mostly *rose* the day after the report ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/briefing/democratic-wave-could-lead-to-sweeping-big-tech-regulation-analysts/)). The lasting damage came later, through agency lawsuits fed by discovered documents.

The 2027 risk is modestly higher than in 2019, for two reasons. Democrats now hold the Senate's subpoena power too. And Republicans such as Hawley, Grassley and Tim Scott co-lead many of the same efforts.

## "Make them pay," not "stop them": power policy moves to cost allocation

**Democrats' federal data-center position is cost causation, not prohibition (moderate-to-high confidence).** The clearest evidence is the **Ratepayer Protection Act**, whose Senate vote is easy to misread.

It passed the House **417–3** in September 2026, with 206 Democrats voting yes. In the Senate it got **57 yes votes and 43 no votes** on cloture on 2026-09-30. Fifty-seven was a majority but short of the 60 needed to end debate. The arithmetic matches 53 Republicans plus four Democrats (Hassan, Klobuchar, Ossoff, Warnock) voting yes. Calling it "57–43 against" is therefore wrong. Senate Democrats filibustered it because it only required states to *consider* making large loads pay, which Schumer called "toothless" ([Valley News Live](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/09/30/senate-democrats-block-bill-shield-ratepayers-data-center-costs-57-43-vote/); [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/ai-data-center-bill-democrats-voting-republicans-list-12509123); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/house-passes-ratepayer-protection-bill-data-centers/830658/); [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/politics/democrats-block-data-center-electricity-bill-calling-it-toothless/)).

The Democratic alternative is Heinrich's **GRID Savings Act** (S. 5199). It would give FERC jurisdiction over interconnecting loads above 150 MW, assign the interconnection costs to the customer, and require a payment guarantee that survives the facility's closure ([NACo](https://www.naco.org/news/senate-energy-and-natural-resources-ranking-member-introduces-legislation-protect-ratepayers)). Schiff has a separate bill along the same lines. Schumer still calls data centers "important" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bloomberg-government-news/schumer-urges-more-clean-energy-to-fuel-important-data-centers)).

Thresholds are inconsistent across sources and low-to-moderate confidence: 100 MW versus 150 MW for the federal bills, and 50 MW versus 20 MW for FERC.

**The Sanders–Ocasio-Cortez moratorium has no path, but Hawley's support for a pause is a warning sign.** The bill would halt builds above 20 MW ([Sanders](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-ocasio-cortez-announce-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/)). Its cosponsor count conflicts: about 20 Democrats per one source, zero Senate cosponsors on S.4214 per another ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-hawley-backs-data-center-pause-senate-bill-has-0-cosponsors/)). Jeffries says a pause is "certainly not a position" he has articulated ([mlq.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/democrats-data-center-moratorium-push-gains-momentum-after-midterms-but-congress-remains-a-long-shot/)), and Goldman calls a national moratorium unlikely under any outcome ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/democratic-sweep-midterms-could-rattle-194401634.html)).

The signal to watch is on the right. Hawley became the first Republican senator to back a nationwide pause until rate protections exist ([The National Desk](https://thenationaldesk.com/news/connect-to-congress/hawley-backs-data-center-moratorium-as-washington-weighs-new-restrictions-artificial-intelligence-midterm-elections-electricity-water-usage)). A populist left-right coalition could harden *cost* terms beyond what industry expects.

**The permitting deal is the most likely energy law, and its status is the least settled fact in the notes.** Capito, Lee, Whitehouse and Heinrich struck a bipartisan Senate deal around 2026-09-30. It expands federal transmission-permitting authority and makes data centers cover the related grid-upgrade costs ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/senate-reaches-bipartisan-deal-on-permitting-bill-to-speed-approvals-of-new-energy-projects)).

Right after the election, Republican aides doubted it could pass before Christmas, with one saying "I'll be surprised," ahead of a procedural vote scheduled for November 9 ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/6202573-gop-loses-hope-trump-agenda/)). A later single source reports that the procedural vote **cleared 73–25**, with Democrats split 22–24, and that a final vote could come the following week ([MS NOW](https://ms.now/news/democrats-early-test-compromise-cost-of-living-permitting-reform)).

The two reports are compatible. A supermajority on the procedural vote supersedes the earlier pessimism about getting to the floor, but final passage, House action and Trump's signature remain open. The House passed only the narrower SPEED Act, 221–196, in December 2025. Treat lame-duck enactment as low-to-moderate likelihood.

If the deal slips into 2027, the Democratic Senate's leverage rises and the data-center-pays terms likely tighten. Either way the result is positive for transmission and grid equipment, and it formalizes higher costs for developers.

**The levers that bind sit in agencies and states Democrats do not control in Congress.** FERC has a 3–2 Republican majority, using commonly reported party labels (moderate confidence). It missed DOE's April 2026 deadline for a large-load interconnection rule and issued show-cause orders to the six RTOs on 2026-06-18 instead ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/ferc_acts_advance_data_center_large_load_integration_six_rto_regions_0626.html)). Restoring the IRA credits is Schumer's stated priority, but it has effectively no chance before 2029 under a veto ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/top-democrat-promises-to-restore-clean-energy-payments-in-2027)). Wind and solar therefore still face the OBBBA placed-in-service cliff after 2027-12-31, which keeps the marginal data-center megawatt tilted toward gas, nuclear uprates and restarts, and storage (moderate confidence).

The state-level changes are concrete:

- **Georgia.** The Public Service Commission flipped to a **3–2 Democratic majority**, seated in January ([Georgia Recorder](https://georgiarecorder.com/2026/11/04/democrats-gain-control-of-georgia-board-that-regulates-utilities-for-first-time-in-decades/)). It will review the roughly 10 GW Georgia Power plan approved in December 2025, about 80% of it for data centers, plus up to 6 GW more that is pending, through an "affordability docket" ([WALB](https://www.walb.com/2026/11/06/election-may-bring-reckoning-data-centers-georgia/); [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/politics/new-dem-regulators-georgia-power)). The PSC does not control where data centers locate.
- **Texas.** Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa ran on extending the current pause into a moratorium on new approvals, plus ending the sales-tax exemption and imposing a rate freeze ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/navigating-new-headwinds-what-the-2026-election-results-mean-for-data-center-projects-in-texas)). The tax and market-design pieces need the Legislature ([Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/energy-policy/hinojosa-can-pause-data-centers-she-cant-rewrite-texas-power-alone/)).
- **Ohio.** Governor-elect Acton wants a conditional moratorium. AEP Ohio's tariff already requires data centers above 25 MW to pay for at least 85% of subscribed capacity for up to 12 years.
- **New Jersey.** Gov. Sherrill signed a separate rate class for data centers of 50 MW and up ([NJ Monitor](https://newjerseymonitor.com/2026/07/07/sherrill-signs-three-bills-to-lower-nj-electricity-costs/)).
- **Local ballots.** Voters rejected development in roughly 37 of 38 local data-center measures ([Time News](https://time.news/voters-reject-data-center-expansions-in-dozens-of-us-local-and-state-level-races/)).

AP reports that 32 of 34 called gubernatorial winners support tighter data-center rules ([Inquirer/AP](https://usa.inquirer.net/215747/ai-data-centers-took-a-beating-in-the-midterm-elections)).

**This state constraint was not created by Democrats in November; the election ratified and accelerated it.** Texas's SB6 cost-sharing law and Gov. Abbott's reported ERCOT energization pause for loads of 75 MW and up came from Republicans, though the pause rests on a single source and is low confidence. Data Center Watch counted about **120 projects worth roughly $198B blocked or delayed in H1 2026**, before any votes were cast ([Data Center Watch](https://www.datacenterwatch.org/q1-2026); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2026/11/us-data-center-projects-face-198.html)).

The economic driver is visible in PJM. The 2028/29 capacity auction cleared at its cap and **6.8 GW short** of the reliability requirement ([Times Leader](https://www.timesleader.com/news/1749209/pjms-2028-2029-capacity-auction-hits-price-caps-again-short-6-8-gigawatts-of-reliability-requirement)). PJM's market monitor now attributes **$29.4B** of capacity costs to data-center load and recommends postponing the December 2026 auction. That figure is moderate confidence, seen via secondary coverage ([Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/electrical-generation/pjms-monitor-put-29-4-billion-on-data-center-load-the-policy-fight-is-who-pays-next/)).

How much this mattered to voters is contested. An NBC exit poll put AI and data centers as a "crucial or major" factor for 56% of voters. AP's voter poll found only about a quarter calling them "crucial" ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/casar-markey-put-ai-executives-174945782.html); [Texas Politics/AP](https://texaspolitics.com/2026/11/06/ai-data-centers-took-a-beating-in-the-midterm-elections-a-reckoning-may-be-ahead-for-tech-giants/)). The two measure different thresholds, and both read the issue as **broad but secondary**, which makes it a cheap target for politicians of both parties.

## Demand doubled; financing, not Congress, is the soft spot

**The demand side shows no political dent (high confidence).** Calendar-Q3 2026 cash capex at AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT, META and ORCL totaled **$230.4B, +118% YoY**. Amazon alone spent $77.3B and Alphabet $51.4B (10-Q XBRL in `tracking/hyperscaler-capex.xlsx`; [GOOGL 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)). Big-4 2026 guidance is about **$720–740B**, and the Street's 2027 estimate is about **$935B** (low-to-moderate confidence; third-party) ([yieldtheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast)).

Contracted backlog shows the spending is not speculative:

- Microsoft commercial RPO: **$677B, +73%**, with Azure growing +48% ([MSFT Ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436122/msft-ex99_1.htm)).
- Google Cloud backlog: **$560.8B**.
- TSMC: Q3 revenue grew 41%, and October revenue was up about 60% ([TSM 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617926000726/a3q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)).
- SK hynix: DRAM and HBM "virtually sold out" through 2029 ([FN News](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202610282128013224)).
- Gas turbines: Siemens Energy reports lead times of at least 3 years ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/siemens-gas-turbine-backlog-nears-70-gw-as-company-expands-manufacturing/827390/)), and WoodMac puts a large US unit ordered today at about 6 years ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/gas-turbine-shortage-just-became-210000180.html)).
- GE Vernova: Q3 orders **+96% organic** and a gas backlog plus slot reservations of 122 GW (GEV 8-K 2026-10-28, logged in `tracking/weekly-news-scan-2026-10-31.md`).

End demand is real. Anthropic's run-rate revenue is about **$81B** ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/11/anthropic-says-its-revenue-run-rate-jumped-to-an-81-billion-run-rate/)) and OpenAI's about **$50B** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/)), though the two use different methods. Even so, revenue still covers only a fraction of the capex.

**The fragility is the funding, and it surfaced in the same weeks as the election.** Meta closed a roughly $37.5B equity raise, then $15B of bonds at 5.85–7.50% and €9B more, all within about three weeks ([META 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312526458525/d177728d8k.htm)). Oracle sold $10B of stock and $10B of notes at **7.000–8.650%** ([ORCL 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526447507/d131790d8k.htm)). Microsoft issued its first US bond deal in about nine years ([MSFT 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526447173/d178557d8k.htm)). Broadcom took on a residual-value backstop of up to about **$43B** to an SPV that funds racks for one customer ([AVGO 8-K filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001730168&type=8-K)). Meta and Alphabet printed negative quarterly free cash flow, and Amazon's trailing twelve-month FCF is −$42.4B.

Much of the backlog also traces back to OpenAI and Anthropic, whose own spending depends on fundraising and on the IPO market. Anthropic's S-1 is on file.

**This is where the political and demand stories touch.** Congress cannot cut private capex. Through 2028, suppliers' sold-out positions mean a policy-driven demand dip would first shorten queues before it cut shipments from bottleneck suppliers (moderate confidence). The one Democratic agenda item that targets the fragile link is Warren's Banking oversight of circular financing, SPVs and bailout backstops. A hearing that exposes vendor financing or off-balance-sheet guarantees cannot ban anything. It can, however, widen credit spreads for borrowers already paying 7–8.65%. That is the most plausible route from a Democratic Congress to the capex line, and the notes contain no evidence that it has started (low-to-moderate confidence; this is an inference).

## The market split "already powered" from "build-new," and Nov 5 is mostly not about Congress

The first trading day priced gridlock as good news. On Nov 4 the S&P 500 rose about 1.6% ([KCRA](https://www.kcra.com/article/midterm-election-stock-market-november-4-2026/74333444)). AMZN rose about 8%, GOOGL more than 6%, MSFT about 4% and META about 3%, and Goldman's desk reported a "clear rotation into hyperscalers." The rationale was that harder new builds raise the scarcity value of compute that already has power ([Yahoo/Investing.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/massive-market-rotation-raises-sell-114727763.html)). The same day, **VST fell about 13.5–15%** on the Texas governor result plus a KeyBanc downgrade, and CEG fell 6–9% ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/vistra-plunges-135-as-texas-election-rattles-ai-power-trade-keybanc-downgrades-5000811); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/vst-ceg-stocks-drop-election-181047161.html)). CRWV fell about 8% and IREN and NBIS about 6% each, though those figures are low confidence and the exact day is unclear.

This Nov 4 pattern is the cleanest election-attributable signal (moderate confidence). Even VST's drop is partly a downgrade effect, and VST rebounded 10.6% on its Q3 report.

**The Nov 5 semiconductor crash cannot be cleanly attributed to the election.** The SOX fell about 12%, its worst day since March 2020, and the S&P 500 fell about 4.4% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/chip-stocks-slaughtered-as-wall-street-suffers-worst-post-midterm-day-since-1896-213204256.html)). Four confounders sit in the same window:

- Disappointing Qualcomm and Arm earnings landed the same day.
- A "sell-AI" rotation was already under way: utilities rose 9.87% in October ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/markets/november-stock-market-outlook-how-position-your-portfolio-remainder-2026)).
- The equal-weight S&P 500 fell only about 2.9% while SMH fell 11.5%, which points to a concentrated positioning unwind (low-to-moderate confidence; aggregator) ([QuantisNow](https://www.quantisnow.com/articles/chip-rout-sends-smh-down-11-5-and-qqq-down-6-3-while-equal-weight-s-p-500-loses-only-2-9)).
- Semiconductors have the *weakest* direct link to Congress: export controls are executive-led and moratoria are local.

The defensible reading is that the election, through the anti-data-center ballot results and the Texas outcome, was a **catalyst for a crowded unwind, not a repricing of federal policy**. That reading is moderate confidence; the size of each component is low confidence. One "SOXX −9% on Tuesday" figure does not fit the timeline and should be discarded.

The week of Nov 9–13 was the S&P and Nasdaq's best since August, even as NVDA fell on the CEO exit ([MLQ](https://mlq.ai/news/wall-street-closes-mixed-as-nvidia-and-amd-sink-nasdaq-still-posts-its-best-week-since-august/)). Morgan Stanley's read is that policy is decided at the state and local level and the AI capex cycle remains intact ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/2026-us-midterm-election-results-market-impact)). UBS calls the selloff a "positioning reset" ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/markets/global-tech-selloff-spreads-ai-stocks-show-signs-recovery)).

## Conclusion

The coordinator's thesis survives with one structural correction. Congress is not where the federal risk lives. The executive branch Democrats are now investigating holds the strongest federal levers: BIS licensing, the 232 levy, FERC's large-load rulemaking and DOE emergency orders. Congress's main power is to make those levers politically costly. The only places Congress itself can write binding constraints are chip exports through the NDAA and data-center cost allocation through permitting. In both, Republicans co-own the policy, which is why they can pass.

For the watchlist, the result is a two-axis risk map rather than a Democrat/Republican one. One axis is **jurisdiction**: energized capacity in friendly states versus new builds in Texas, Georgia and Ohio. The other is **financing dependence**: self-funded hyperscalers and sold-out suppliers versus negative-FCF buyers, SPV-backed vendors and neoclouds with unfunded capex. Names that are weak on both axes face compounding risk. The scenario the notes cannot rule out, and that no source modeled, is a Banking Committee hearing on circular AI financing that lands just as the 2027 capex debt markets are being tapped. In that case the politics would hit demand through credit spreads rather than through law.
