## Pre-research mental model

Datadog is the default observability platform for cloud-native infrastructure, monetized on consumption, so its revenue rides customer cloud spend rather than seat counts. My prior model held that AI-native companies (OpenAI most prominently) had become a visible growth driver and also a concentration risk, that the company had been re-accelerating off the 2023-24 optimization trough, and that the strategic question was whether Datadog could turn LLM observability into a durable second act before Grafana/OpenTelemetry commoditized the ingest layer. Most recent quarter I believed reported: Q1 2026. Product cycle: Bits AI moving from assistant to agent.

## DIFF vs prior mental model

- **Growth was faster than I assumed, then guided slower.** +36% is an acceleration, not the steady-state high-20s I carried. But Q3 guidance implies ~29%, so the acceleration has a known end date.
- **The concentration risk stopped being hypothetical.** My May model treated AI-native concentration as a risk to watch; it is now a disclosed, quantified sequential decline in the largest account.
- **The renewal cuts against the churn framing.** The account did not leave. A usage step-down on a renewed nine-figure contract is a very different fact from a customer loss, and the market appears to have priced them similarly.
- **Datadog is buying its way into the model layer** (Adaptive ML) rather than only consuming it — new since May.
- **The disruption vector I had wrong:** I assumed commoditized ingest was the threat. The live threat looks like agentic SRE layers sitting *above* the data (Resolve AI), which makes Datadog's data gravity a defense rather than a liability.

## Implications for ratings

*Research pass only — no rating values proposed here (rule 12).*

- **R1 (customer concentration)** — the dimension most clearly implicated. A single nine-figure customer whose usage is now declining sequentially is exactly what R1 measures, and the exposure is now company-disclosed rather than inferred.
- **D1 (AI revenue %) / D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — cuts both ways: AI-native customers are demonstrably material to growth (supporting the tie), and demonstrably capable of throttling it. The evidence should update both the magnitude and the *durability* reading.
