## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE pulling data)

Generac is primarily a residential standby-generator company whose fortunes track storm activity and
home-improvement demand, with a smaller Commercial & Industrial segment. My model treated its
AI/data-center exposure as aspirational — a small C&I product line being marketed into the data
center opportunity but without material contracted volume, and well behind Cummins and Caterpillar
at the large-megawatt end. I expected residential to be the dominant revenue line and the swing
factor, and I did not have any hyperscaler agreement in the model.

**Critical quality-of-earnings item, stated in the release itself:** net income, adjusted net income
and adjusted EBITDA **all include a ~$71M pre-tax benefit from tariff refunds** recorded in the
quarter. That is roughly 24% of the $291M adjusted EBITDA. The 700bps YoY adjusted-EBITDA-margin
expansion (17.7% → 24.8%) is substantially a one-time item — this must not be read as clean
operating leverage. Acquisitions/divestitures/FX added a net favorable 2% to sales growth.

Subtitle of the release: "Rapidly increasing data center backlog provides visibility to significant
2027 growth." The release confirms a **global supply agreement with a leading hyperscale data center
operator** for backup power generators, with product-specific terms agreed during the quarter.

### 2b. FCF conversion red-flag check
TTM FCF $0.38B ÷ TTM net income $0.26B = **146.9% conversion** (TTM revenue $4.44B). Above 100%, so
the rule-2b flag cannot fire. Note the interpretive caveat from the rule itself: conversion >100%
often reflects non-cash addbacks and is *not* evidence of quality by itself. **Not flagged.**

### 2c. Expectations red-flag check (rule 14)
`expectations_flag.py GNRC` → **clean**: P/S 2.84 = 87.3rd percentile of own 3-year range; revenue
YoY +24.6% vs 3-year median −0.0%. The 3-year median of ~0% growth is the tell — this company was
flat-to-shrinking, and is now growing 25%. The multiple is re-rating on a genuine inflection, which
is the argument the rule asks the briefing to make explicitly. The risk is that the inflection is
being extrapolated while residential (still 53% of sales) is *declining*.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flagging only — not pre-deciding)

- **D1 (AI-revenue %)** — revisit. ~$450M expected 2026 data-center revenue on ~$4.4B TTM revenue is
  roughly 10%, up from approximately nothing. Still a minority of revenue; rate on the actual share,
  not the backlog narrative.
- **D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — revisit, likely **upward**. Two hyperscaler agreements with
  a quantified $1.6B backlog and ~$700M committed for 2027 is contracted, quantified evidence — the
  standard the rule-23/L11 D5 bands ask for. This is the single strongest candidate for a rating
  move across this week's eight names.
- **D3 (moat)** — caution against over-crediting. Generac is the newest entrant of the three at
  large megawatt; the skeptic case (mission-critical execution risk) is unresolved.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — revisit **downward-leaning** (i.e. more risk). Two undisclosed
  hyperscalers driving the entire growth story concentrates the forward revenue base sharply.
- **R3 (balance sheet)** — check FCF: $63M FCF on $1.17B sales is thin, and a 3× capacity expansion
  is a working-capital and capex commitment.
- **Quality inputs generally** — when the rule-9 objective refresh runs, be alert that TTM margins
  now embed the $71M tariff refund. Flag it in the row so the Quality percentile isn't misread.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh:** reported 2026-07-29 — **overdue by ~12 days.** Adjusted EPS $2.91 vs
  ~$1.95 consensus is a >15% beat, which under rule 9 is **priority-1 (immediate)**. Escalating this
  as the top objective-refresh item from this week's rotation.

**No thesis-break signals.** The tariff-refund inflation is an earnings-quality caveat, not a break.
