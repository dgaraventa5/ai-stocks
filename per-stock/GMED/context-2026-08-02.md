## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE searching)

- Globus is a **spine implant company that also sells a robot**, not a robotics company. ExcelsiusGPS is a navigation/guidance platform whose commercial job is to lock surgeons into Globus implants.
- Post-NuVasive (2023), it is the #2/#3 spine player at roughly $2.5–3B of revenue with unusually high gross margin (~68%) for a device company, vertically integrated manufacturing, and a founder-controlled share structure (David Paul).
- I expected robotics to be roughly 10% of revenue — that was my number and it came from industry commentary, not a filing.
- I assumed growth was high-single-digit organic, and that any headline 25%+ number would be acquisition-driven.
- I had **no** knowledge of the leasing/rental pivot, and no view on the stock price.

### ★ THE HEADLINE FINDING — robotics revenue is 3.5%, not 10–15%
Revenue disaggregation (10-Q Note 4, corroborated in Note 18 segment table):
| Category | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 | Δ | % of Q1 2026 sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Musculoskeletal Solutions | **$732.984M** | $575.932M | +27.3% | **96.5%** |
| **Enabling Technologies** | **$26.870M** | $22.189M | **+21.1%** | **3.5%** |

Enabling Technologies **is** the robotics line — the 10-Q defines it as "imaging, navigation and robotics ('INR') solutions… advanced computer-assisted intelligent systems" (Note 4 / MD&A). Excelsius sits entirely inside it. The roster's "~10–15% direct" is **not supported by the filing**; the disclosed direct figure is **3.5%**, and it grew *slower* than the company (+21% vs +27%).

Two honest offsets:
1. The **leasing/rental pivot mechanically depresses this line** — a lease is recognized over time instead of upfront, so reported ET revenue understates units placed.
2. Robot-attached implant/disposable pull-through lands in Musculoskeletal Solutions and is **not disclosed**, so the economic contribution is larger than 3.5%. The company does not quantify it.

### Growth is substantially inorganic
The **Nevro merger closed 2026-04-03** (10-Q Note 3) — $5.85/share cash, plus $18.5M to retire Nevro's term loans/warrants; "Globus was deemed to be the accounting acquirer… **prior periods… may not be comparable**."
Q1 2026 MD&A attributes **$67.2M of the U.S. increase and $15.5M of the international increase to Nevro** = **$82.7M**, none of which was in the Q1 2025 base.
→ **Ex-Nevro revenue growth ≈ (759.9 − 82.7) / 598.1 − 1 = +13.2%**, not +27%. Consensus agrees: FY2026 revenue estimate is **$3.20B, +8.96%**, because Nevro laps from Q2 2026 onward.

## 5. Rating implications

- **D1 is the whole argument for GMED's inclusion, and the filing says 3.5%.** I propose **2**, not 1, because (a) the leasing pivot mechanically suppresses the reported ET line, and (b) robot-attached implant pull-through is real but undisclosed. On a strict reading of the disclosed number, **1** is defensible — this is the most debatable rating in the five-name batch and should be discussed. What is *not* defensible is the roster's 10–15%.
- **D2 = 2.** Downstream implant/device maker in a contested spine market (Medtronic, Stryker, Alphatec, ZimVie). Vertical integration is a cost advantage, not a bottleneck.
- **D3 = 3.** Genuine ecosystem lock-in (robot + navigation + AR + implants at 68.5% GM) and NuVasive scale, but spine share shifts constantly and the enabling-tech competitors are larger.
- **D4 = 4.** Four FDA-cleared enabling-tech products across three anatomies, Nevro integration, plus a leasing pivot that puts systems into the field **on Globus's own balance sheet** — a funded, capital-consuming expansion.
- **D5 = 2 (L11 lens, proposed).** AR navigation and surgical planning are real, but the filing's AI language is future-tense ("as new technologies such as… artificial intelligence **are introduced**"). No shipped AI product, no data-network effect, no infrastructure tie. Honest read: this is a navigation-and-guidance business riding surgeon workflow, not a physical-AI business.
- **M1 = 4.** EPS/revenue estimates being revised up; Q1 was a record with guidance reaffirmed. PT cuts are multiple compression, which M1 does not measure.
- **M2 = 5 on the 12-month window used consistently across this cohort (+47.4% vs IHI −14.1%). FLAGGED AS DEBATABLE:** the rubric specifies "last 3–6 months," and on that window GMED is roughly −20% and below both moving averages, which would argue 3. I used 12 months because it is the only window I could source consistently for all five names and both benchmarks; the limitation is disclosed rather than papered over (rule 3).
- **M3 = 3.** No buying at all; every sale is option-exercise-same-day or 10b5-1, which the rubric explicitly treats as routine (step 2). The absence of buying after a −22% move off the high is the mild negative, but the rubric's default stands.
- **R1 = 5** (thousands of hospital accounts, no >10% customer disclosed), **R2 = 5** (79.6% U.S., 20.4% international with no China concentration; worst plausible shock is EU MDR/tariffs on ~20% of revenue), **R3 = 5** (~$799M cash+securities, undrawn revolver, no notes outstanding), **R4 = 3** (accrued $43.1M Pimenta; 4WEB injunction risk unquantified), **R5 = 5** (non-Layer-10 default).
