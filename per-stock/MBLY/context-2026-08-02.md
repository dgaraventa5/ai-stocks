## 1. Pre-research mental model (before this pass)

- The cheapest genuinely profitable autonomy asset: ~19x forward earnings after a −50% drawdown caused by a **non-cash** $3.788B goodwill impairment, with adjusted operating profit growing 46%.
- Mentee Robotics ($900M, humanoid) = a free option priced at zero.
- Intel still controls it; CEO succession unresolved; robotaxi fleet ownership is a capital-intensive pivot.
- Rule-15 EPS blank applies (impairment-dominated).

Four things change materially: the *source* of the 46% profit growth, the *nature* of the Mentee transaction, the state of the litigation docket, and the fact that Q2 revenue was flat while gross margin fell 350bps.

## 4. DIFF — where fresh reality departs from the prior model

### 4a. ★ The +46% adjusted operating profit is a tax subsidy, not operating leverage
Adjusted operating profit rose 46% **because of a $93M retroactive credit** under a law enacted 2026-03-29. On the same revenue base, **GAAP gross margin fell 350bps (49.8% → 46.3%)** and **GAAP operating income was −$30M**. FY2026 adjusted operating income guidance of $365–425M includes **$180–200M** from the incentive — i.e. **roughly half of guided adjusted operating profit is a government credit**, not product economics. The "adjusted operating profit grew 46% while the stock fell 50%" framing in `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md` is true but materially incomplete, and should not be carried into the thesis unqualified.
Consensus already sees this: **2027E EPS is guided *down* 6.9%** even as revenue grows 10%.

### 4b. ★ The Mentee acquisition was a related-party transaction with the CEO and CTO
Form 4s filed **2026-02-05** disclose that **CEO Amnon Shashua and CTO Shai Shalev-Shwartz were Mentee Robotics shareholders** and sold their Mentee shares to Mobileye for **cash equal to 50% of their total consideration plus Mobileye Class A stock**:
- **Shashua received 15,543,098 MBLY shares** (1,554,310 subject to escrow + 13,988,788 as deferred consideration) — **~59% of the entire 26,279,824-share equity consideration** — *plus* cash equal to ~50% of his total consideration.
- **Shalev-Shwartz received 5,368,363 MBLY shares** (536,835 + 4,831,528) on the same terms.

Neither research pass surfaced this. It does not make the deal wrong — Mentee was founded by Shashua, so his ownership is unsurprising — but it means a $900M purchase transferred substantial value from Mobileye to its own CEO and CTO, approved by a board at a company where **Intel holds 96.9% of the voting power** and minority holders have no practical say. Combined with the CEO's departure announcement five months later, this belongs in the governance column, and the "humanoid option priced at zero" framing should be read alongside "the humanoid option was bought from the CEO."

### 4c. ★ The litigation docket is CLEANER than assumed, not dirtier
- The IPO securities class action is **over**: Second Circuit affirmed dismissal in full on 2025-12-16 and the cert window closed with no petition.
- Facet's patents are **expired**, and the PTAB **invalidated every claim asserted** in the live Texas case. Both district actions are stayed. No provision on either matter.
This is a genuine upgrade to R4 relative to a default assumption of pending litigation.

### 4d. Q2 revenue was flat and gross margin fell — the core is not growing
$508M vs $506M. EyeQ units +3%. EyeQ SoCs are **90% of revenue** and their share is *declining* (92% → 90%), which is the mix shift toward SuperVision/Chauffeur the bull case wants — but it came with a 350bps gross-margin decline, not an expansion. H1's +12.9% was front-loaded into Q1 (+27%).

### 4e. The trading setup: below book, below both moving averages, mid-succession
$7.94 vs a $9.84 200DMA and a $9.22 50DMA. **P/B 0.81 — below tangible book after the write-down.** 52-week change −43.08%. Net cash $1.70/share = 21% of the share price. EV/Sales 2.63. The market is pricing terminal decline in EyeQ; the counter is that the goodwill written off was Intel's 2017 purchase price, which has zero cash meaning and zero bearing on the business.

### 4f. Geography is not the "Israel risk" story the shorthand suggests
The shipment-geography table shows **China as the largest destination at 24.0% of Q2 revenue** — larger than the USA. The Israel exposure is not revenue; it is **where essentially all of the engineering is**, in a country the 10-Q discloses entered joint US-Israel combat operations against Iran on 2026-02-28. For a fabless company (STMicro fabs EyeQ), the at-risk asset is people and R&D continuity, not production lines. Both legs need to be named explicitly in R2.

## 5. Rating implications

- **Rule 15 — BLANK EPS YoY. Documented one-time dominance:** H1 2026 GAAP net loss of **−$3,839M** contains a **$3,788M non-cash goodwill impairment** (10-Q Note 12), i.e. **98.7%** of the loss. TTM GAAP EPS of **−$4.98** vs FY2025's −$0.48 is a pure write-down artefact with zero cash effect. Growth should average the revenue metrics only. **Cite: Mobileye 10-Q, period ended 2026-06-27, Note 12 — Goodwill, impairment $3,788M, Mobileye reporting unit.**
- **Quality flag (not a blank, but do not read the adjusted numbers naively):** ~half of FY2026 guided adjusted operating income is an Israeli refundable R&D tax credit; GAAP operating income was −$30M in Q2.
- **R3** — $1.44B net cash, no debt, positive operating cash flow, active buyback. Unambiguous.
- **R1** — top customer 28%, top four 73%; these are Tier-1 automotive suppliers, so underlying OEM diversification is broader than the table implies, but the rubric scores the top customer.
- **R2** — two legs to name: an R&D/personnel asset leg in an active conflict zone, and a 24%-of-revenue China demand/policy leg.
- **R4** — litigation genuinely cleared; the residual is governance (96.9% Intel voting control + a related-party acquisition), which is what holds it below 4.
- **M3** — no open-market buys and no discretionary sells in 12 months → rubric default 3. Intel is the controlling holder; any Intel disposition would be corporate portfolio action, not insider signal — and none appears in the Form 4 record over the period.

### DEBATABLE — flag for Dom
- **M1 = 4 vs 5.** By the rubric's letter this is a beat-and-raise and should be 5 — the rubric even says "if you find yourself agonizing, you're overthinking it." I went to 4 because the raise is a **tax credit**, not operations: $180–200M of the $365–425M adjusted-operating-income guide, on a quarter where revenue was flat and GAAP gross margin fell 350bps, and 2027E EPS is now revised *down*. If you want M1 kept mechanical, take the 5 — but then note the quality caveat elsewhere.
- **The $93M / $180–200M Israeli R&D credit deserves a house decision.** It is a *recurring statutory regime* (enacted 2026-03-29, applying to tax years from 2026-01-01), not a one-off, so it does not trigger a rule-15 blank. But it means "Mobileye's adjusted operating profit grew 46%" and "the cheapest profitable autonomy asset at ~19x forward" both lean heavily on a government subsidy. **Worth deciding explicitly whether the Value inputs should be stress-tested ex-credit.**
- **R4 = 3 vs 4.** On litigation alone this is a 4 — everything is dismissed or neutered. I held at 3 entirely on governance: 96.9% Intel voting control plus a $900M related-party acquisition from the CEO. If you think governance belongs somewhere other than R4 (it arguably has no clean home in the rubric), then R4 should be 4 and the governance concern needs a different vehicle. **This is a rubric-coverage gap worth naming.**
- **D3 = 3 vs 4.** REM map data plus homologation switching cost is a legitimate case for 4, and EyeQ volume did outgrow customer production by >8pts. I stayed at 3 because band 4 requires "share held vs credible competitors, pricing roughly with inflation," and flat revenue with a 350bps gross-margin decline is not that.
- **R2 = 2 vs 3.** The rubric's asset leg is written as "production assets physically located in a risk geography." Mobileye's production is at STMicro in Europe/Asia; what sits in Israel is R&D. I treated engineering concentration as economically equivalent for a fabless IP company in an *active* combat theatre. A strict textual reading gives 3 (two legs: China demand + China policy, no production asset leg).
- **Succession + strategy risk is not in any rating.** A founder-CEO of 27 years is leaving mid-pivot into two capital-intensive new businesses (robotaxi fleet ownership, humanoids) neither of which he will see through, at a company where minorities cannot vote. The rubric has no dimension for that. Worth carrying in the thesis narrative.
