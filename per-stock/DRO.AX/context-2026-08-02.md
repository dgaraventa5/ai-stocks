## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

DroneShield is the only profitable listed counter-UAS pure play: RF-based detection (DroneSentry, RfPatrol) and defeat (DroneGun) sold to allied militaries and government agencies. My prior: TTM revenue +274%, committed revenue A$206M, a growing European order book, TTM net income modestly positive, and a stock down ~75% from its high after guiding CY2026 revenue below consensus (decelerating to +15–25%). AUD-trading and AUD-reporting so no rule-19 currency issue. The parent's brief expected **H1 audited results reported in late July**.

Two things were badly wrong: the July release is **not** audited results, and there is an **active ASIC investigation into insider trading by the former CEO and Chairman** that the roster research does not mention at all.

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

**① There is an active ASIC investigation into insider trading by the former CEO and Chairman, and both are gone. The roster research does not mention it.** This is the single most important fact about DroneShield and it re-writes two ratings (M3 and R4) on its own. A retracted contract announcement issued on the same day that the CEO and Chairman were liquidating their entire positions, followed six months later by both departing, is not a governance footnote — it is the reason the stock trades where it does, and no amount of committed revenue offsets it until ASIC concludes.

**② The 2026-07-28 release is a preliminary trading update, not audited half-year accounts — correct the parent's premise.** DroneShield has a 31 December balance date, so the reviewed/audited half-year report for the period to 30 June 2026 has **not yet been lodged**. Every H1 figure above is management-prepared and subject to change. Given ①, that distinction is not pedantic.

**③ Growth is decelerating hard *into* the budget inflection, and the margin is going with it.** TTM +274% → CY2026 guidance **+15–25%**. Gross margin **65% → 60%**. Guidance implies H2 roughly flat on H1 despite A$206M committed. This is the exact opposite of what a company should be printing when the FY27 US budget puts ~$21B against counter-UAS and munitions — and it is the strongest available evidence on whether DroneShield's moat produces pricing power (see D3).

**④ The forward P/E (56.5x) sits above the trailing P/E (44.9x).** Mechanically, that means consensus expects **lower** EPS next year than trailing — a quantitative confirmation of negative revisions independent of any analyst commentary.

**⑤ Profitability is real but paper-thin.** A$3.52M of TTM net income on A$216.81M of revenue is a **1.6% net margin**. "The only profitable counter-UAS pure play" is true and is a genuine differentiator versus ONDS and RCAT — but it is not a margin structure, it is a rounding error above breakeven, and a 500bps gross-margin decline can erase it.

**⑥ Dilution:** one commentary source references ~43% dilution alongside the ASIC probe. **I could not verify this against a primary ASX filing and am not treating it as established** — flagged in §6.

## 5. Rating implications — proposed 13 subjective ratings

### AI Thesis (D1–D5)

**D1. AI/Robotics Revenue % — 5**
- *Rationale:* **100%** of revenue is counter-UAS detection and defeat — DroneSentry / DroneSentry-X, DroneGun, RfPatrol hardware plus DroneOptID / SensorFusionAI classification software. There is no non-robotics line.
- *Source:* Company product disclosures; `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md`.
- *Confidence:* High.

**D2. Supply Chain Position — 3**
- *Rationale:* Mid-chain with real differentiation. The accumulated RF-signature library and AI classification models behind DroneOptID are genuine IP, and DroneShield is the volume leader in man-portable counter-UAS. But counter-UAS is now the most crowded procurement in defense — Anduril, ONDS, RTX, Leonardo DRS, Hensoldt and dozens of startups chase the same programs — the low end of the product line is an exportable commodity, and the **deceleration to +15–25% while the budget line explodes is itself evidence of competitive pressure, not scarcity**.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update (guidance); `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md` (competitive set).
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**D3. Moat Strength — 3**
- *Rationale:* Modest moat. What exists: the RF-signature dataset and classification models (a genuine data advantage that compounds with fielded units), allied-government qualification, and an installed base across NATO and Five Eyes. Not 4: **gross margin fell from 65% to 60%** and growth guidance decelerated to +15–25% in the same period that the addressable budget inflected — the two facts together say customers are getting better prices, not that DroneShield holds pricing power. Share stability cannot be verified from disclosure.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update (margin and guidance).
- *Confidence:* Medium. *Bias check applied:* the −75% drawdown did not set this rating; the margin and guidance did, and both are management's own numbers.

**D4. Capacity Expansion — 4**
- *Rationale:* Active expansion in progress, self-funded: capacity and headcount scaled through 2025–26 to service **A$206M of committed revenue**, funded by **four consecutive quarters of positive net operating cash flow**, with a European order book being built out (**A$23.2M** of new European military contracts announced with the H1 update). Not 5: no committed multi-year capex program is disclosed, and guidance implying flat sequential H2 revenue argues against a step-change buildout.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**D5. AI-buildout tie — 3 · L11 lens (proposed)**
- *Rationale:* Real but **medium**-strength on the parallel scale. Positives: 100% counter-UAS revenue, A$206M of FY2026 committed revenue, a growing European defence order book — counter-UAS is the fastest-moving line in the FY27 budget cycle. Held to 3 because the tie is primarily to **allied European and Australian budgets rather than the US $75B FY27 line**; DroneShield holds **no US program of record**; and the CY2026 guide of +15–25% is direct evidence that the buildout is **not yet converting into its orders** at the rate the budget implies.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update; `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md`.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**M3. Insider Activity — 1**

### Risk (R1–R5) — 5 = lowest risk

**R1. Customer Concentration — 3**
- *Rationale:* Customers are allied defence and government agencies across the US, Europe, the Middle East and Australia; **A$206M of FY2026 committed revenue** and **A$23.2M of newly announced European military contracts** indicate multiple distinct buyers rather than a single program. But DroneShield's revenue is famously lumpy and single-order-driven — the entire November 2025 episode turned on one **A$7.6M** line item — and as an ASX filer it does **not publish a >10%-customer table** the way a 10-K filer must. Estimated top-customer/top-program share **25–40%** → band 3.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update; November 2025 announcement history.
- *Confidence:* **Low-Medium — this is an estimate, not a disclosed figure.** Flagged.

**R2. Geographic / Export Risk — 4**
- *Rationale (mandatory named shock + % exposed):* The worst plausible shock is an **export-licensing or end-user restriction on counter-UAS equipment into the European/Ukraine theatre**, which is the disclosed source of order-book growth (**A$23.2M** of European military contracts announced with the H1 update, and Europe repeatedly described as the growth driver). Exposed revenue: **not separately disclosed**, but plausibly **>25%** — a demand leg. No China policy leg. No asset leg (operations are Sydney-based; Australia is not a risk geography). One leg, demand only.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update (European contracts and commentary).
- *Confidence:* **Low-Medium — DroneShield does not publish a geographic revenue split in any source available this session.** The >25% is inferred from management's own characterisation of Europe as the growth driver. Flagged.

**R3. Balance Sheet Risk — 4**
- *Rationale:* Modest and improving. DroneShield is **profitable** (TTM net income A$3.52M), has posted **four consecutive quarters of positive net operating cash flow**, and was funded through equity rather than debt — so leverage is minimal and there is no maturity wall. Not 5: profitability is razor-thin (**1.6% net margin**), gross margin fell 500bps to ~60%, and the equity funding came at meaningful dilution.
- *Source:* 2026-07-28 trading update; [stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/DRO](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/asx/DRO/) (TTM net income A$3.52M).
- *Confidence:* **Low-Medium — I could not obtain an audited balance sheet this session.** The reviewed half-year accounts to 30 June 2026 have not yet been lodged. Net cash, total equity and the actual share count on issue are **unverified**. One commentary source references **~43% dilution**; that figure is **secondary and unverified** and I have not scored on it. **This rating should be re-run against the half-year report when lodged.**

**R4. Regulatory / Litigation Risk — 2**
- *Rationale:* **An active regulatory investigation is under way.** ASIC is investigating DroneShield over **market announcements made in November 2025 and related share trading by two former senior executives** — specifically the **10 November 2025 A$7.6M "new business" release that was retracted hours later** as re-issued purchase orders, and trading between **6 and 12 November 2025** by the then-CEO, then-Chairman and a director. The company says it is co-operating fully. Both the CEO and Chairman were replaced in **April 2026**. This is squarely band 2 ("material litigation or **active regulatory investigations**"). Not 1: no enforcement action, penalty, class action or civil proceeding has been commenced on the record available.
- *Source:* ASIC investigation reporting, May–June 2026 (secondary press — see M3 sourcing note); company leadership announcements April 2026.
- *Confidence:* Medium.
- **Flag:** verify against primary ASX announcements / ASIC statements before locking. If an enforcement action or shareholder class action is commenced, this becomes a **1**.

**R5. Disruption Risk — 5**
- *Rationale:* Rule-16 default for all non-Layer-10 names. Physical RF-sensing and defeat hardware sold to defence agencies; no credible AI/structural disruption to the revenue model on a ~5-year view — AI drone proliferation is the demand driver.
- *Source:* CLAUDE.md rule 16 / rubric R5 scope note.
- *Confidence:* High.
