## 1. Pre-research mental model

Written after reading the June briefing, before opening the new filings or searching:

> KLA is the process-control (inspection + metrology) near-monopoly in wafer fab equipment, at roughly 60%+ gross margin, with a recurring services annuity of about 20% of revenue. Process control intensity rises with leading-edge logic, HBM/DRAM and advanced packaging, which makes KLA a second-derivative AI play through fab capex rather than through hyperscaler sales. As of June, the latest quarter was Q3 FY26 (Mar-26, $3.415B revenue). WFE 2026 had been raised to >$140B, advanced packaging was heading to ~$1B, and China was expected to fall from ~33% to the mid/high-20s under export controls. I expect the September quarter to be a beat with continued AI-led growth. China mix should be stepping down. The open question is why the stock is down ~30% in a month: an export-control escalation, or a sector-wide AI-capex de-rating?

## 6. Implications for ratings (flags only; ratings unchanged)

Current: D1 4, D2 5, D3 5, D4 4, D5 4 | M1 5, M2 2, M3 3 | R1 4, R2 3, R3 5, R4 4, R5 5.

- **D1 (AI revenue %):** growth is explicitly attributed to leading-edge, HBM and advanced packaging demand "supported by continued data center investment and the adoption of AI" (10-Q MD&A). But 34.5% of last quarter was China mature-node, which is not AI-driven, and advanced packaging (~$1.1B) is only ~7% of CY26 revenue. A quantified >50% AI share is not demonstrable; the 25–50% band still fits.
- **D2 (position):** lead times are extending and demand exceeds supply. This matches the band-5 text ("lead times widening") better than in June.
- **D3 (moat):** near-monopoly intact. Record operating margin and value-based pricing on new capabilities. No erosion signal.
- **D4 (capacity):** every factory expanding, a new India campus, capex guide $600–800M/yr (vs $195M last quarter alone and lower historically), and $8.87B of purchase commitments behind 12-month lead times. Arguably moving toward band 5 ("major buildout... customer commitments backing it"). But the rubric anchors semi-equipment at 4 and the capex is ~4% of revenue. Flag for discussion only.
- **D5 (supplier lens):** KLA sells to chipmakers, not hyperscalers. Read literally, the supplier bands describe indirect exposure (band 2). The rubric anchor "Most semi-equipment: 3–4" is what holds it at 4. Nothing changed this quarter; the literal-vs-anchor tension predates this briefing.
- **M1:** beat-and-raise, WFE raised, Dec guide well above the Sep run-rate. Supports the existing 5, but revision data was **not** checked.
- **M2:** 3m KLAC −28.1% vs SMH −18.5% = ~9.6 pts behind (band 2). 1m −29.6% vs −23.6%. 6m SMH not pulled.
- **M3:** tax withholding plus 10b5-1 sales only; no buys. Default 3.
- **R1:** top customer ~21% (band 4: 15–25%).
- **R2:** worst plausible shock = a broad China equipment ban or Affiliates-Rule reimposition hitting KLA's China customers. **34.5% of Q1 FY27 revenue exposed** (10-Q MD&A). Two legs (Taiwan 24.7% shipment concentration plus China policy) gives band 3. If the Affiliates Rule is reimposed 2026-11-10 and KLA discloses a material revenue hit, band 2 ("policy currently impairing material revenue") comes into play. **Re-check after 2026-11-10.**
- **R3:** literal band text: 5 = "Net cash position". KLA is **net debt** (~$1.2B, ~0.2x EBITDA, well-spread maturities, $4B TTM FCF). That fits band 4 ("Modest leverage. ND/EBITDA <2x. Maturities well-spread") better than band 5. This is a band-fit observation, not a new event; the position was the same at 2026-06-30.
- **R4:** routine legal note; the generic IP risk-factor update is not a matter. Step 2d was skipped, so the absence of a docket is unverified.
- **R5:** default 5 (non-Layer-10).

**Thesis-break triggers:** none. **Rule-35 events:** none recorded. No enforcement action, no securities case found (with 2d skipped), no guidance cut (guidance was raised), no transformative transaction. The Affiliates-Rule lapse is a dated policy risk, not one of the four rule-35 categories.
