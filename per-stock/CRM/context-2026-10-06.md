## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before any data pull, quoted verbatim)

> Written 2026-10-06 before any filing/web/yfinance pull (after reading only the prior briefing and news-log, which the task required).
> - AI thesis / revenue mix: Salesforce is the incumbent seat-priced CRM suite (Sales, Service, Platform/Slack, Marketing & Commerce, Integration & Analytics incl. Tableau/MuleSoft/Informatica). My training has revenue ~$41B for FY26 growing high-single digits, non-GAAP operating margin ~33-34%. AI revenue is "Agentforce + Data Cloud ARR"; I remember ~$1.4B combined with Agentforce alone ~$0.5B in late FY26. I expect AI-labelled ARR to still be a single-digit % of revenue, and I expect a meaningful part of the "Data and AI" number to be relabelled existing product (Data Cloud, and after the deal, Informatica), i.e. D1 = 2 is probably right on a literal reading and 3 would need the broad bundle to count.
> - Customers / competitive position: extremely diversified (no customer near 10%); competitors on the agent layer are Microsoft (Copilot/Dynamics), ServiceNow, plus model vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic) selling agents directly. Moat is the system-of-record data + ecosystem, not the agent.
> - Most recent quarter per training: Q3/Q4 FY26 (late 2025 / Feb 2026). I do not have Q1/Q2 FY27 from training; the prior briefing gives secondary figures (rev ~$11.35B +11%, cRPO +14%, Agentforce ARR >$1.5B) that I treat as unverified.
> - Product cycle: Agentforce (launched Oct 2024), pricing moved from $2/conversation to Flex Credits ($0.10/action) and agentic enterprise licence agreements; Informatica (~$8B) closed late 2025.
> - Things I suspect are wrong in the carried-forward notes: (a) "~$25B Informatica debt" — the deal was ~$8B, so $25B more plausibly ties to a debt-funded buyback; (b) "$5.90 non-GAAP EPS" including an investment gain — non-GAAP EPS normally DOES include strategic-investment gains at Salesforce, so this may be legitimately reported; (c) cRPO +14% probably includes Informatica (inorganic), so organic cRPO is likely ~9-10%; (d) I expect seat-erosion evidence to be mixed rather than absent, and NRR to remain undisclosed (Salesforce discloses attrition, not NRR).

### 2.2 Agentforce / Data 360 — what is disclosed

- Agentforce ARR "exceeded $1.5 billion, up over 240% Y/Y" — **and the definition was widened this quarter**: "Effective Q2 FY27, Agentforce ARR includes our AI offerings, Slackbot and Headless 360" ([PR] company highlights and "Other Metrics" note). The +240% is therefore not like-for-like with Q1's figure; the size of the reclassification is not disclosed.
- "Agentforce and Data 360 ARR reached nearly $3.9 billion, up over 210% Y/Y" ([PR]). The metric note defines it as Data 360 plus "certain generative AI subscription agreements" and, in the same note, defines "Informatica Cloud ARR". At Q4 FY26 the same metric was $2.9B "including $1.1 billion Informatica Cloud ARR and $800 million Agentforce ARR" (Q4 FY26 release, https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/02/25/fy26-q4-earnings/ — via search summary, not re-read in full). **The Q2 release does not restate the Informatica Cloud component**; a >200% growth rate is only arithmetically plausible with it included. Treat $3.9B as including roughly $1.1–1.4B of acquired Informatica Cloud ARR (inferred, unverified for Q2).
- Data 360 is shown at "$1B AOV" (annual order value) on the Investor Day financial slide, next to Sales $9B and Service $10B ([Deck], "Driving durable revenue growth & margin expansion").
- Usage: 3.2B Agentic Work Units in Q2, +97% QoQ; 7.0B cumulative ([PR]). Accounts with agents in production +70% QoQ ([Call], Benioff). Agentforce bookings roughly doubled YoY and about half came from existing customers buying more credits ([Call], Milano).
- **Paid-deal counts were not given this quarter.** The last hard count is the Q4 FY26 release (29,000 deals; the Rating Audit entry of 2026-05-18 records 9.5k paid). The Q2 materials substitute growth rates (+70% accounts in production) for counts. Gap flagged.
- Premium-edition penetration: only ~5% of Sales/Service knowledge workers have moved to the premium editions, which carry a 60–80% price premium ([Call], Milano). Bookings of the premium SKUs (Agentforce One Edition, Agentforce for Apps) "more than doubled Q/Q" ([PR]).
- **Pricing model is hybrid and getting harder to separate.** Per-seat editions with bundled credits, consumption (Flex Credits, $0.10 per standard action), per-resolution ($2), and unlimited multi-year agreements all coexist ([Call]; pricing timeline at https://pricinginnovation.substack.com/p/agentforces-pricing-journey-every, published 2026-10-06 — secondary). The [10-Q] risk factors now say consumption models "may make it more difficult to optimize our pricing, predict attrition rates, and accurately forecast revenue" and that the company has "limited experience" pricing consumption contracts.
- **Net-new versus repackaged:** not disclosed. Three facts argue a material share is relabelling or bundling rather than new money: (i) the revenue line containing the entire legacy seat business was *renamed* "Agentforce Apps" in Q1 FY27 ([10-Q] MD&A, revised disaggregation); (ii) Agentforce ARR absorbed Slackbot and Headless 360 this quarter; (iii) the AI+data ARR figure includes acquired Informatica Cloud ARR. Against that, usage (work units +97% QoQ) and credit refills are real consumption.

### 2.3 Rule 15 — the strategic-investment gain (primary, confirmed)

- "Gains on strategic investments, net" = **$2,613M** in Q2 vs ~$0 a year earlier ([PR] income statement). It "include[s] gains of $2.7 billion" on the Anthropic stake, an **unrealized** mark-up of a private holding, partly offset by $285M of impairments on other holdings ([10-Q] MD&A, gains on strategic investments; Note on fair value).
- Anthropic carrying value ≈ $5.1B = ~45% of the $11.3B strategic portfolio, up from ~22% at 2026-01-31 ([10-Q] concentration-of-risk note and Item 3).
- Per-share effect: $2.43 of the $4.29 GAAP diluted EPS and $2.53 of the $5.90 non-GAAP diluted EPS ([PR] footnote 3). **Salesforce's non-GAAP EPS includes investment gains by policy** ([PR] non-GAAP explanation) — so the prior briefing's question is answered: $5.90 is a genuine non-GAAP figure, and the gain is in it.
- Stripped of the gain: non-GAAP EPS ≈ $3.37 vs $2.91 (+16%); GAAP EPS ≈ $1.86 vs $1.96 (**−5%**) — GAAP operating income was flat and interest expense rose to $473M from $67M, so underlying GAAP earnings per share fell despite ~11% fewer shares (my arithmetic from [PR]).
- **Conclusion:** the rule-15 blank logged in the Rating Audit on 2026-09-07 is correct on primary evidence. If an ex-item value were ever substituted, the honest GAAP figure is about −5%, not a positive number.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flags for the rating session — nothing changed here)

Current: D1=2, D2=3, D3=4, D4=4, D5=3, M1=4, M2=2, M3=3, R1=4, R2=4, R3=4, R4=4, R5=3.

**D1 (AI revenue % of total) — evidence supports 2; cannot reach 3.** Denominator: Q2 revenue annualized = $45.4B (11,345 × 4).

| Reading | AI ARR | % of revenue | Rubric band |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agentforce only (even on the widened definition) | >$1.5B | ~3.3% | 1 (<5%) |
| Agentforce + Data 360, excluding acquired Informatica Cloud (≈ $1.5B + ~$1B Data 360 AOV) | ~$2.5B | ~5.5% | 2 (5–10%) |
| Company's full "Agentforce and Data 360 ARR" | ~$3.9B | ~8.6% | 2 (5–10%) |
| Threshold for a 3 | $4.5B | 10% | — |

Even the most generous company-defined figure is below 10%, and ARR is a run-rate, ahead of recognized revenue. The rubric's named failure mode — "legacy business with AI marketing applied" — describes the renamed "Agentforce Apps" line; it must not be counted. The strict agent-only reading would be a 1. The full bundle could cross 10% within a few quarters at current growth, but a third of it is an acquired data-integration business.

**R5 (disruption risk) — evidence supports holding 3.** For a 4 the rubric requires AI to be net-neutral/tailwind *or* to threaten "only a peripheral line." Service ($10B AOV) is the direct target of resolution-priced agents, and the company itself reports "some seat optimization" at agent adopters, so "peripheral" does not fit. For a 2 or 1 the rubric needs durability in question or visible erosion; primary numbers show the opposite (core +8% CC, attrition flat ~8%, no pricing concession in MD&A). Band 3 — "contested but a structural moat (data, ecosystem) resists core disruption" — fits both halves. Movement toward 4 would need reported (not guided) organic re-acceleration with attrition still ~8%; movement toward 2 would be attrition rising above ~8% or the core apps line decelerating below ~6%.

**R3 (balance sheet) — evidence points to 3, not 4.** The 2026-05-18 audit rationale for the 4 was "ND/EBITDA 0.65"; that input was already stale (the notes were issued in March) and is now ~2.2x (Watchlist input 2.4), ~2.6x after the two cash deals. Rubric: 4 = "<2x"; 3 = "ND/EBITDA 2–4x." Strong cash generation and laddered maturities are real mitigants but the rubric's numeric band is met for 3.

**R4 (regulatory/litigation) — arguably 3 on a strict reading.** [10-Q] Note 12 discloses named pending matters (Slack IPO securities action; a growing set of trafficking-statute suits with a Seventh Circuit reversal against the company) that management calls immaterial — which is the literal wording of band 3 ("some pending matters disclosed but immaterial") rather than band 4 ("routine matters only"). The 2026-05-18 rationale was one word ("Clean."). Low confidence; worth a look at how peers with similar disclosure are rated.

**D2, D3, D5 — no evidence to move.** D2=3 ("mid-chain with some differentiation… switching has cost") still describes an application layer downstream of model vendors.

**D4 (capacity) — flag only.** The 4 rests on "Agentforce deal count scaling"; capital expenditure is ~1.5% of revenue ([PR] guidance). Whether sales-capacity hiring (15,000 account executives, +1,200 planned — [Call]) and ~$13B of acquisitions count as "capacity" is a Layer-10 convention question, not a CRM-specific one.

**M1=4, M2=2 — hold.** Six-month return +26.5% vs IGV +38.7% = −12 points → band 2 ([yf]); the three-month figure is +17.6 points ahead, so M2 is likely to move up if that persists. M1: revisions net positive but the FY27 jump is the gain. **M3 — not checked** (Form 4s not pulled).

**Objective inputs:** EPS-YoY blank is correct (§2.3). Revenue growth inputs (10.8% YoY) are acquisition-inflated; there is no rule for that, so it stands — but the Growth score is, if anything, flattered.

### Can a rating review close the ~11-point gap? No.

By the scoring rules (AI Thesis 20% on (mean−1)×25; Risk 15% on the same map; Momentum 10%), one notch on a single AI-Thesis dimension is worth 1.0 total point, one Risk notch 0.75, one Momentum notch 0.5. Raising **all five** AI-Thesis dimensions to 5 would add only 9 points. The upward moves that are even arguable (D1→3, R5→4, M2→3) total +2.25, and the evidence does not support the first two today. The moves the evidence does support (R3→3, possibly R4→3) subtract 0.75–1.5. The gap is structural: CRM scores ~78 on Value but ~58 on Quality (return on capital 8.8% on a goodwill-heavy base) and ~51 on Growth (≈10% reported, ≈6–7% organic), in a watchlist built around AI-infrastructure names growing far faster.
