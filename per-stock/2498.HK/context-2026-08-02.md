## 3. DIFF — where fresh reality departs from the prior model

### 3a. ★ THE ROBOTICS RAMP REVERSED IN Q2. Neither research pass caught this.
Both passes cite the **+510.4% H1 YoY** headline. The *sequential* arithmetic inside that same release tells a different story:

**Robotics units: Q1 2026 185,500 → Q2 2026 97,100 = −47.7% sequentially.**
**Robotics share of units: 56% in Q1 → 25.0% in Q2.**

So "robotics surpassed ADAS for the first time" (Q1) was followed immediately by robotics falling back to a quarter of volume while ADAS more than doubled sequentially (147,800 → 291,800). The +510% YoY is real but it is measured against a Q1-2025 base of ~11,900 units — an artefact of a tiny denominator, not evidence of a sustained ramp.

**Two candidate explanations, neither verified — do not pick one without the interim report:**
1. **Robotic-lawnmower channel fill.** RoboSense claims #1 share in lawnmowers; northern-hemisphere mower inventory is loaded in Q1. *Counter-evidence:* in 2025 the pattern was the reverse (Q1 ~11,900 → Q2 ~34,400 robotics units), so the seasonality story does not reconcile with the prior year.
2. **A one-off Q1 order/design-in event** that did not repeat.

Explanation 2 fits the data better, which is the less comfortable reading. **Flagged as the single most important open question for the 2026-08-13 interim report.**

**Minor data inconsistency (rule 3):** Q1 total (333,300) + stated Q2 total (388,900) = 722,200 vs the stated H1 total of 719,200 — a 3,000-unit gap between the two RoboSense releases. Same 3,000-unit gap appears in the ADAS derivation. Immaterial to the conclusion but noted.

### 3b. Robotics is a genuinely disclosed ~half of revenue — better evidence than Hesai has
RoboSense is the only name in the perception bucket with any published robotics **revenue** share: **~49% of Q4-2025 product sales revenue**. Hesai discloses units only. That makes RoboSense's D1/D5 evidence base *stronger* than Hesai's, even though its business is weaker.

Note the implication: robotics was ~32% of FY2025 *units* (303k of ~950k) but ~49% of Q4 product *revenue* → **robotics ASPs are materially HIGHER than ADAS ASPs at RoboSense.** This directly contradicts the machine-vision pass's assumption (applied to Hesai) that "robotics lidar carries a fraction of ADAS ASPs." At RoboSense the reverse holds, because its ADAS line sells into mass-market Chinese EVs at collapsing prices.

### 3c. Margins and cash burn are worse than the multiple suggests
Gross margin at 21.7% (Q1) is the lowest in the perception cohort and is *falling while volume triples*. FY2025 operating cash flow was **−CNY 581.8M** and free cash flow **−CNY 667.4M** — against CNY 3,039M of cash. That is ~3.7 years of runway at the FY2025 burn, but "reaffirmed full-year profitability" for 2026 requires a large second-half swing that has not yet been evidenced.

### 3d. Capex is small for a company guiding 2–3x volume
FY2025 capex of **CNY 85.5M on CNY 1,941M revenue (4.4%)** is light. Either the ramp is asset-light/outsourced, or the 2.0–2.7M unit guide leans on partners. Not resolvable from available disclosure — flagged.

### 3e. Information gaps I could not close this session (rule 3)

- **No accessible legal-proceedings disclosure.**

RoboSense is *not* named on the DoD list in any source I reached, but **absence of evidence is not evidence of absence** and this must be closed before the rating is trusted.

### DEBATABLE — flag for Dom
- **The Q2 robotics reversal (185,500 → 97,100 units, −48% sequentially) is the finding of this pass and it undercuts the reason RoboSense was included.** Both research passes led with "+510% robotics growth" and "robotics surpassed ADAS for the first time" — both true, both measured off a ~11,900-unit base, and both already reversed by the time the H1 release printed. If the 8/13 interim confirms this is a lost design-in rather than seasonality, D1/D5 should come down and the inclusion case needs re-examining. **I would not size this name before 8/13.**
- **D2 = 2 vs 3.** Giving a global #1 share-holder with its own silicon a 2 feels harsh. I went there because D2 measures *pricing power*, and RoboSense's gross margin fell while volume tripled — the cleanest possible evidence of none. If you read #1 share plus proprietary chipsets as conferring position regardless of current pricing, 3 is defensible.
- **R1 = 3 and R4 = 3 are low-information defaults, not judgments.** Two of the thirteen dimensions are effectively unrated. That is exactly the rule-12 gate condition — this name is not research-backed to the same standard as the US filers in this batch, and both should be revisited at the interim.
- **The 1260H status gap is the biggest single unknown.** Hesai's rating carries an R2 = 2 *because we know it is listed*. RoboSense may be cleaner (supporting a 3) or equally exposed. Rating it 2 on the asset leg alone is defensible either way, but the underlying uncertainty is larger than the rating suggests.
- **RoboSense vs Hesai on the same thesis.** RoboSense is cheaper (P/S 4.78 vs 5.73; EV/Sales 3.58 vs 4.19) with a *higher disclosed* robotics revenue share — but loss-making at every line, with half Hesai's gross margin, one-third the cash, no verified policy status, and a robotics ramp that just reversed. On the corrected Hesai market cap (see the HSAI briefing), the "one-tenth Hesai's multiple" framing in the research passes **is wrong by 8x** — the two names now trade within ~20% of each other on sales, and Hesai is the profitable one. **That comparison flips the relative preference and should be surfaced to you explicitly.**
