### Balance sheet and capital structure
- Sept 30 actual: cash + short-term investments $131.8B; total assets $154.3B; current liabilities $11.7B; convertible notes payable $130.5B (fair value); stockholders' deficit $(149.9)B (p.29, F-6).
- **Pro forma as adjusted for the IPO:** cash $232.4B, total stockholders' equity $128.9B (p.29). Add ~$12.2B if the 147M-share greenshoe was exercised (Yahoo's share count assumes it was).
- **Raise:** $81.3B IPO (956M primary + 24M secondary shares) + **$26.0B concurrent Class N private placement** at $83: Google $10.0B, NVIDIA $10.0B, AMD $2.5B, Broadcom $1.0B, Micron $1.0B, Microsoft $1.0B, TSMC $0.5B (p.~19).
- **Shares after offering:** 7.73B Class A (with greenshoe) + 5.64B Class N (non-voting, mainly strategic partners) + 1 Class T + 1 Class F = **13.37B** (p.20). Amazon holds 1.94B Class N (14.6% of capital stock); Google 1.77B (13.4%) (p.~21).
- **Amazon rollover notes:** $6.2B principal converting at $2.09–$5.05/share. Conversion is capped at 12% of capitalization, and Amazon chose not to waive the cap. The unconverted part (~1.55B shares if fully converted: 3.48B − 1.94B) stays a liability and converts quarterly at the lowest of fixed price, IPO price and 10-day VWAP as the cap allows. Unconverted principal is repaid in cash at maturity (2034) (p.172, p.~21). **This is ~$138B of latent dilution at today's price.**
- **Revolver:** $15.0B A&R senior secured credit facility (Sept 2026). No drawn balance was found in the prospectus text; treat as undrawn but unverified.
- **Governance:** PBC with a Long-Term Benefit Trust that elects directors via Class T stock; Class F stock carries voting control; Class N has no votes (p.~3, 6884+). Ordinary Class A holders have limited governance influence. 846.8M Class N shares (5% of fully diluted) went to the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned subsidiary, with a board-approved framework for **further contributions tied to future share-price increases** (p.~150). That is a standing dilution mechanism.

### Legal and regulatory (424B4 "Legal Proceedings", p.249–250)
- **Department of War "supply-chain risk" designation.** Feb 2026: after Anthropic said its models should not be used for mass domestic surveillance or fully autonomous weapons, the President directed federal agencies to stop using its technology and the Secretary of War designated it a supply-chain risk, barring military contractors from commercial activity with it. N.D. Cal. granted a preliminary injunction, then largely granted summary judgment for Anthropic on 2026-08-27 (First Amendment retaliation, due process, APA). **The D.C. Circuit, 2–1 on 2026-09-25, upheld the separate §4713 (FASCSA) designation.** Anthropic has petitioned for rehearing en banc; the government is appealing the N.D. Cal. judgment. **Unresolved.**
- **FTC civil investigative demand (Oct 2026)** on consumer protection, data protection, privacy and product safety/security. No claims asserted. **New since the last reported quarter.**
- **Copyright:** Bartz settled for $1.5B (approved July 2026). Other suits pending, including Concord Music (song lyrics) and international actions.
