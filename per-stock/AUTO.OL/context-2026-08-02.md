## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE searching)

Norwegian cube-storage ASRS — bots driving on top of a stacked grid of bins. IPO'd on Oslo
in Oct 2021 at NOK 31 backed by SoftBank/THL; the stock de-rated brutally. Reports in USD,
trades in NOK — the rule-19 trap. Sells almost entirely through a distribution-partner
network (Element Logic, Kardex, Bastian et al.), which is why gross margin is ~70%+ and
EBITDA margin ~40%+ despite being a hardware company: it ships modules, partners do
integration. Revenue ~$550–650M. 2023–2024 were a downcycle — warehouse-automation orders
fell hard after the e-commerce hangover, and 2024 revenue actually declined. Patent
litigation with Ocado was resolved.

**What I expected to be stale:** the 2026 reacceleration, order intake/backlog, whether the
RaaS/3PL framework model is scaling, and management changes.

## 6. Implications for ratings

- **The cleanest business model in the bucket.** 72.7% gross margin and 44% EBITDA margin on
  a hardware product is a genuine structural advantage created by the partner channel —
  AutoStore ships modules and lets integrators carry the project risk and working capital.
  That supports above-average D2/D3 and is the main reason to own it over Symbotic.
- **But it is not a bottleneck and it does not own the customer.** Substitutes exist
  (Exotec, Ocado, Dematic multishuttle, Chinese tote-to-person entrants), and the largest
  channel partner is a listed competitor. Cap D2/D3 at 4, not 5.
- **Capacity is not the constraint — demand is.** There is no disclosed multi-year capex
  program; the company spent 2025–26 cutting debt and refinancing rather than expanding
  plant. D4 should reflect that honestly.
- **Momentum is mediocre despite the operating inflection** — six-month relative performance
  is negative, order intake slipped sequentially, and the Q2 print lands Aug 13.
