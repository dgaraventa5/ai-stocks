## Pre-research mental model

RRC is a pure-play Appalachian (Marcellus) gas + NGL producer, ~2.2–2.3 Bcfe/d, roughly a third liquids, with multi-decade core inventory and a low full-cycle cost structure. Coming into this refresh my model was: (a) the AI link is second-derivative — datacenter-driven gas demand tightens the market and lifts realizations, but Range is a price-taker on NYMEX plus a structurally negative in-basin basis; (b) the only signed power-related contract is the 10-yr / 75 MMcf/d Midwest power-plant deal from Q4 2025, ~5% of gas volumes, zero revenue until the plant completes in late 2027, counterparty undisclosed; (c) Fort Cherry (Liberty Energy / Imperial Land alliance, April 2025) and NextEra's South Mon hub are optionality, not contracts; (d) balance sheet is in good shape after 1H26 deleveraging, with a partial hedge book that caps upside. My expectation for Q2 was continued NGL premium capture, a wider (seasonally normal) gas differential, and no new datacenter contract.

## Implications for ratings

Which dimensions the new evidence bears on, and why. **No rating numbers proposed** — this is the evidence, not the verdict.

**D1 (AI Revenue %) — most affected; evidence is unchanged-at-zero and now better documented.** Genuinely AI-tied share of current revenue remains **~0%**. Two more quarters of primary filings (Q1 PR, Q2 PR, Q2 10-Q) contain no datacenter revenue, no AI-linked segment, and no Item 1.01 contract. The one power contract (75 MMcf/d ≈ 4.8% of Q2 gas volumes at 1,548,871 mcf/d) starts **late 2027** and its end-use is undisclosed — and the "serves datacenters" claim attached to it in secondary sources is a documented conflation with Fort Cherry. Strictly: **gas sold into a generally tightening market is commodity beta, not AI revenue.** Additionally, the Q2 margin outperformance came from NGL export premiums, which are unambiguously non-AI — so to the extent D1 was rated on "AI is driving the business," the actual driver this quarter was something else.

**D2 (Supply-chain position) — affected, mildly negative-to-neutral.** Q2's −$0.47/mcf differential re-establishes price-taker status after the Q1 anomaly; FY guidance is still a discount. Counter-evidence has *strengthened* on the liquids side, though: a record +$3.49/bbl MB premium and two successive guidance raises are real, contract-structure-driven pricing power — but in NGL export markets, not in AI/power markets. The CFO's point that a sub-investment-grade producer can still win a bilateral 10-yr power deal is genuine but singular evidence. Whether D2 should reward pricing power that is real but AI-irrelevant is the judgment call for the rating session.

**D4 (Capacity expansion) — affected, points toward "maintenance-plus, deliberately deferred."** Capital budget unchanged at $650–700M; annual production guide unchanged; the raise is an exit rate; 2027 D&C capital to hold 2.6 Bcfe/d is guided at **<$600M**; growth beyond that is explicitly conditional on demand materializing in 2028; drilling is being "toggled" into 2027. This is not a funded multi-year buildout backed by customer commitments — it is optionality held in reserve.

**D5 (Hyperscaler / AI-buildout tie) — affected; the evidence base is unchanged and one prior data point weakened.** Still no named hyperscaler, no named datacenter developer, no disclosed MW, no offtake volume, no economics attributable to Range. Fort Cherry is now **16 months** post-announcement with pad-ready sites and PA SITES funding but **no named tenant**. The Midwest contract's datacenter linkage is unsupported by the primary source. Under the rule-23 "strength of contracted/quantified tie" framing, the contracted tie is one undisclosed-counterparty contract on ~5% of gas volumes with first revenue in late 2027.

**M1 (EPS revisions) — affected, positive.** Adjusted EPS $0.79 vs ~$0.69 consensus; revenue $833.6M vs ~$752M; three of four price-differential guides raised. This should push near-term revisions up. Verify against an actual revisions feed before rating.

**M2 (Relative strength) — affected, mixed.** +17% over 12 months and +15% over 6 months, but −5% over 3 months, and the objective 50DMA % sits at exactly **50.0%** (a middling band). Post-print reaction was modestly positive (+2.6% close-to-close).

**M3 (Insider activity) — check before rating.** Form 4 activity clusters at 2026-05-15 (six filings) and 2026-06-03 (two), with 144s in March. I did **not** parse transaction codes, so I cannot say whether these are open-market buys, sells, or routine vesting/withholding — that parse is required before touching M3. (Filing index: https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000315852.json.)

**R3 (Balance-sheet risk) — affected, net positive with a small caveat.** $600M of 8.25% notes retired; only $500M of fixed notes (4.75%, 2030) plus a $381M revolver remain; $1.5B liquidity; covenant compliant; facility runs to 2030. The caveat: net debt rose $47M in Q2 because shareholder returns outran a thin-FCF quarter, and the revolver is now the swing financing (floating at 5.4%), so a low-gas-price stretch would show up on the revolver first.

**R2 / R4 (geography, regulatory) — no new evidence.** Single-basin Pennsylvania concentration is unchanged; nothing new in the 10-Q's risk discussion.

**R5 (Disruption risk) — unchanged, defaults to 5** per rule 16 (non-Layer-10).

**Not a rating input, but flag for the session:** the strongest thing that happened to RRC this quarter (NGL export premium) is orthogonal to the AI thesis, and the AI-thesis evidence base did not move at all. If AI-Thesis ratings stay where they are while the business's actual driver is export arbitrage, that is exactly the uniform-bias failure rule 12 exists to catch.
