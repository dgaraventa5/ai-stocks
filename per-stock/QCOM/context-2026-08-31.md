## 1. Pre-research mental model

Qualcomm is a handset-concentrated licensing-plus-silicon franchise facing a long-telegraphed loss of the Apple modem socket as Apple internalizes. Diversification runs through automotive and IoT, with a Snapdragon X push into PCs that has underdelivered. My model treated Qualcomm's data-center ambitions as announcement-stage with no disclosed customers, and the QTL licensing stream as the durable earnings core.

## 5. DIFF vs prior mental model

- **The Apple loss is being realized now and is worse than the 20% share I assumed** — a −50% sequential step-down in Apple revenue into the December quarter is a discrete earnings event, not a slow bleed.
- **Data center went from vapor to named customers.** HUMAIN 200MW, plus two hyperscaler custom-silicon wins with Microsoft and Meta attached, with revenue starting fiscal Q1 2027. This is the single largest correction to my model.
- **Qualcomm bought a software moat.** I had no software-layer asset in my model. Modular/Mojo/MAX is a credible, if unproven, answer to "why would anyone port off CUDA."
- Automotive at +61% YoY is running far ahead of what I assumed.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flag only — do not pre-decide)

- **D1 (AI revenue %)** — still small today, but the FY2027 revenue start and the $15B FY2029 data-center target change the trajectory materially. Revisit.
- **D3 (moat)** — Modular is a genuine, non-obvious addition to the moat argument. Whether a hardware-agnostic stack owned by a silicon vendor gets adopted by rivals is the open question; do not credit it as settled. Revisit.
- **D5 (AI-buildout tie)** — HUMAIN 200MW plus two hyperscaler wins is exactly the contracted/quantified tie D5 asks for. Revisit; likely the strongest upward case on this name.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — genuinely two-sided. The Apple exposure that made R1 risky is shrinking, which *reduces* concentration risk over time while *causing* a near-term earnings hit. Do not conflate the two.
- **M1 (EPS revisions)** — the December-quarter Apple step-down points down; treat near-term revision direction as negative despite the long-range raise.

## ⚠️ Thesis-relevant signal

The Apple content loss is a **realized customer step-down**, not a rumor: below-20% share on iPhone 18 Pro and ~−50% Apple revenue September→December. It was long-telegraphed, so it is not a thesis *break*, but the magnitude exceeds the prior guided assumption and should be explicitly priced in the next rating pass.
