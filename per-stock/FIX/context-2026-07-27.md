## 1. Pre-research mental model (verbatim, written before any data pull)

> "FIX is a Houston-based mechanical/electrical contractor (~45 decentralized operating companies) that has become a near-pure-play on data-center construction: as of the Q1 2026 baseline in the repo, technology customers were 56.4% of revenue, backlog was $12.45B (+81% YoY) led by the Texas modular operation, gross margin had re-rated to ~26% (~25.2% core ex-closeouts), and FY2026 guidance was mid-to-high-20s% same-store growth with labor, not demand, as the binding constraint. The competitive position rests on modular fabrication capacity (expanding ~3M to 4M sq ft during 2026) and skilled-labor depth — capacity scarcity gives FIX multi-year volume commitments and pricing leverage, but not a structural moat. Risks I carry into Q2: tech customer concentration (simultaneously the thesis and the risk), heavy insider selling (~$152M trailing-12mo, zero buys), a reported ~43x forward multiple, and tougher H2 comps. The orchestrator says Q2 2026 (reported 7/23) delivered EPS $12.53 (~20% beat), revenue +50.3% to $3.27B, same-store backlog $8.12B→$13.70B YoY, and a dividend raise to $0.90 — I have not yet verified any of this or seen the mix, margin, closeout-benefit, or guidance detail underneath it."

(Minor correction found immediately: FIX now has 51 operating locations, not ~45 — 10-Q MD&A.)

## 2. Latest filing highlights

### Q2 2026 results (Ex. 99.1; 10-Q Results of Operations)
- Revenue $3,265.7M, +50.3% YoY — first quarter ever above $3B. Same-store +43.8%; +6.5% from Hunt/Feyen Zylstra/Meisner acquisitions. "Especially strong in the technology sector, particularly for data centers" (10-Q MD&A).
- Gross profit $844.2M = 25.9% of revenue (23.5% Q2'25). **No closeout/change-order benefit disclosed for Q2** — unlike Q1's ~$43.1M — so 25.9% is a clean print above Q1's ~25.2% core (10-Q Gross Profit).
- SG&A leverage: 8.8% of revenue vs 9.7% (10-Q SG&A). Operating income $558.0M = 17.1% (13.8%). Net income $441.6M; diluted EPS $12.53 vs $6.53 (+92%). Adjusted EBITDA $600.5M = 18.4% (15.4%) (Ex. 99.1 non-GAAP table).
- Segments: Mechanical $2.30B (+40.2%, GM 25.6% per call summary); Electrical $969.0M (+81.2%, of which $132.6M from acquisitions) (10-Q segment discussion).
- H1: revenue $6,131.0M (+53.1%), EPS $23.03 (vs $11.28), OCF $1,528.3M (vs $164.5M) (Ex. 99.1).

### Customer mix (10-Q, Revenue by Type of Customer)
- **Technology: $1,917.4M = 58.7% of Q2 revenue** (43.0% Q2'25); H1 57.6% vs 40.2%. Manufacturing 16.4%, Healthcare 7.1%; every non-tech category shrank as a share.
- New construction 75.1% of revenue (57.9% Q2'25); service lines now ~9.6% combined (10-Q Revenue by Activity Type).

### Backlog (10-Q Backlog section; Ex. 99.1)
- **$14.06B** at 6/30/26: +12.9% seq ($12.45B), +73.1% YoY ($8.12B). Same-store $13.70B, +68.7% YoY.
- Sequential drivers: Texas electrical +$1.00B, Texas modular +$510.2M bookings; partial offset −$118.5M at a NC operation on completed tech project work (first big visible roll-off).
- YoY same-store drivers: Texas modular +$1.87B, Texas electrical +$1.13B, a Texas op +$860.0M, NC +$671.9M, Indiana +$540.3M — still a handful of tech mega-operations.
- RPO $14.06B, 65–75% expected to convert within 12 months (10-Q Note, Remaining Performance Obligations). Backlog mix by customer type still NOT disclosed — gap persists.

### Cash machine / balance sheet
- Q2 OCF $1,139.4M (vs $252.5M); Q2 FCF $999.3M (OCF − $141.4M capex + $1.3M asset sales) ≈ 2.3x quarterly net income (Ex. 99.1 non-GAAP FCF table).
- Driven by advance payments: contract liabilities $3,231.1M vs $2,120.3M at 12/31/25 (10-Q contract liabilities note). CFO explicitly says this normalizes toward NI + non-cash items (call Q&A) — H1 FCF $1,241.5M is NOT run-rate.
- Cash $1,854.8M (vs $981.9M at 12/31/25), debt de minimis (current maturities $0.2M; revolver undrawn, $1.01B available, expires Oct 2030) (10-Q balance sheet + liquidity). 27th consecutive positive-FCF year intact.
- H1 capex $288.8M (vs $53.5M H1'25), incl. $188.9M of building purchases for modular growth (10-Q Investing Activities).
- Buyback nearly idle: <0.1M shares / ~$8.2M in H1 at avg $1,438.19 (10-Q repurchase note).

### Acquisitions / other
- **R.C. Hunt Electric** closed 5/1/26: $206.0M preliminary purchase price ($167.2M cash + $15.0M notes + earn-out), Utah, electrical segment, ~$250M annualized revenue per call Q&A (10-Q acquisitions note).
- Dividend raised to **$0.90**/quarter (+$0.10), payable 8/24/26 (Ex. 99.2).
- Tax: $29.8M R&D-credit refunds received July 2026; Q2 provision includes $5.3M benefit + $1.4M interest income (10-Q income tax note). One-time, small relative to $441.6M NI — does not trigger rule-15 blanking on its own.

## 5. DIFF: what changed since the 2026-06-12 baseline / training

1. **Guidance, not just results, stepped up:** mid-year raise from mid-to-high-20s to mid-to-high-30s same-store growth — H2 implied high-20s/low-30s against the toughest comps in company history. The Q1-era worry "H2 comps get much tougher" got answered with a raise.
2. **Concentration kept climbing:** tech 56.4% → 58.7% of revenue in one quarter; new construction 74.5% → 75.1%. The DC-pure-play framing keeps getting *more* literal.
3. **Margins are clean now:** Q2's 25.9% GM has no disclosed closeout benefit (Q1's 26.3% had ~150bps of it). Core margin actually improved ~70bps sequentially. Margin-regime-shift thesis strengthened.
4. **Modular race extended a year:** target moved from "4M sq ft by end-2026" to "~5M sq ft by late summer 2027," still commitment-gated. Capex intensity is real: H1 capex 4.7% of revenue vs ~1.5% historical.
5. **The cash flow quarter was an outlier by management's own admission** — $999M FCF in a quarter, 2.3x NI, driven by advance payments (contract liabilities +$1.1B YTD). Expect reversion; don't extrapolate TTM FCF-margin/FCF-yield inputs mechanically (rule 9 TTM caveat, in the flattering direction this time).
6. **New demand vectors appeared:** frontier-lab + colo pilot programs and hyperscaler service pilots — not yet material, but the first counter-evidence to "FIX is only hyperscaler new-build construction."
7. **First visible roll-off:** −$118.5M NC backlog on project completion — a reminder that the 18–24-month conversion clock forces continuous mega-project re-booking to hold backlog flat, let alone grow it.
8. **What did NOT change:** labor-scarcity framing, decentralized model, net-cash balance sheet (now $1.85B cash), dividend-raise cadence ($0.90, 5th straight raise), insider-selling overhang (article-level ~$59.7M/3mo, unverified), rich multiple with sell-the-news price action.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flags only — no scores assigned, no pre-decisions)

- **D1 (AI revenue %) / D2 (supply-chain position):** direction UP-pressure — 58.7% tech revenue, backlog led by modular DC bookings, guidance raise confirms conversion. Frontier-lab/colo pilots marginally broaden the buyer base.
- **D5 (hyperscaler exposure):** reinforced — demand conviction quote is literally about hyperscaler certainty; still no named-customer disclosure.
- **D3 (moat):** the debate sharpens: capacity-commitment model now extends to 2027 (multi-year commitments as a condition of expansion), but it remains scarcity-based, not structural. Both sides got stronger evidence this quarter.
- **Growth inputs (objective, rule 9):** refresh required — Q2 revenue +50.3%, EPS +92%, guidance raised. EPS YoY is operational (the $5.3M tax item is immaterial vs $441.6M NI) — no rule-15 blank indicated.
