## Pre-research mental model

Post-SanDisk separation, WDC is a hard-disk-drive pure play: a two-and-a-half-player oligopoly (WDC, Seagate, Toshiba) selling nearline capacity into hyperscaler data centres, historically a brutal cycle with commodity margins. My prior model held that AI data-lake growth was tightening supply and that HDD had been reprieved from the "SSDs eat everything" narrative because QLC NAND still can't touch HDD on cost-per-terabyte at scale. Most recent period I believed reported: fiscal Q3 2026. Product cycle: HAMR/UltraSMR areal-density transitions.

## DIFF vs prior mental model

- **I had the direction right and the magnitude badly wrong.** "Tightening supply" understates 54% gross margins, +44% revenue, and a 41.7% operating margin in a business I still mentally filed under commodity cyclical.
- **The cycle now has contractual duration.** Multi-year LTAs with named top-five customers through 2027/2028 change the character of the earnings stream — this is the single most important change since June and the main thing to test at the rating session.
- **The SSD-substitution risk I carried as the key structural threat is not visible in the data.** Supply is short by 300EB and widening; NAND is not absorbing nearline demand.
- **Pricing is the un-modelled upside.** ASPs at ~$14–15/TB against $25–30/TB targets is a gap I had no view on.
- **The GAAP earnings figures are not what they appear** — a ~$1.8B quarterly and ~$5.4B annual GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap that must be handled under rule 15.
- **Valuation moved from "cheap cyclical" to the 82.5th percentile of its own three-year multiple range.** The business inflected and so did the price — though less extremely than the pre-fix 91.8th-percentile reading suggested.

## Implications for ratings

*Research pass only — no rating values proposed here (rule 12).*

- **Rule 15 (Growth input)** — strongest mechanical finding: EPS YoY looks non-operating-dominated at both the quarter and full-year level. Read the 10-K reconciliation, identify the item, then blank and cite.
- **D1 (AI revenue %) / D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — hyperscaler forward orders at 85–95% of nearline capacity with LTAs into 2028 is about as contracted a tie to the AI buildout as a component supplier can have. Both dimensions have strong new evidence on the rule-23 parallel scale.
- **D2 (supply-chain position)** — the dimension most changed. Being sold out with multi-year customer commitments and ASPs far below stated targets is pricing power in the literal sense, not just favourable positioning.
- **D3 (moat)** — a three-supplier oligopoly with a multi-year capacity shortfall; the ten pending NPE patent dockets are noise-level by comparison but were not read.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — the flip side of D5: hyperscalers absorbing 85–95% of capacity with firm orders from the top seven customers is real concentration, currently expressed as strength.
- **R5 (disruption risk, rule 16)** — non-Layer-10, defaults to 5 under the rule-16 scope; nothing in this pass challenges the default (physical storage infrastructure, no agentic-AI vector).
- **M1 (EPS revisions)** — Q1 FY27 guided to +42–49% revenue growth and $4.00 non-GAAP EPS; the revision direction is up.
- **M2 (relative strength)** — mixed and worth pausing on: +78.4% over six months but −11.3% over three and currently below the 50DMA. Refresh `momentum_50dma.py`.
- **Expectations discipline (rule 14)** — the flag does not fire, and after the same-day fix the multiple sits at the 82.5th percentile rather than the 91.8th, so this is no longer a near-top-of-range name. The argument the briefing owes is unchanged: this is a genuine inflection with contractual duration, and the risk is that a supply cycle is being extrapolated as a secular re-rating.
