## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE pulling data)

Sandisk is the NAND flash business spun out of Western Digital in early 2025, operating the Yokkaichi
and Kitakami fabs through a long-standing joint venture with Kioxia. My model was that NAND is the
*less* AI-levered memory — HBM (an DRAM product) is the AI bottleneck, and NAND is a commodity with
a brutal cyclical history and consumer/client exposure. I expected Sandisk to be a cyclical recovery
story with enterprise SSD as a growing but secondary line, thin margins relative to DRAM players, and
capex constrained by the JV structure. I did **not** model a NAND supercycle.

**Full fiscal 2026:** revenue **$20.25B, up 175% YoY**; GAAP net income $11.43B ($73.76 diluted EPS);
non-GAAP diluted EPS $70.88. **Datacenter revenue up 437%.**

Sequential revenue growth composition, per the release: **~one-third volume, ~two-thirds price.**
This is the single most important sentence in the filing — the bulk of the growth is pricing, which
is the part that mean-reverts in a commodity.

An 84.6% gross margin in a NAND business is far outside any historical norm and is a pricing artifact,
not a structural cost position.

**New Business Model (NBM) agreements:** five additional signed since the April call (three with new
customers, two expansions), on top of the original five. Per prior disclosure, NBM contracts covered
**one-third of fiscal 2027 bit needs**, with **>$11B of enforceable commitments and ~$42B of
contracted revenue backlog**
([Yahoo Finance preview](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/buy-sandisk-stock-q4-earnings-163400602.html)).
These long-term agreements are the mechanism converting a spot-price spike into contracted revenue —
the key structural change on this name.

**Capital return:** Board approved an **additional $14B buyback**, bringing remaining authorization
to **$15.5B**.

**Q1 FY2027 guidance:** revenue **$10.30B–$10.80B**; non-GAAP diluted EPS **$44.00–$46.00** — another
large sequential step up.

### 2b. FCF conversion red-flag check
TTM FCF $4.46B ÷ TTM net income $4.51B = **99.0% conversion** (TTM revenue $13.18B — note this TTM
window lags the reported FY26 $20.25B because it blends pre-spike quarters). **Clean, not flagged.**

### 2c. Expectations red-flag check (rule 14)
`expectations_flag.py SNDK` → **clean**: P/S 13.64 = 82.8th percentile of own 3-year range; revenue
YoY +217.2% vs 3-year median +48.0%. No flag. But the honest framing the rule demands: SNDK's P/S is
computed against a revenue base inflated by a two-thirds-pricing-driven spike. If NAND pricing
normalizes, both the numerator (price) and the denominator (revenue) move against the multiple
simultaneously. **A "cheap" trailing multiple on peak-cycle earnings is the classic commodity value
trap**, and the expectations flag — built around P/S percentiles and revenue growth — is structurally
unable to detect it. Recording this as the main analytical caveat on the name.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flagging only — not pre-deciding)

- **D1 (AI-revenue %)** — revisit, likely **materially upward**. Datacenter +437% and a named
  AI-infrastructure demand driver (Stargate QLC) is the strongest D1 evidence shift in this rotation.
- **D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — revisit **upward**. ~$42B contracted revenue backlog and
  >$11B enforceable commitments is precisely the quantified, contracted evidence the D5 bands ask
  for. Verify these figures against the 10-K when filed before locking a rating on them.
- **D3 (moat)** — genuine tension. The Kioxia JV extension to 2034 plus BiCS10 is a real
  technology/supply moat; but NAND remains a commodity where the moat does not price the product.
  The NBM contracts are arguably the more durable moat than the technology.
- **R3 (balance sheet)** — revisit **upward**: $11.43B FY26 GAAP net income and a $15.5B buyback
  authorization transform the balance-sheet picture.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — check: NBMs concentrate revenue in a small set of large
  datacenter buyers. Better visibility, more concentration.
- **⚠ Cyclicality caveat that belongs in the rating session, not in a dimension:** the scoring rubric
  has no cycle-position input. Value metrics computed on peak-cycle TTM earnings will score SNDK as
  extremely cheap precisely at the point of maximum cyclical risk. This is the same
  garbage-input-family problem that motivated rules 10/13, and the same "Total Score peaks where
  expectations are most crowded" concern behind rule 14. **Flag for Dom as a judgment item** — I am
  not proposing a methodology change here.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh:** SNDK reported **2026-08-05 — within the one-week window, now due.**
  Revenue +51% QoQ and gross margin **+620bps sequentially** exceeds the >500bps trigger →
  **priority-1 (immediate).** Third priority-1 name this rotation, alongside GNRC and ALAB.
- **10-K pull:** queue when filed (fiscal year ended early July 2026).

**No thesis-break signals.** The cyclical-peak risk is a valuation and timing concern, not a thesis
break — the business is executing far above expectations.
