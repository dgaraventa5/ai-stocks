## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

**① The 20,000-component Army order is a *signal*, not a booked award — and this matters for D5.** Every source traces back to the same framing: the Army *"indicated plans to expand procurement, targeting an additional order of 20,000 components... in 2026."* I found **no 8-K, no 10-Q disclosure and no press release announcing an actual 20,000-unit award.** What is confirmed and delivered is the **3,500-motor 101st Airborne order** and a **$2.1M drone order**. This is the largest gap between the narrative and the filings on this name, and it must be flagged rather than assumed.

**② A new ~13% equity-comp overhang was created on 2026-07-24 — and it is simultaneously the strongest alignment signal in the cohort.** 5,000,000 CEO warrants at a **$25.00 strike** (~18% above the current $21.16), with vesting hurdles running to **$100**, in exchange for the CEO **waiving all cash compensation after 2026-12-31**; plus 1,275,000 executive options at $19.36. It is real dilution (13.1% of shares outstanding). It is also a CEO betting his entire cash compensation on the stock roughly doubling. Both facts are true and both belong in the record.

**③ Customer concentration is the best in the Layer-11 defense cohort — and that is the whole point of the position.** Top customer **16.7%**, second **15.9%** (FY2025 10-K), versus AVAV's DoD at 63%, RCAT's Customer A at 56% and KTOS's USG at 68.6%. Selling components to every OEM instead of owning one program is exactly what produces this, and R1 should reward it.

**④ Cash is larger than the roster figure and is being deployed.** ~$271.1M of cash plus short-term investments at 2026-03-31 (vs. the roster's $259M), with **$30M subsequently committed to Powerus** and an Orlando facility expansion underway.

**⑤ The company that sells NDAA compliance is not itself free of China.** UMAC's own 10-K risk factor concedes that *"certain specialized components may be available from a limited number of qualified suppliers, some of which are located in China"* and that rapid transition may not be feasible. This is a genuine thesis risk — but it is a **supply-side** dependency, and the R2 scope note says supply-side dependence is not an R2 leg, so it lives in the narrative rather than the rating.

**⑥ UMAC is the only name in this cohort with positive relative strength.** $21.16 versus ~$14.17 on 2026-05-22 and ~$18.57–$20.20 on 2026-03-17 (Form-4 prices), while KTOS, AVAV, RCAT and DroneShield all sit at or near 52-week lows. Whatever the multiple says, the tape is saying something different about UMAC than about the rest of the roster.

**⑦ Two of the three most senior operating officers sold heavily into that strength.** See §5 M3 — this is the counterweight to ⑥, and it is the most contestable rating here.

## 5. Rating implications — proposed 13 subjective ratings

### AI Thesis (D1–D5)

**D1. AI/Robotics Revenue % — 5**
- *Rationale:* **100%** of revenue is drone/robotics componentry — NDAA- and Blue-UAS-compliant motors, ESCs, flight controllers, cameras and VTX, plus precision-wound propulsion from Rotor Lab (Canberra). There is no non-robotics line.
- *Source:* FY2025 10-K business description and Item 2 Properties; 10-Q Q1 2026.
- *Confidence:* High.

**D2. Supply Chain Position — 4**
- *Rationale:* Genuinely upstream. UMAC supplies the propulsion and flight-control layer to essentially every US drone OEM rather than betting on one platform, and the position is protected by NDAA/Blue-UAS sourcing rules plus the **2027-01-01 prohibition on Chinese-origin rare earths in critical defense platforms** — a regulatory bottleneck with a hard date. Not 5: motors and ESCs are not a technical bottleneck in the ASML sense; the barrier is regulatory and qualification-based and can be replicated by other domestic entrants — and UMAC's own 10-K concedes some specialised components still come from China.
- *Source:* FY2025 10-K (supplier-concentration risk factor, business description); `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md` (rare-earth prohibition).
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**D3. Moat Strength — 3**
- *Rationale:* Modest moat, rated ruthlessly. What exists: Blue-UAS/NDAA qualification, the real switching cost of re-qualifying propulsion on a fielded airframe, and Rotor Lab's in-house winding capability. Not 4: the moat is a **policy artifact rather than IP** — if a domestic-sourcing mandate is what keeps competitors out, every new domestic motor entrant erodes it — and margins at scale have not yet been demonstrated (TTM net loss −$5.64M on $17.25M of revenue).
- *Source:* FY2025 10-K; 10-Q Q1 2026.
- *Confidence:* Medium. *Bias check:* would I rate this 3 if the stock were at $7.25 rather than $21.16? Yes — nothing in the moat argument depends on the price.

**D4. Capacity Expansion — 5**
- *Rationale:* Funded, disclosed expansion well ahead of demand: **~$271.1M of cash and short-term investments at 2026-03-31 with zero debt**, an Orlando facility expansion, a **$30M strategic investment in Powerus**, and Rotor Lab motor production in Canberra — capacity being built for an Army ramp that has not yet been ordered. This is committed capital on the balance sheet.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 balance sheet; [stockanalysis.com/stocks/umac](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/umac/) (Powerus, Orlando).
- *Confidence:* Medium-High.

**D5. AI-buildout tie — 4 · L11 lens (proposed)**
- *Rationale:* Strong **structural** tie — as the component supplier to all NDAA-compliant OEMs, UMAC is levered to the whole FY27 ~$75B drone/autonomy line rather than to any one program, and it is the direct beneficiary of the 2027-01-01 Chinese-rare-earth prohibition, which mechanically converts demand toward domestic motors. Held at 4 rather than 5 because the tie is structural rather than **contracted**: the only confirmed direct Army business is the delivered **3,500-motor** 101st Airborne order plus a **$2.1M** drone order, and the widely-cited "20,000 components in 2026" is an Army **signal**, not a disclosed award (see §4 ①).
- *Source:* ACCESS Wire 2025-10-15 (3,500 motors); [StockTitan $2.1M order](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/UMAC/unusual-machines-announces-2-1-million-order-for-drone-99vrk6zvbem4.html); FY2025 10-K.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

### Risk (R1–R5) — 5 = lowest risk

**R1. Customer Concentration — 4**
- *Rationale:* **Customer A 16.7% and Customer B 15.9%** of FY2025 total revenues (FY2025 10-K, Significant Concentrations) — top customer in the 15–25% band, and comfortably the best-diversified name in the Layer-11 defense cohort. This is the direct structural payoff of selling components to every OEM rather than owning one program. Not 5: top-customer share is above the 15% line, and a single Army decision (the signalled 20,000-component order) could push it well past 25% during 2026.
- *Source:* FY2025 10-K, Significant Concentrations note (verbatim in §2).
- *Confidence:* High.

**R2. Geographic / Export Risk — 5**
- *Rationale (mandatory named shock + % exposed):* The worst plausible geographic/policy shock is a **China export restriction on motor magnets and rare earths** — which for UMAC is a **demand tailwind**, since it is precisely why customers are switching to NDAA-compliant domestic motors. On the three scored legs: **no demand leg** (revenue is ~100% US/allied with no disclosed country concentration), **no policy leg** (no China revenue; no BIS/entity-list exposure), **no asset leg** (Orlando, FL plus a leased motor facility in **Canberra, Australia** — not a risk geography). Zero legs.
- *Source:* FY2025 10-K Item 2 Properties; FY2025 10-K Significant Concentrations; 10-Q Q1 2026.
- *Confidence:* Medium-High.
- *Narrative risk deliberately excluded from this rating:* UMAC's **own supply chain still touches China** (*"certain specialized components may be available from a limited number of qualified suppliers, some of which are located in China"*). Per the R2 scope note, **supply-side dependence is not an R2 leg** — it belongs in the thesis. It is a real risk and should be tracked; it is not scored here.

**R3. Balance Sheet Risk — 5**
- *Rationale:* Net cash with no debt: **$222,939,674 of cash plus $48,156,983 of short-term investments = ~$271.1M** at 2026-03-31, against **zero borrowings** and a TTM net loss of only **−$5.64M** — many years of runway at the current burn, and the cash was raised into strength rather than distress.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 balance sheet.
- *Confidence:* High.
- *Flag (does not change the band):* the 2026-07-24 grants add **~6.28M potential shares (5.0M CEO warrants at $25 + 1.275M executive options at $19.36) on 47.79M outstanding ≈ 13.1%** dilution overhang; the warrants are subject to shareholder approval and vest only on price hurdles of $25/$40/$60/$80/$100.
