## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE pulling data)

Fabrinet is a Thailand-based contract manufacturer of precision optics and optical transceivers —
not a designer. Its AI relevance is as the outsourced manufacturing arm for the optical
interconnect build-out, with NVIDIA and Cisco historically its largest customers and NVIDIA's
InfiniBand/Ethernet transceiver programs the key AI driver. My model was that FN carries classic
contract-manufacturer economics (low-to-mid-teens gross margin, high customer concentration, limited
pricing power) and that its growth is a levered but low-margin derivative of the 800G→1.6T optical
transition. I assumed extreme concentration risk with NVIDIA as the dominant customer.

### 2b. FCF conversion red-flag check — **the notable finding on this name**

TTM FCF $0.04B ÷ TTM net income $0.42B = **10.7% conversion** (TTM revenue $4.24B). That is far below
the 60% threshold, so I ran the full rule-2b conjunctive test rather than flagging on the headline:

| Fiscal year | Revenue | Net income | FCF | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 (Jun) | $3.42B | $333M | $207M | **62.1%** |
| FY2024 | $2.88B | $296M | $365M | 123.1% |
| FY2023 | $2.65B | $248M | $151M | **60.9%** |
| FY2022 | $2.26B | $200M | $34M | 16.8% |

Working capital vs revenue growth:
- FY25 vs FY24: revenue **+18.6%**, receivables **+28.1%**, inventory **+25.4%** → WC *is* outgrowing
  revenue.
- FY24 vs FY23: revenue +9.0%, receivables +11.4%, inventory −10.8%.

**Verdict: NOT flagged.** Rule 2b requires conversion <60% for **2+ consecutive fiscal years** AND
working capital outgrowing revenue. FN has never posted two consecutive sub-60% years (62.1 / 123.1 /
60.9). The 10.7% TTM figure is working-capital absorption from a revenue ramp that accelerated to
+39% YoY — exactly the false positive rule 2b's conjunctive structure exists to prevent, and the
expected signature of a contract manufacturer funding a customer ramp.

**But record it as a monitor item:** receivables growing 28% against 19% revenue growth in FY25, with
TTM conversion at 10.7%, means FN is financing a large share of this ramp off its own balance sheet.
If FY26 (reporting Aug 17) posts a second consecutive sub-60% year with WC still outpacing revenue,
**the flag fires** and this becomes a real earnings-quality question. Check this first on Aug 17.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flagging only — not pre-deciding)

- **D1 (AI-revenue %)** — revisit. Optical Communications at 73.2% and growing 35% YoY, driven by
  datacom/datacenter programs. But note the D1 definition asks for AI-driven revenue, and FN's
  optical segment also contains telecom — do not read 73.2% as the AI share without decomposing it.
- **D2 (supply-chain position)** — genuine tension worth deliberating. FN is a contract manufacturer:
  high volume, real precision-optics capability, but limited pricing power and no IP ownership. The
  direct-hyperscaler programs modestly strengthen the position.
- **D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — revisit. Direct hyperscaler programs ramping through FY2027
  plus merchant scale-out programs is better evidence than an NVIDIA-derivative framing.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — **do not re-rate until the 10-K lands Aug 17.** Direction is
  favorable (diversifying), but I have no current primary-source concentration figures. Rating this
  on secondary sources would repeat the pattern rule 12 exists to prevent.
- **R3 (balance sheet)** — check against the FCF conversion finding above. Not a flag today; a
  live question on Aug 17.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh:** **queue for 2026-08-17** (Q4 + full FY2026 + 10-K). This is the
  highest-value single upcoming datapoint in the rotation, because it resolves three open items at
  once: customer concentration, the second-year FCF-conversion test, and full-year margins.

**No thesis-break signals.** The FCF-conversion pattern is a monitor item with a defined trigger
date, not a break.
