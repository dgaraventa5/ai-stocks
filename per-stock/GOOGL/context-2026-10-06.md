### (5) Antitrust remedies (R4)

- **US Search (D.D.C., Mehta):** final judgment entered December 2025 — distribution restrictions, search-data sharing and syndication to "certain competitors"; Google appealed January 2026 and its stay motion was "denied ... as premature"; DOJ and states cross-appealed February 2026 (10-Q Note 10). Since the filing: Google's opening brief filed 2026-05-22; reply brief was due 2026-09-29; **oral argument not yet scheduled**; DOJ's cross-appeal still seeks stronger structural relief including Chrome (https://www.litigationlogic.io/legal-news/google-and-doj-both-challenge-judge-mehta-s-antitrust-remedies-order-at-dc-circuit-2026/, https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318733/20260620/google-antitrust-appeal-filed-dc-circuit-can-strengthen-data-rules-even-without-chrome-sale.htm). Remedies are in force while the appeal runs.
- **US Ad tech (E.D. Va., Brinkema):** the 10-Q (7/23) said "we are awaiting a final judgment" and that DOJ's proposal "includes structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect on our business". **Resolved in Google's favour on structure on 2026-09-02:** the court declined to order divestiture of AdX or the DFP ad server and adopted behavioural remedies — real-time bid data to rival ad servers, ending Unified Pricing Rules, per-bidder price floors, no first/last look (https://www.adexchanger.com/antitrust/google-wont-have-to-break-up-its-ad-tech-business-judge-brinkema-rules/). Secondary sources add Prebid interoperability and a six-year court monitor, with a jointly proposed final judgment due 2026-10-02 (https://corp-intl.com/news/us-judge-orders-six-year-antitrust-monitor-over-google-ad-te — not verified against the docket; the opinion was filed under seal). Google has said it will appeal the liability finding; whether DOJ appeals the remedy is unknown. The Texas state-AG ad-tech trial follows this judgment (10-Q).
- **EU DMA:** €890M fine 2026-07-23 with a 60-day cure period. **Google rolled out a redesigned EU results page on 2026-09-08**, placing third-party comparison services above its own units and stripping features such as live pricing; Google publicly called it a quality reduction; **the Commission has not said whether it accepts the redesign**, so the periodic-penalty question (up to 5% of average daily worldwide turnover) is open (https://beingguru.com/google-rolls-out-dma-search-overhaul-in-europe-and-calls-it-its-biggest-quality-drop-in-29-years/, https://www.techpolicy.press/the-high-stakes-behind-the-eus-890m-google-dma-fine/). Revenue effect: first visible in Q3/Q4 EMEA numbers; nothing measurable yet.
- **Other, from the 10-Q:** EU ad-tech €3.0B fine under appeal (guaranteed, $3.5B charged Q3 2025); Epic — joint motion to modify withdrawn July 2026, Google "is complying with the October 2024 remedies decision"; Play state-AG settlement awaiting final approval; private follow-on actions pending in the US and Europe.
