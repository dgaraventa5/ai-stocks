---

## 1. One-line thesis

> UiPath is the test case for whether agentic AI destroys robotic process automation or inherits it — and as of Q2 FY2027 the disclosed metrics point to inheritance, with retention *rising* while the market prices displacement.

---

## 2. Position in the AI supply chain

- **What they do:** Enterprise automation platform, historically deterministic RPA, now repositioned around Maestro as an orchestration and governance layer for agentic workflows — model-agnostic across Claude, GPT and self-hosted models.
- **Where they sit:** The control plane between enterprise systems and probabilistic models. Management's economic claim is specific and falsifiable: tokenless deterministic execution is cheaper than reasoning at every step, so orchestration remains valuable even as models improve.
- **Unique vs. commoditized:** Governance, auditability, on-premise and data-sovereignty deployment for regulated industries — the attributes that made the DoD choose UiPath explicitly. Third-party corroboration that others build UiPath into their own delivery stack: Databricks validated-partner status and the Deloitte Ascend embed.
- **AI revenue mix:** **Not disclosed.** 18 of the top 20 deals in the quarter included AI, and Maestro Case and Maestro Flow are shipped product — but there is **no agentic-specific revenue, ARR or consumption figure**, and management says pricing is still being experimented with (moving toward transaction/outcome-based with token costs bundled). Rated D1=2: some evidenced exposure, growing, not yet material or separately disclosed.

## 4. Pricing & demand

- **Pricing trend:** Unsettled by management's own account — the shift toward transaction/outcome-based pricing with token costs bundled is in progress. This is the single largest open question in the model.
- **Lead times / backlog:** ARR guided to $2.065–2.070B FY27. **Net new ARR $37M vs $31M a year ago**; net new ARR is guided to **decelerate in H2** against a $20M FY FX headwind.
- **Retention — the core evidence:** dollar-based **net** retention **109%, up 2pts YoY and 3pts YTD**; **gross retention 97%**.
- **Gross margin trend:** GAAP gross margin fell **82% → 80%**, because professional services runs at roughly **−94% gross margin** ($20.3M revenue on $39.4M cost). Coding agents cutting implementation effort ~60% is deflationary to that line — ambiguous, since the line loses money.

## 6. Moat

- **IP / process know-how:** Large deployed automation estate and the orchestration/governance layer built over it.
- **Scale advantage:** Modest; partner-delivery ecosystem (Accenture, Deloitte) is the real distribution asset.
- **Switching costs:** **The strongest evidence in the file — 97% gross retention.** Automations are embedded in customer operating processes.
- **Network effects:** Weak.
- **Regulatory / geographic:** Genuine and underrated — on-prem and data-sovereignty deployment for regulated industries and the DoD is a segment hyperscaler SaaS cannot trivially serve.

**Moat rating:** Narrow, rated D3=3. Pro: 97% gross retention, >$1M cohort +21%, DoD selecting on governance, Rule 14 LLC patent suit terminated 2025-09-09. Against: gross margin compressing on services-heavy delivery, **R&D cut 15% during a platform pivot** (which is also why D4 was moved down to 2), and Microsoft's price advantage.

## 9. Risks

- **Thesis-killer #1:** Gross retention breaks below ~95% — the first place genuine agentic displacement would appear.
- **Thesis-killer #2:** The agentic pricing transition destroys more seat/bot revenue than the new model captures, so ARR growth stalls even with customers retained.
- **China / export controls:** Not material. The quantified geographic exposure is currency: a **$20M FY27 FX headwind ($18M in H2)**, ~300bps of translation drag; accumulated OCI fell $36.6M → $14.7M over six months. Rated R2=4 — a translation exposure, not a risk-geography or asset-location shock; **no geographic revenue breakout pulled this pass**.
- **Cyclical risk:** Automation budgets are discretionary and back-half weighted.
- **Technology substitution:** The whole debate. Rated **R5=3** — contested, but a structural moat (governance, sovereignty, regulated industries, DoD) resists core disruption, and rule-16 band 1 requires *visible active erosion*, which the disclosed metrics contradict. **The countervailing "LLM agents kill RPA" literature traces overwhelmingly to competing AI-agent vendors' own marketing** (Kognitos, Shogo, moveo, tendem), with cost and displacement claims I could not trace to any primary source — near-zero evidentiary weight.
- **Regulatory / governance:** Dual-class structure at **35 votes per Class B share** (~11% of shares → ~89% of votes), with >99% say-on-pay support. Two 2024 securities/derivative matters remain **PENDING** in S.D.N.Y. — allegations, not findings. Rated **R4=3** (corrected this pass from 5, for consistency with MDB on materially the same facts).
- **Insider activity:** Daniel Dines sold 1.40M shares at ~$16.07 on 2026-08-19 under a 10b5-1 plan, retaining ~26.5M (~5% of holdings) — routine under the rubric. Recorded but not over-weighted: **0 open-market buys across 109 transactions over five years**, including through the $9–11 lows. An absence-of-buying observation, which the project's own asymmetry rule treats as the weaker signal. Rated M3=3.

## 10. The "inverse the thesis" test

1. **If agentic AI is inheriting rather than destroying RPA, gross retention stays ≥95% and the >$1M-ARR cohort keeps growing double-digits through FY28.** If either breaks, the displacement thesis is right and R5=3 / D3=3 are both too generous.
2. **If the orchestration layer is defensible, UiPath discloses a growing agentic revenue or consumption line within four quarters.** If AI stays permanently un-quantified while "AI attached to the deal" is the only evidence offered, the attach is marketing and D1=2 is too high.
