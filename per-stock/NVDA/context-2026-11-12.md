## 5. DIFF vs mental model, and what the market is now pricing

| Prior belief | Fresh evidence | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| Huang is CEO; succession not a live issue | Resigned 2026-11-11 under board and congressional scrutiny; CFO is interim CEO; outside search under way | **Very large.** Key-person event for the most founder-identified company in the universe. |
| Regulatory = DOJ Groq probe + routine antitrust | Board-commissioned foreign-intelligence investigation, House testimony, a Senate subpoena threat, customer export-diversion probes (Megaspeed; SDNY/Supermicro) | **Large.** NVIDIA is not a named target, but its export-compliance program is now under political examination. |
| Q3 report ~11-19/20 | Confirmed 11-17 | Small. |
| Stock lagging, ~10% off highs | −32% from the high, −25% in 16 days, P/S 0.1th pctile of 3-yr range, ~10x forward EPS | **Large.** |
| China ≈ 0 | Still 0; 600k H200 stuck in Hong Kong | Small. |

**What changed in the capitulation argument.** On 10-27 the low multiple had four *business* causes (peak-cycle discounting, vendor financing, margin peak, share erosion). Today a fifth is layered on top and it is not a business-model fact: a **leadership and governance discount** plus an **export-compliance overhang**. The two cut differently:

- **Leadership.** The product roadmap is set through Rubin and its successor; one analyst calls it "solidified through 2028" (CNBC live). The CUDA ecosystem, supply commitments and customer contracts don't depend on one person in the next 4–6 quarters. Where Huang mattered most is the long-horizon bets: the capital allocation behind $108.5B of guarantees, $366B of commitments, OpenAI and Anthropic stakes, Groq and Hugging Face. An interim CEO who is also the CFO is a caretaker. Expect fewer new bets and more scrutiny of the existing ones, which may *reduce* the vendor-financing worry rather than add to it.
- **Export compliance** is the part that could become a revenue or penalty fact. It is a known pathway: Supermicro was subpoenaed, and Megaspeed is NVIDIA's largest SE-Asian customer. No charge or formal target status is reported. Exposure to Megaspeed alone (~$2.4B a year on the NYT figure) is under 1% of the ~$412B FY27 consensus revenue, so the risk is penalty, licensing and political, not lost demand.

**My read.** The 0.1th-percentile multiple now reflects a real, identifiable event, so this is a *weaker* capitulation setup than on 10-27, not a stronger one: the market has a concrete new reason. But nothing yet touches reported demand, and consensus estimates haven't moved. The falsifiable tells are near-dated:
1. Q3 print and Q4 guide on 11-17: is demand still supply-constrained?
2. Whether estimates fall after the print.
3. Whether any customer comments on orders.
4. The scope of the Wachtell findings and of the House testimony.
5. Whether any agency names NVIDIA rather than its customers.
