## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

Novanta is the one name in the whole robotics screen with **audited, disclosed robotics revenue** — Robotics & Automation was 32.5% of FY2025. It owns ATI Industrial Automation, the merchant leader in six-axis force/torque sensors (McKinsey names this a humanoid bottleneck; the only other credible merchant, OnRobot, is private), plus Celera Motion frameless motors and encoders. It has a $50M/3-year warehouse-automation design win with "the world's largest e-commerce player" and a Varo force/torque sensor for humanoids. The bear case is valuation (34–38x forward) against only 3% organic growth — bookings up 35–50% that have not converted. I expected the business mix to be stable and the balance sheet to be lightly levered.

**The mix and the balance sheet both moved materially in the last eight weeks. I did not know about the deal.**

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

1. **Novanta just spent $1.45B (incl. the milestone) to become a medical company.** Medical goes to **60% of revenue**; robotics & automation, 32.5% of FY2025, becomes roughly **26–27% pro-forma** (solving 0.529×1.05B + X)/(1.05B+X) = 0.60 implies Riverpoint ≈ **$185M** of revenue, so R&A ≈ $330M / ~$1.23B). The 10-K states the mix shift is *deliberate strategy*. **A Layer-11 rating on NOVT is now rating a shrinking share of the company.**
2. **The balance sheet flipped from net cash to net debt in three weeks.** Net cash ~$147.4M at 2026-04-03 → consolidated debt **$854.7M** at 2026-07-22, plus **$250M** payable by 2027-01-08. Pro-forma net debt ≈ $700–1,000M against pro-forma EBITDA ≈ $310M → roughly **2.3x–3.0x**. This alone moves R3 down a band.
3. **The 10-K does say "humanoids."** The deep-pass SEC full-text screen reported **0 "humanoid" hits for NOVT** — that is wrong on the primary source. The FY2025 10-K lists humanoids as a named application for Robotic and Precision Motion. Flagging per rule 3.
4. **Insider selling is genuinely routine here** — unlike RRX. All 10b5-1, across three people, none discretionary.
5. **Short interest is 14% of shares outstanding** — the highest in this six-name group by a wide margin, and a fact I did not have. That is a crowded bear position into an Aug 5 print that has to explain a $1.2B medical acquisition and $300M of dilution.
6. Confirmed as expected: organic growth still only 3.1% while bookings run +37%; the conversion gap is the whole debate.

## 5. Rating implications

- **D1** stays band 4 on the audited FY2025 32.5% but sits at the **very bottom of the band** pro-forma (~26–27%) and the trajectory is down by explicit corporate strategy. If Riverpoint is larger than my $185M estimate, this becomes a 3 at the next refresh — flag for the Aug 5 print, where management will quantify it.
- **D2/D3** are the strongest in this six-name group: ATI is a genuine merchant bottleneck with one private competitor, and the 10-K's "long life-cycle OEM customer platforms" language plus a 12%-of-revenue anchor medical OEM evidence real switching costs.
- **D5 (L11 lens)** is the best-evidenced robot-program tie in the group: a **named, contracted $50M/3-year** warehouse-automation win with the world's largest e-commerce player deploying from 2026, plus the Varo humanoid F/T sensor shipping. But at ~1.6% of revenue per year it is not a band-5 exposure.
- **D4** is a funded, committed, multi-year capital deployment — but into **medical consumables, not robotics**. Rate the company's build-out cycle honestly and note where the money went.
- **R3** must reflect the deal, not the April balance sheet.
- **M3** is the clean case for the 10b5-1 → routine → default 3 path.
- **Rule-9 flag:** refresh after 2026-08-05. Riverpoint's 2026 contribution, the restated segment mix, and pro-forma leverage all land on that call.
