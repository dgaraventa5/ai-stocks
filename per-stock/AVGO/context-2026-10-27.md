# AVGO — event briefing 2026-10-27 (daily-watch): second AI-rack residual value backstop

**Source:** 8-K filed 2026-10-27, Item 2.03 (acc. 0001193125-26-434271, [EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1730168/000119312526434271/d148323d8k.htm)), read in full from the primary document.

## 1. What the filing says

- On 2026-10-26, Broadcom arranged for financial partners to take on part of its agreements to buy AI racks built on Broadcom custom accelerators, plus the related lease agreements with **"the Company's customer"** (singular, not named).
- Broadcom provides **residual value support to a newly formed SPV** ("the Backstop"). Broadcom describes it as a contingent liability with a low probability of occurring.
- **Liability formula:** Broadcom owes the gap between **72.5% of the outstanding lease balance** and the value of the AI racks when they are sold, if the customer defaults. The exposure grows as racks are deployed and shrinks as the customer pays down the lease.
- **Maximum potential liability, undiscounted, once all racks are deployed: about $43 billion.**
- If the customer defaults, Broadcom can limit its loss by taking over the lease, selling the racks back to the seller at a fixed price (under conditions), or arranging a sale.

Only the theoretical worst case, with a customer default **and** zero rack recovery, reaches the band-3 line (ND/EBITDA 2–4x). On any realistic recovery value, leverage stays in band 4 (<2x). **No proposal written.** This stays a question for Dom (first raised 2026-09-09): should R3 treat the guarantees as debt-like the way S&P does? Under that convention the answer depends on recovery assumptions, not on the bands.

The concentration point matters more than the leverage arithmetic. Both backstops appear to hang on **one** private counterparty's ability to pay its leases. That ties AVGO's balance-sheet risk to a frontier-lab credit, which R1 (customer concentration, current 3) only partly captures.
