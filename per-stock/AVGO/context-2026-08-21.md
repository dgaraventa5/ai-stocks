## 3. WebSearch findings

### 3a. The Marvell/Google warrant — RESOLVED: predominantly incremental, *not* displacement

**The two research passes disagreed on this and I am resolving it in favour of the incremental read.** One pass concluded "a genuine D2/D3 negative"; the other concluded "predominantly incremental." I side with the latter because its evidence is specific and primary-adjacent (product-scope language, BofA's BOM teardown, the Sunfish/Zebrafish split) while the bearish read rests substantially on **Macquarie's 95%→65% share path — which is a *June* number driven by MediaTek, recycled in post-deal coverage without that context.** Reasoning:

1. **Scope is *attach* silicon, not the compute die.** Marvell's disclosed scope: "custom silicon programs that **attach to** the TPU ecosystem, including AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers, memory interface controllers, and near-memory compute" ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/marvell-google-tpu-custom-silicon-120-billion/), [Evertiq](https://evertiq.com/design/2026-08-20-marvell-and-google-expand-custom-silicon-partnership)).
2. **Google's compute-die second source was already MediaTek — since June.** TPU v8 is split: Broadcom **"Sunfish"** (training) / MediaTek **"Zebrafish"** (inference), both TSMC 2nm ([wccftech](https://wccftech.com/google-splits-tpuv8-strategy-two-chips-broadcom-training-mediatek-inference-duties/)). Digitimes' framing: *"Google keeps Sunfish at Broadcom, Zebrafish at MediaTek — Marvell's US$12.2bn warrant buys a category nobody was defending"* ([Digitimes 8/20](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260820VL209/marvell-google-broadcom-mediatek-tpu.html)); follow-up: *"keeps Broadcom at bay, **limits MediaTek impact**"* ([Digitimes 8/21](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260821PD211/marvell-google-mediatek-broadcom-chips.html)). Marvell is the **third or fourth** partner in an already-diversified chain.
3. **BofA's BOM teardown:** Marvell won peripherals; **core compute stays with Broadcom** — core ASIC, advanced packaging, HBM integration, high-speed SerDes, switching/PHY, which BofA calls the highest-value portions of the TPU system BOM ([techflowpost](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/33395)).
4. **The $12.2B headline is an incentive ceiling, not an order book.** Warrant issued 2026-08-18: 58,970,907 shares @ $206.58 (~$12.18B, ~7% of MRVL shares out), exercisable to 2033-08-18. 1,360,867 shares (2.3%) time-vest over year one; the other 57,610,040 (97.7%) vest in **240 tranches, one per $500M of Custom Products revenue on *discretionary* Google purchases**, from MRVL FQ3 2027 to end-FY2033 → **full vest requires $120B cumulative**. Reporting states **no minimum purchase, no volume floor, no exclusivity** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/20/google-s-marvell-warrant-doesn-t-fully-vest-until-google-buys-usd120-billion-of-chips/), [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/marvell-attaches-across-googles-tpu-stack-with-a-warrant-vesting-toward-120b/)). It signals the *scale of pie Google contemplates for attach silicon* — not volume taken from Broadcom.
5. **Sell-side did not cut AVGO.** JPMorgan (post-deal): April agreement secures **v8–v11**, "market underestimates Broadcom's 18-month+ lead" ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/reiteration/26/08/61336169/broadcoms-ai-competition-fears-look-overblown-analyst)). BofA (post-deal): AVGO **55–60%** of a $400–600B 5-yr TPU value pool; MRVL 10–20%. Morgan Stanley (pre-deal, 7/14): AVGO keeps **~80%**, OW $502. Morningstar: *"a growing pie at Google for new sources, rather than a competitive displacement."* **No post-news AVGO estimate or PT cuts were found**; consensus 23 Buy / 3 Hold, avg PT $511–516. *(Caveat: absence of cuts within 48h is weak evidence — sell-side often waits for the print.)*
6. **Hock Tan pre-announced it.** Q2 call, 2026-06-03: *"we fully expect that there will be some diversity of sources for them."* Already captured in `thesis.md` §3.

### 3b. The financing structure — this is where the new risk actually is

**The ~$370B figure is not Broadcom's debt.** It is BofA **credit** analyst Tom Curcuruto's projection of the senior debt stack *inside the AI XPV Platform* at 20 GW scale by mid-2029 (incl. ~$150B new issuance in 2027) ([Benzinga 8/14](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/08/61219624/broadcom-ai-financing-370-billion), [247wallst 8/14](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/14/broadcom-sinks-6-as-bofa-flags-370b-in-ai-debt-amd-climbs-4-on-bairds-1250-call/)). AVGO fell 5–6% on it.

**What Broadcom actually owes:** it **backstops the lease payments** via a **five-year residual value guarantee (RVG)**. On the initial $35B transaction, max exposure is reported **up to ~$29B**. BofA's own loss math: all-customers-default at 20 GW ≈ **$42B**; at a 25% default rate ≈ **$10.5B**.

**The part that matters for R3:** **S&P is reported to treat the residual-value support as a "contingent debt-like obligation" and add it to adjusted debt**, and tagged the first AI tranche **credit negative** (AVGO carries A−) ([cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/sp-broadcom-ai-tranche-credit-negative/)). BofA cut its **credit** view to **Marketweight** (~8/11) — note this is the credit desk; BofA's equity analyst remains constructive. **These are different desks answering different questions and should be kept separate in the file.**

**The new deal (2026-08-20/21) is unsigned and its size is disputed:**

| | Bloomberg 8/20 | CNBC/Faber 8/21 |
|---|---|---|
| Senior secured | ~$60–70B | ~$45B |
| Junior | ~$30B | ~$35B |
| **Total** | **up to ~$100B** | **~$70–80B** |

CNBC says "the numbers are fluid"; Broadcom, Anthropic, Apollo and Blackstone **all declined to comment**; no 8-K or company release found. **Defensible range $70–100B; do not model a point estimate.** Broadcom would guarantee **"a portion"** of the senior tranche — **the fraction is undisclosed in every source found, and it is the number that determines actual balance-sheet risk.**

**Credit-market reaction, in-window and material:** **Broadcom CDS spreads rose sharply to record levels this week** per LSEG data ([Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/big-tech-credit-risk-flashes-192645668.html)). Counterparty context: **Anthropic has assembled ~$71B of chip-lease SPV debt in ~60 days, off its own books, with Broadcom backstopping senior tranches** ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-spvs-stack-71-billion-000514097.html)).

*(Terminology conflict flagged: sources describe the first tranche's assets as both "Google TPUs" and "Broadcom custom XPUs" — reconcilable since AVGO co-designs Google's TPUs, but the reporting is loose. And the $370B-vs-$42B framings cannot all be right; needs the actual BofA note or the 10-Q footnote.)*

### 3c. VMware CVE-2026-59310 — **my weekly-scan characterisation was wrong**

I called this a "zero-day" in `weekly-news-scan-2026-08-21.md`. **It is not.** The patch (2026-07-29) **preceded** exploitation (first callbacks 2026-08-03) by five days — this is n-day mass exploitation of an unpatched estate, which matters for liability and for how it reads in a thesis. CVSS 9.8 path traversal in the vCenter Syslog server → unauthenticated RCE; **no workaround, patch only**. 361 compromised IPs / 47 countries, 343 of them by 8/05; `.babyk` (Babuk-derived) encryption assessed as a forensics smokescreen; attribution to a suspected Chinese-nexus actor at **moderate** confidence only.

**The genuinely in-window item: CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 2026-08-18**, federal remediation deadline **2026-08-21** under BOD 26-04 ([CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog?field_cve=CVE-2026-59310)).

**Materiality: low as an independent driver.** No litigation and no customer-loss announcement tied to this CVE were found. But it lands on a live VMware churn narrative and alongside an **open EU antitrust/licensing matter** — on 2026-08-03 the EU General Court President **dismissed** Broadcom/VMware's application to suspend a Commission information request in the VMware licensing investigation (pre-window, no update since). Competitor-sourced churn figures (Nutanix's "~30,000 migrated") are self-serving and should be discounted. **Log to news-log; do not adjust Infrastructure Software assumptions on the CVE alone.** Q2 FY26 Infra Software was $7.18B, +9% YoY, 93% GM — but **missed** the $7.32B consensus, which is the thing actually worth tracking.
