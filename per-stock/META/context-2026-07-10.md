## 2. What "Meta Compute" actually is (WebSearch findings, cited)

1. **The report (2026-07-01, Bloomberg):** Meta is building a cloud business to sell access to
   AI computing power and models, in direct competition with AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud. An
   internal org, **"Meta Compute,"** oversees the buildout/operation of Meta's AI infra, led by
   **Santosh Janardhan** (head of infrastructure), **Daniel Gross** (Meta Superintelligence Labs),
   and **Dina Powell McCormick** (Meta President). Stock jumped ~6% premarket 7/1 (closed the day
   up ~8.8–9.7% per the 7/3 scan). — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-01/meta-is-building-a-cloud-business-to-sell-excess-ai-compute),
   [Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-building-cloud-business-sell-131712263.html),
   [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/meta-like-spacex-looks-to-turn-excess-ai-compute-into-cash/)

2. **Two models under consideration** (not yet chosen): (a) let outside developers pay to run
   queries against hosted AI models — *including Meta's closed-weight Muse Spark* — on Meta-owned
   infra (a Bedrock-style layer); or (b) sell raw capacity the way neoclouds like CoreWeave do
   (bare-metal GPU rental). — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/meta-goes-the-way-of-xai-considers-renting-computing-power-as-own-model-flails-2000780274),
   [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/meta-reportedly-plans-to-rent-out-its-ai-compute)

3. **Why:** the surplus exists because Meta's 2026 AI-infra spend ($125–145B, up from ~$72B in
   2025) is likely more than its own products need; selling the excess offsets some of the cost
   while demand is high. Same logic TechCrunch draws to SpaceX/xAI monetizing spare compute. —
   [cloudcomputing-news](https://www.cloudcomputing-news.net/news/meta-ai-cloud-business-excess-compute/)

4. **Management has telegraphed this, so it's not pure speculation:** at Meta's May 2026
   shareholder meeting Zuckerberg said entering cloud computing was **"definitely on the table,"**
   adding that companies approach Meta **"almost every week"** to buy access to its AI models or
   spare compute. So there is real *inbound* demand per management — but no signed resale revenue
   and no announced pricing/launch date. — [thenextweb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-zuckerberg-ai-cloud-business-makes-sense),
   [techrepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-meta-ai-compute-cloud-business/)

5. **Analyst framing / size:** estimates that selling ~15% of total GPU capacity as spot/reserved
   could generate **~$5B/yr with minimal incremental cost** — but cloud is a *lower-margin
   services business Meta has never operated*, and skeptics note the hyperscalers' moat is
   enterprise lock-in (Entra/AD, M365 adjacency, compliance, procurement), not GPU landlording.
   So the incumbents' core isn't trivially contestable. — [finance.yahoo/Barron's](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-wants-sell-ai-compute-171055420.html),
   [fierce-network](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/metas-cloud-play-may-hinge-bare-metal-not-winning-full-stack-fight)

6. **Confirmation event:** Meta's **Q2 2026 earnings are July 29, 2026** (after close) — the first
   scheduled venue where management could formalize or walk back the plan. —
   [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-7-29-facebook-inc-stock/)

## 3. ⚠️ Correction to the 2026-07-03 briefing (Rule 3 — flag contradictions, don't propagate)

The 07-03 weekly scan (and its Notion page) stated as established fact that **"Meta discloses it
already resells capacity today — Anthropic pays Meta ~$1.25B/month, Google pays ~$920M/month."**
Fresh searches this pass attribute those exact figures to **SpaceX**, not Meta:
**Anthropic pays SpaceX ~$1.25B/month** for the Colossus 1 data center (Memphis), and
**Google pays SpaceX ~$920M/month** for compute. Multiple current sources state Meta **does not
currently rent or sell compute capacity** — it is *exploring* it. —
[TechCrunch (Google→SpaceX $920M)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/05/google-will-pay-spacex-920m-per-month-for-compute/),
[techrepublic (Meta does not currently sell)](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-meta-ai-compute-cloud-business/)

**Implication:** Meta Compute is a *prospective* revenue line with **zero disclosed run-rate
today**, not an existing business being scaled. This materially lowers how much weight the rating
session should give it right now. Treat as an option, not a segment. (I could not fully reconcile
against the original 07-03 source since Bloomberg is paywalled to WebFetch — flagging the
discrepancy rather than silently overwriting, per rule 3.)

## 5. Implications for ratings (flags only — no pre-decided scores; for the human session)

Layer-9 hyperscaler dims. Current META ratings: **AI-Rev%(D1)=3, Position(D2)=3, Moat(D3)=5,
Capacity(D4)=5, Hyperscaler-tie(D5)=5; Momentum EPS-Rev 5 / RelStr 1 / Insider 3 / 50DMA 35.8;
Risk CustConc 4 / Geo 4 / BS 4 / Reg 2 / Disrupt(R5)=5.**

- **D1 (AI-Rev %) = 3 → watch, do NOT move yet.** Today META's AI revenue is ad-*derived*
  (indirect), which is why it sits at 3 not 5. A real Meta Compute revenue line would be *direct*
  AI revenue and would argue D1 up-pressure — but there is **no revenue, no pricing, no launch
  date, and the "already reselling" basis was a misattribution (§3).** Revisit only if the July 29
  Q2 call quantifies it. Rating on the rumor would be exactly the "priced-in / consensus-crowded"
  trap rule 14 warns about.
- **D2 (Position) = 3 → mild up-pressure, defensible either way.** Meta shifting from pure buyer
  to buyer+supplier of compute modestly strengthens its supply-chain position (more of the stack
  captured). But it's entering a business it has never run against entrenched incumbents whose
  moat is enterprise lock-in, not GPU supply — so "position" gains are speculative. Discuss.
- **D5 (Hyperscaler/buildout tie) = 5 → no change.** Already maxed as a buyer ($125–145B capex).
  Becoming a seller doesn't raise a capped dimension; rule 23's D5 is "strength of AI-buildout
  tie," and Meta's is already max-for-role.
- **Risk — two-sided, net probably neutral now:**
  - *Balance-sheet/durability (BS Risk = 4):* capex monetization is a **mild positive** — a path
    to offset the $237B commitment book / rising debt load flagged 6/12. But cloud is
    lower-margin and unproven for Meta; execution risk offsets. Net: no clear move.
  - *R5 Disruption Risk = 5:* unchanged — META is Layer 9, R5 defaults to 5 (rule 16); Meta
    Compute doesn't expose Meta's *own* revenue model to AI disruption.
- **Momentum — positive catalyst, but one event.** Stock +~9% on 7/1; but 50DMA% was 35.8 (weak
  trend) and RelStr = 1 at the last rating. This is a genuine EPS-narrative catalyst; whether it
  sticks depends on the July 29 print. Refresh `momentum_50dma.py` when yfinance returns before
  re-rating Momentum.

**Thesis-note action (independent of any score change):** the one-line thesis in `thesis.md`
("rather than selling AI as a cloud service") should be updated to acknowledge Meta Compute as an
emerging buyer→seller option, even if no rating moves. The framing is now factually incomplete.
