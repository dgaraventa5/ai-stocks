## 1. One-line thesis

> CrowdStrike is consolidating enterprise security onto one agent, and the arrival of frontier models capable of finding novel exploits has turned "AI security" from a roadmap slide into a budget line — making AI a demand catalyst for CrowdStrike rather than a threat to it.

---

## 2. Position in the AI supply chain

- **What they do:** Cloud-native endpoint, cloud, identity and SIEM security delivered through a single lightweight agent (Falcon), sold increasingly through Falcon Flex — a consumption-style commercial model that lets customers draw down a committed pool across modules.
- **Where they sit:** Not a supplier to the AI buildout. CrowdStrike's tie to AI is on the *demand* side: AI expands the attack surface (agent identities, model supply chain) and AI-armed attackers raise the baseline threat.
- **Unique vs. commoditized:** Single-agent architecture plus module breadth. The differentiator is that adding the eighth module costs the customer no new deployment.
- **AI revenue mix:** **Not separately disclosed.** Rated D1=2 on that basis — the demand tie is evidenced by catalyst and product, the magnitude is not. A disclosed AI/agent-security revenue line is what would move it (rubric D1 sourcing rule).

## 6. Moat

- **IP / process know-how:** Single-agent architecture and the threat-graph data asset accumulated across the installed base.
- **Scale advantage:** Detection quality improves with telemetry volume — a genuine data flywheel.
- **Switching costs:** High, and unusually well evidenced: CrowdStrike caused a global outage in July 2024 and dollar-based **gross** retention is now improving. Customers had maximal cause and provocation to leave and largely did not.
- **Network effects:** Indirect, via shared threat intelligence.
- **Regulatory / geographic:** None material.

**Moat rating:** Narrow-to-wide, rated D3=4 — deliberately *not* 5. Applying the rubric's own bias check (would I rate this the same if the stock were down 40% rather than up 96% over six months?), Microsoft Defender's bundling economics remain a live threat to the 10+ year defensibility that band 5 requires.

## 9. Risks

- **Thesis-killer #1:** Microsoft converts Defender's bundling advantage into share at the mid-market, and gross retention rolls over.
- **Thesis-killer #2:** The "Mythos moment" demand pull proves to be a one-off budget acceleration rather than a durable step-up, and FY28 laps an inflated base.
- **China / export controls:** No material direct exposure. Rated R2=4; **no geographic revenue breakout was pulled this pass — flagged.**
- **Cyclical risk:** Security budgets are among the most defended in enterprise IT, but Flex's consumption mechanics introduce more usage sensitivity than pure subscription.
- **Technology substitution:** AI-native security startups attacking specific workflows; the platform's answer is breadth.
- **Regulatory / execution:** **All three July-2024-outage-era securities and derivative dockets TERMINATED** between 2026-01-28 and 2026-04-16 (1:24-cv-00857 W.D. Tex.; 1:24-cv-01031; 1:25-cv-00543). No pending federal securities docket in RECAP. Termination is a docket status and does not distinguish dismissal from settlement — terms not read. Rated R4=4; the pending GoSecure DTSA action is what stands between this and a 5.

## 10. The "inverse the thesis" test

1. **If the moat is real, dollar-based gross retention continues improving through FY27 while Microsoft pushes Defender.** If gross retention turns down for two consecutive quarters, the outage recovery was cyclical goodwill rather than structural lock-in and D3=4 is too high.
2. **If the AI catalyst is durable, net new ARR growth stays above +30% into FY28 as the Mythos-driven budget cycle laps.** If it reverts toward the low-20s, the 97.9th-percentile multiple was pricing a one-off as a trend.
