## Pre-research mental model

CrowdStrike is the endpoint-security platform consolidator: Falcon's single-agent architecture lets it attach module after module to an installed base, so the investable question is module adoption and net-new ARR rather than headline revenue. My prior model held that the July 2024 outage had cost it some deal velocity and left litigation and customer-commitment packages in its wake, that Falcon Flex was the mechanism for re-accelerating consumption, and that Microsoft Defender bundling was the structural competitive threat. Most recent quarter I believed reported: Q1 FY2027. Product cycle: Charlotte AI moving toward agentic SOC work.

## DIFF vs prior mental model

- **The outage overhang is no longer the story — operationally or legally.** I carried deal-velocity damage, customer-commitment packages and open litigation as live drags. The quarter shows record net new ARR, improving gross *and* net retention, and record new-logo net new ARR; and all three outage-era securities/derivative dockets terminated between January and April 2026.
- **Net new ARR re-accelerated to +51% while revenue decelerated to +26%** — I did not have this divergence. Revenue is the lagging, ratable expression of ARR; the two metrics tell materially different stories about the same quarter and the market rewarded the leading one.
- **An exogenous demand catalyst appeared that no May-2026 model contained:** a frontier model capable of finding novel exploits, reframing AI security from a roadmap item to a budget line.
- **Falcon Flex scaled faster than I assumed** — >$2.29B at +101% is past the point where it can be dismissed as a packaging experiment.
- **Valuation is now the risk, not execution.** At the 97.5th percentile of its own three-year P/S range, the fundamental re-acceleration is the base case in the price.
