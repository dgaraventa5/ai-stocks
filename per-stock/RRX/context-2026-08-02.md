## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

What I thought I knew coming in: RRX is a $6B-revenue diversified motion-control industrial, the product of the Rexnord PMC and Altra roll-ups, carrying too much acquisition debt (~3.3x) and struggling with a weak residential-HVAC motor business. The robotics angle is that it owns Kollmorgen (frameless servo motors), Thomson (linear motion) and Altra (brakes/clutches) — on paper the most complete US-listed humanoid joint stack — but humanoid revenue is a rounding error and the story is sell-side. I expected the CEO to still be Louis Pinkham, the data-center switchgear business to be a modest kicker, and the insider selling flagged in the deep pass to turn out to be routine 10b5-1 program sales.

**Three of those four expectations were wrong.** See §4.

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

1. **The CEO changed and I did not know it.** Aamir Paul (Schneider Electric NA President, ex-Dell server) took over ~2026-07-01. This is materially bullish for the data-center thesis — RRX just hired an electrical-distribution and server executive to run a company whose fastest-growing line is data-center switchgear and ePODs — and it means the Q2 call on Aug 5 is a **new-CEO first call**, historically a kitchen-sink / re-baselining risk.
2. **The insider selling is NOT routine.** I expected 10b5-1. The Form 4 XML shows `aff10b5One = 0` on every executive sale. The CFO — who is staying — sold 37.1% of his position across three dates with no option exercise. This is the single biggest downgrade in this briefing.
3. **The data-center number is much bigger than "a kicker."** A **$735M ePOD order from hyperscale data-center customers**, majority shipping 2027, against a $600M switchgear funnel, taking aggregate 2027 data-center revenue to **~$900M ≈ 14% of revenue** at 20%+ EBITDA margins. 2026 is only $180M (~2.9%).
4. **The humanoid story got worse, not better.** $40M of orders in the prior year → **$1M in Q1 2026**. Management did not explain whether the $40M was one-time. Humanoid is ~0.02% of quarterly revenue. Any Layer-11 rating carried on humanoids alone would be unsupported.
5. Confirmed as expected: leverage still ~3.3x; PES still shrinking (−10.3% organic); Kerrisdale's June 2026 note is a **LONG** thesis (the deep pass's correction stands).
6. **Order momentum decelerated after the quarter**: +8.5% daily orders in Q1 → +4.6% in April. Watch this on Aug 5.
7. Stock is **below its 50DMA** ($205.23 vs $212.06) into the print.

## 5. Rating implications

- **D1** is the hard call. AI-infrastructure revenue in 2026 is $180M / ~$6.2B = **2.9%** — arithmetically band 1. But it is *disclosed, contracted and in backlog* ($735M), not an IR-deck claim, and steps to ~14% in 2027. See DEBATABLE below.
- **D5** is carried entirely by the hyperscale data-center tie, not by robotics. Under a narrow L11 robot-program lens RRX would rate 2.
- **M3 is the most consequential rating.** Mechanically the rubric lands on 1: >30% discretionary selling across multiple executives, zero buys, only one 10b5-1 designation.
- **R3**: ND ≈ $4,305M against TTM adjusted EBITDA ~$1.3B ≈ 3.3x — squarely band 3, with a deleveraging trajectory and $650M guided 2026 adjusted FCF.
- **R4** is dragged below "clean" by the CPSC sub-fractional-motor fire claims and the company's own "cannot assure … no material adverse effect" language.
- **Rule-9 flag:** re-run this briefing after the Aug 5 print. A new-CEO first call plus a decelerating April order rate is a real risk of guidance reset.
