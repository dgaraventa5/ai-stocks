## Pre-research mental model

BWXT is a ~63%-US-government-revenue nuclear manufacturer: sole-source naval propulsion reactors/components for the US Navy plus DOE/NNSA special-materials work (Government Operations), with Commercial Operations (~33%) being Canadian CANDU components/refurbishment (Pickering, Darlington), Kinectrics utility services (acquired May 2025), medical isotopes, and the AI-adjacent slice — BWRX-300 SMR components (reactor pressure vessel for OPG Darlington) plus TRISO fuel and the DoD-funded Project Pele microreactor. The June briefing's key conclusion was that the AI/data-center linkage is **indirect at best**: the CEO, asked directly on the Q1 call whether BWXT has data-center exposure, answered *"I don't know the details on that. I suspect that we do."* Last quarter known in detail was Q1 2026 (revenue $860.2M, +26%; backlog $8.7B, +77% YoY; FY26 guide raised to >$3.75B revenue / $4.60–4.75 non-GAAP EPS). Open catalysts going in: Q2 print, PCG acquisition close, and whether any SMR/data-center order converts.

It is now being removed. Post-close, Commercial Operations becomes a purer nuclear-power-components business (CANDU + SMR + PCG heavy manufacturing + Kinectrics utility services). This **mechanically raises the nuclear-power share of the remaining mix** and delivers up to $800M plus a retained minority stake to redeploy — but note the same divestiture also cuts a real, profitable revenue line, so FY2027 revenue optics will be muddied.
- **NEW — PCG closed 2026-07-01** (June briefing had it as "announced/pending"). The US commercial-nuclear manufacturing footprint is now real, not planned, and is inside Commercial Operations. It is also the main reason FY26 revenue guidance moved to ~$3,800M.

This tempers the June briefing's "+77% YoY, record backlog" framing — that Q1 spike was driven by the lumpy $1.4B May naval award landing in the book, and Q2 did not repeat it. **FLAG: one quarter is not a trend; watch Q3.**

## Implications for ratings

*No rating numbers proposed — per the task scope. These are the dimensions where the evidence moved and why.*

**D1 (AI-tied revenue %) — pressure in both directions, net likely unchanged-to-marginally-better.** Downward: the two Government lines closest to the AI story (advanced nuclear / microreactor) shrank for a second straight quarter, and the $3.1B NNSA awards are pure defense, further diluting any AI share of revenue. Upward: divesting medical removes a non-AI slice from Commercial, and PCG adds US nuclear-component capacity. Net: the *denominator* argument from June — that directly AI-tied revenue is ≈0% and indirect SMR-component revenue is a single-digit % — is unchanged and, if anything, better documented. **The gap that blocked precision in June persists: SMR component revenue is still not separately disclosed anywhere in the Q2 filings.**

**D2 (supply-chain position / bottleneck) — evidence strengthened.** PCG closed, giving BWXT an actual US heavy-nuclear-component footprint rather than a plan; the HPDU plant is described as the only active US site capable of the process for the government; DUECE is a sole-source domestic enrichment award. These are all monopoly-ish or near-monopoly positions in Western nuclear materials and heavy components. Counterweight unchanged from June: BWXT's chokepoint is strongest inside the GE Vernova BWRX-300 and CANDU ecosystems, and the SMR designs hyperscalers actually contract (Oklo, Kairos, X-energy, TerraPower, and now Aalo) largely do not anchor on BWXT.

**D5 (strength of tie to the AI buildout, rule-23 parallel scale) — the weakest dimension, and the June evidence got slightly worse, not better.** Still no named data-center customer. The Q2 release does not mention AI or data centers. And the first microreactor-to-AI-datacenter demonstration (Crusoe/Aalo at INL, announced 2026-07-30) went to a competitor while BWXT's own microreactor line is reporting declining volumes. **Re-examine D5 against this.**

**D3/D4 (moat / durability of position) — mildly supported** by the sole-source NNSA awards and the five-year naval task-order structure running through 2030, both of which extend visibility. Nothing contradicts the June read.

**M1/M2 (EPS revisions / relative strength) — supported.** Third consecutive guidance raise in 2026 (FY revenue $3,750M → ~$3,800M; EPS $4.60–4.75 → $4.70–4.80; FCF $315–330M → $345–360M), with a modest EPS beat. Objective 50DMA% should be refreshed alongside (rule 11) rather than judged from narrative.

**M3 (insider activity) — mechanically neutral, no change warranted.** All Form 4 activity since May is RSU vesting/tax-withholding and director dividend-equivalent accruals; two Form 144s were filed in May. **No open-market purchases** — and per the rubric's asymmetric treatment, absence of buying is weak evidence, not bearish evidence.

**R1 (customer concentration) — re-examine, but the direction is favorable.** US Government is 62.1% of Q2 revenue on its own (63.0% including Canada/other government). Per the rule-26 government-customer convention, the right lens is program/branch diversification within the government book — and that book is genuinely diversified across Naval Reactors (submarines + carriers), NNSA defense fuels (DUECE), NNSA HPDU, and DOE site work, with the naval piece now structured as five annual task orders through 2030. Concentration also fell ~5pts YoY as Commercial grew. Offsetting: the FY2027 budget request is a *request*, and the 8-K's own risk language leads with "federal budget uncertainty… continuing resolution funding mechanisms and the debt ceiling, the potential for government shutdowns."

**R3 (balance sheet) — re-examine on the funding picture.** PCG (~$200M) closed with cash out on 2026-07-01, but up to $800M of medical proceeds is coming in (2026 or Q1 2027), FCF guidance was raised ~$30M, and TTM FCF conversion is 0.95×. Directionally this is a strengthening liquidity position. **FLAG: the divestiture has not closed and is subject to regulatory approval — do not book the proceeds as certain.**

**R4 (regulatory) — no adverse action found**; NRC/DOE licensing is a schedule risk on DUECE/HPDU/BANR, not a compliance problem.

**R5 (disruption risk) — no change.** Non-Layer-10; defaults to 5 per rule-16 scope.
