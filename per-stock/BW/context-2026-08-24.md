> **6.50% Senior Notes due Dec 31 2026** (~$69M) needing refinance, (2) extreme single-counterparty
> concentration (~89% of backlog is Base Electron/Applied Digital), (3) warrant dilution tying the

> its own 3-year range. Expectation going in: the Base Electron ramp is on track and the 2026

**Verdict on the prior model: right on the operations, and materially incomplete on the risk.**

### What this does and does not establish
**Established:** the case exists, is pending, names the CEO and CFO, and was on file well before the
Q2 balance-sheet date; and BW's filings do not mention it.

**NOT established, and not for this briefing to decide:** whether the omission is improper. Reg S-K
Item 103 requires disclosure of *material* pending legal proceedings (excluding ordinary routine
litigation incidental to the business — which a securities-fraud class action is not), and ASC 450
requires disclosure where a material loss is reasonably possible. Management may have concluded the
matter is immaterial or that no material loss is reasonably possible at this stage, which is a
judgment call available to them. I have not read the complaint's substance beyond the docket record,
and I am not drawing a legal conclusion.

**What it means for the ratings process:** the *research premise* is what changed. BW's current
subjective ratings rest on a briefing that treated Base Electron as an independent counterparty and
knew nothing of the short report or the litigation. That premise is now demonstrably contested in
federal court, and the risk is not discoverable from BW's own periodic filings — so a filings-only
refresh would miss it again. **R4 can now be re-rated on verified fact rather than on a flag.**

## 6. Implications for ratings (flag only — NOT pre-decided)

- **R1 (customer concentration)** — the sharpest question on the board. Concentration was already
  extreme (~89% of backlog one counterparty); the allegation is that the counterparty is affiliated
  with BW's own ~19% holder. If verified, R1 as currently rated understates the exposure, because
  concentration and related-party risk are the same exposure here, not two.
- **R3 (balance-sheet risk)** — genuine upward (better) pressure, independent of the litigation:
  $382.8M cash vs a $61.8M current maturity, $23.0M of notes repurchased, going concern alleviated a
  year ago. This is the cleanest positive of the pass.
- **R4 (regulatory/legal)** — **actionable now, verification complete (§5).** *Cho v. Babcock & Wilcox*, 5:26-cv-00886 (N.D. Ohio), filed 2026-04-14, is **pending**, names the Chairman/CEO and CFO, and is **not disclosed in any BW periodic filing**. This is verified fact, not a flag.
- **D1 (AI revenue %)** — upward pressure on the trailing mix (revenue +130% on the Base Electron
  ramp), but note only 16% of backlog converts in 2026 and 58% is 2028+.
- **D2/D3 (position / moat)** — unchanged; boiler + steam-turbine island at GW scale is real
  engineering with few US suppliers, but it is EPC-style project work.
- **D5 (AI-buildout tie)** — nominally strong, but its *quality* is exactly what §3/§5 puts in
  question. A tie to a counterparty whose independence is disputed is not the same as a tie to a
  hyperscaler.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh: DUE** (Q2 revenue +130% YoY is far past the >15% surprise bar).
- **Rule-14 expectations flag: re-run** — P/S has gone 3.65 → ~1.39 while growth accelerated, so the
  June reading (95th percentile of own range) is stale in the opposite direction.
