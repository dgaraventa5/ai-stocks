## Pre-research mental model

AppLovin sells Axon, a machine-learning ad-targeting engine, originally against mobile-game install inventory and latterly expanded into e-commerce advertisers — the bull case being that Axon's model improvements compound into pricing power, the bear case being that the whole thing is a black box built on data access it may not be entitled to. My prior model held that growth and margins were extraordinary, that a 2025 short-seller campaign alleged reverse-engineering of Meta's advertising data, that an SEC probe and securities class actions followed, and that the e-commerce self-serve expansion was the main growth vector. Most recent quarter I believed reported: Q1 2026.

## DIFF vs prior mental model

- **Fundamentals did not break — expectations did.** I would not have predicted +53% revenue growth and an 84% EBITDA margin coexisting with a −43% three-month drawdown. The business is compounding; the multiple is deflating.
- **The bear case moved from "is this legitimate" to "is this still improving."** My June model centred on the short-seller/SEC data-practices allegations. Those remain open, but the August de-rating was driven by something different and more mundane: a self-diagnosed slowdown in the rate of Axon model improvement.
- **The SEC probe is confirmed active by the regulator itself**, which is a firmer fact than the press-release-driven picture I carried.
- **A second, independent data-practices challenge exists** (the Dutch privacy class action) that my prior model did not contain.
- **The exit has been vindicated on price, not on fundamentals** — a distinction worth recording explicitly rather than banking the −41% as evidence the model read the *business* correctly.
- **Capital return is real:** $551.3M of buyback/withholding in one quarter against 335M shares outstanding.

## Implications for ratings

*Research pass only — no rating values proposed here (rule 12).*

- **D1 (AI revenue %)** — essentially all of APP's revenue is AI-model-driven; unchanged and high.
- **D3 (moat)** — the dimension most directly challenged. The moat *is* Axon's rate of improvement, and management has disclosed that rate slowed. One quarter is not a trend, but this is the specific, falsifiable thing to track.
- **D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — APP is an AI *application*, not a supplier to the buildout; on the rule-23 parallel scale its tie should be judged on role, and nothing structural changed.
- **R4 (regulatory)** — the strongest evidence in the pass: an SEC investigation the regulator confirms is active and ongoing into the data practices underpinning the revenue model, plus a pending consolidated securities class action naming the CEO and other executives, plus a Dutch privacy class action. All allegations; all unresolved; all pointed at the same mechanism.
- **R5 (disruption risk, rule 16)** — Layer 10 name. The relevant question is not whether agents replace APP's workflow but whether a better model (Meta Advantage+, Google) out-improves Axon — which is the D3 question in different clothes.
- **M1 (EPS revisions) / M2 (relative strength)** — guidance below consensus, multiple price-target cuts, −43% over three months, 43% of 120 days above the 50DMA. Both dimensions have clearly negative evidence. Refresh `momentum_50dma.py`.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — not examined in this pass; the advertiser mix shift toward e-commerce self-serve was not quantified. **Flagged as not done.**
- **Rule 32-A relevance** — the capitulation flag does not fire on a name the model recently exited that is down 43%. That is informative in itself and should be stated at the rating session rather than left implicit.
