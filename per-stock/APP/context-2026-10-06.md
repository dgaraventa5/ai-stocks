I do not know of any new regulator action, state AG suit, short report, or Apple/Google policy change in Sept-Oct 2026, nor why the stock fell from ~$320 (Sept 7) to ~$282; that is the gap I expect research to fill.

## 5. DIFF: what changed vs. my pre-research model

1. **The SEC probe is closed, not open** — I (and the Sept briefing) had this wrong. The single largest regulatory overhang from 2025 is gone, on management's word.
2. **A government enforcement action replaced it** (San Diego County, 2026-10-05) — one day old, aimed at the data-collection mechanism itself, with an injunction request. Not in my model at all.
3. **A second securities class action is filed** (*Talbot*, 3:26-cv-10584) covering Feb-Aug 2026 and targeting the AI-improvement narrative specifically, not the 2025 data-practices allegations.
4. **APP is fighting Unity over data and lost the emergency round.** I did not know APP had sued a competitor claiming its mediation data was being siphoned. A company with a secure data moat does not usually need a TRO to protect it.
5. **Growth is 100% yield.** I knew growth was decelerating; I had not registered that installs are *down* (−2% Q2, −10% H1) while revenue per install is +58%/+75%. Every point of growth depends on the model pricing each install higher.
6. **The e-commerce diversification story has taken two independent hits** (BofA July, Wells Fargo September) on the same leading indicator, while management reports record spend from existing advertisers. Both can be true: deeper, not broader.
7. **The Sept-to-Oct leg down (~$320 → ~$283) was competition and legal, not fundamentals** — no new company-reported number exists since 2026-08-05.
8. **Unchanged:** margins, cash generation, buyback, 2b clean. Reported numbers remain exceptional.

## 6. Implications for ratings

*Research pass only — nothing is changed here (rule 12). Current: D1=5, D2=4, D3=3, D4=3, D5=2, M1=2, M2=1, M3=3, R1=4, R2=5, R3=5, R4=1, R5=4.*

- **R4 (currently 1) — evidence cuts both ways; net, the 1 still fits.** For an upgrade to 2 ("material litigation or active regulatory investigations"): the SEC inquiry is closed. Against: a government civil action seeking an injunction over data collection plus per-violation penalties, two pending securities class actions (one with an undecided motion to dismiss), a Dutch class action, a patent suit. Band 1 reads "major regulatory threat… class action that could be material." A public-enforcement UCL action against the core data mechanism is that. The mix changed; the level did not.
- **R5 (currently 4) — holds.** Band 4: "durable; AI net-neutral/tailwind… AI-native beneficiaries." Nothing found suggests agentic AI or the model layer displaces an ad-optimization engine; the threat is a rival's model (Unity Vector, Meta, Google), which is a moat question. The legal threat to data access is R4, not R5 — do not double-count.
- **D3 (currently 3) — holds, with the evidence now leaning toward the lower edge.** Band 3: "modest moat; share roughly stable but pricing is competitive." Management says waterfall share is steady; Edgewater says gains have stalled; APP is litigating to stop a competitor reading its auction data. Band 2 requires "losing share possible; price-taker behavior" — revenue per install +58% is the opposite of price-taking. Watch item, not a move. A Q3 print without the promised uplift would be the trigger to revisit.
- **D2 (currently 4) — holds; note the tension.** MAX mediation remains the chokepoint and pricing power is demonstrable in the 10-Q. The Unity SDK dispute shows the chokepoint is observable from inside by a competitor.
- **D1 (currently 5) — holds.** The 10-Q attributes essentially all growth to ad-engine performance. "How much is genuinely AI-driven": nearly all of the *growth*; the durability question lives in D3.
- **D4/D5 — no evidence either way.**
- **M1 (currently 2) — holds.** Band 2: "net negative revisions; some cuts." FY2026 EPS −2.5% and FY2027 −5.1% over 90 days with 5-6 down vs 1 up in 30 days. Band 1 needs consensus "moving sharply lower" — these are cuts, not a collapse.
- **M2 (1) — left per instruction;** price data here is consistent with it (−13.1% vs 50DMA; 41.7% of last 120 sessions above it).
- **M3 (currently 3) — holds.** No open-market buys or sells since August.
- **R1 (4) — not re-verified.** No ≥10% customer disclosure in the 10-Q; 10-K not re-read.

**Rule 32-A / is the entry a trap?** The capitulation flag does not fire and should not: 12.7× sales is the 31st percentile of a range that includes 2023-24, not a trough multiple. So rule 32-A offers no support for reading this as maximum pessimism. The model wants to enter because Value rose as price fell while trailing Quality/Growth inputs are still at their best — the rule-16 "rewards a falling quality name because it is falling" pattern. What the trailing numbers cannot show: (1) growth is entirely yield on shrinking volume, and management has said the yield lifts are lumpy; (2) the first hard evidence on the promised Q3 uplift arrives ~2026-11-11, after the model would have entered; (3) a day-old government action asks a court to restrict the data collection that feeds the yield engine; (4) no insider bought the drawdown. None of these is a thesis break on its own. Together they say the score is high for reasons that are backward-looking and the open questions are forward-looking.

**Thesis-break signals:** none confirmed. Closest candidates, in order: an injunction or settlement terms in the San Diego action that restrict SDK data collection; a Q3 print without the model step-up management described; platform (Google/Apple) action in response to the parental-controls allegation.
