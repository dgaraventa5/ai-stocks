## 2. Latest filing highlights

**Primary source:** 8-K filed 2026-07-14 (exhibit `clsk-ex99_1.htm`, acc 000119312526302448), covering a lease **executed 2026-07-10**. SEC EDGAR direct fetch is blocked in this session (confirmed: `sec_edgar.py` and direct WebFetch to `sec.gov` both 403); the terms below are cross-sourced from wire/press coverage of the same 8-K exhibit, flagged per rule 3.

- **Lease:** 20-year triple-net (NNN) lease, with **two 5-year extension options**, for **175 MW of critical IT load** at the Sandersville, GA campus. Counterparty is a "high-investment-grade, leading global technology company" — **tenant identity not disclosed** in the filing.
- **Revenue:** ~**$6.6B contracted over the initial 20-year term**, up to **$11.6B** if both extensions are exercised.
- **Delivery:** first data hall Q4 2027; remainder Q1 2028.
- **Texas:** the same tenant executed a **letter of intent + exclusivity arrangement** over CleanSpark's entire 718-acre, **up to 885 MW** Texas portfolio — not a signed lease, framed as a likely "phase two" of a multi-site relationship.
- **Advisors:** Morgan Stanley (financial advisor to CleanSpark), Davis Polk & Wardwell (legal counsel) — a bank-grade deal team, consistent with a real institutional counterparty.
- **Financing — the open question, not yet answered by the filing:** landlord project costs are estimated at $10–12M per MW of critical IT load, implying **~$1.75–2.1B of capex** needed for the Sandersville build before the lease recognizes revenue in 2027. The 8-K/press coverage **names no lender, no committed financing amount, no pricing, no sponsor-equity contribution, and no draw schedule**. The lease carries **financing, construction, and delivery milestones** — missing one can trigger rent abatement or lease termination. This is the single largest open risk in the deal as disclosed.

## 6. Implications for ratings (FLAGS ONLY — no rating decided here)

Per CLAUDE.md rule 2/12 the human owns the rating call. Flags for the collaborative session:

- **This is the trigger event the 6/22 briefing was waiting on — but it argues for a smaller upgrade than the headline number suggests.** A *signed* 175MW/$6.6B lease is a genuine, material change from "zero contracts" — D1 (AI Rev %)/D2 (Position)/D5 (Hyperscaler exposure), currently 2/2/1, likely deserve *some* upward move. But AI revenue doesn't start until Q4 2027, the counterparty is unconfirmed, and the financing to actually build the asset is not yet secured — so this reads as "contracted future optionality materially de-risked," not "AI revenue arrived." Recommend a partial upgrade (e.g., one point on D1/D2, hold D5 until tenant identity or a hyperscaler-level confirmation surfaces) rather than treating it as equivalent to IREN's named/producing Microsoft contract.
- **New R3 (Balance Sheet Risk) flag: the $2.1B financing gap.** This is a materially new risk not present in the 6/22 briefing — a signed lease with no committed financing, subject to milestone-based abatement/termination. Current R3 rating (BS Risk=3 on the sheet) should be revisited with this specifically in mind; a debt-funded build on top of existing ~$1.8B LT debt (per Q2 10-Q) into an asset that doesn't cash-flow until 2027 is a real leverage/execution risk, independent of the bitcoin-price risk already flagged.
- **Rule-13 EV/MW (Value) vs AI-Thesis — divergence should narrow, not close.** The capacity is still 1,809 MW of secured *bitcoin-mining* power (unchanged, EV/MW input); the new 175MW+885MW-LOI is a separate, not-yet-cash-flowing AI/HPC layer on top. Don't fold the new lease into the EV/MW capacity figure until `capacity-mw.json` is refreshed with an appropriate basis distinction (mining MW vs. contracted-but-undelivered AI MW) — recommend a `cohort_membership.py`/capacity-mw refresh pass alongside the next rating session.
- **Tenant-identity uncertainty is itself a flag.** Unlike IREN's named Microsoft deal, CLSK's "high-investment-grade global technology company" is unconfirmed. If it is later confirmed as Meta, that's a stronger signal (validates the broader thread of Meta's aggressive multi-vendor AI-supply-chain lock-in this quarter — see SNDK news-log); if the counterparty turns out to be a lower-credit-quality neocloud, the "high-investment-grade" framing would need re-examination. Recommend not pre-committing a D5 (hyperscaler exposure) upgrade until the tenant is confirmed.
- **Rule-14 (expectations) — inconclusive, flagged not scored.** `expectations_flag.py` could not run (yfinance blocked this session). Manual read: the stock rallied on the news but has since fallen ~20%+ from its post-announcement level to ~$13.90, below even the pre-announcement price — this does NOT look like a "priced for perfection" setup right now, more like unresolved-risk-discount + broad-selloff overlay. Re-run the script once yfinance access is available before the next scoring pass.
- **FCF conversion check — not meaningfully computable.** Both TTM FCF and TTM net income are negative (BTC fair-value marks dominate the loss), so the conversion ratio doesn't map cleanly to the accrual-quality red flag this metric is designed to catch. No flag raised; note as N/A rather than force a reading.
- **R5 Disruption Risk = 5 (default) — unchanged.** Layer 09, not Layer 10; rule 16 default applies.
- **Bitcoin-price exposure — still the primary near-term thesis-break vector, unchanged from 6/22.** The AI lease doesn't reduce this; CLSK is still 100% BTC-mining revenue today.

**THESIS-BREAK watch (updated):** (1) Bitcoin-price drawdown — unchanged, live risk. (2) **Financing execution risk on the Sandersville build — newly concrete** (milestone-based lease, no disclosed financing plan as of this filing) — the biggest near-term watch item. (3) Tenant-identity confirmation (positive if a confirmed hyperscaler; watch if it turns out to be a weaker credit). (4) Fiscal Q3 2026 earnings (2026-08-11) — first chance to see if management addresses the financing plan directly.
