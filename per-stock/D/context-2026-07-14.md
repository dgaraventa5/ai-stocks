## 3. WebSearch findings (each with source URL + date)

1. **Sen. Angus King (I-Maine) formally asked FERC to reject the NextEra–Dominion merger (2026-06-29).** Grounds: it would create "the largest electric utility in the United States" with anticompetitive market dominance; cites NextEra's $20M campaign against the NECEC transmission line (dark-money groups fined $210K by Maine's Ethics Commission), a Seabrook interconnection-upgrade refusal, the merchant-generation-plus-regulated-assets structural conflict, and a prior $150M NextEra settlement over Florida political-interference allegations. Calls the $2.25B customer bill-credit "inadequate." (Utility Dive, article dated 2026-06-30: https://www.utilitydive.com/news/senator-king-ferc-nextera-dominion-merger-necec/824073/)

2. **New Virginia state law mandates review of Dominion's data-center load forecasting (2026-04-30).** State law now requires independent review of Dominion's load forecasts as data centers raise reliability/cost-allocation concerns — direct political scrutiny of the credibility of the headline DC pipeline. (Virginia Mercury, 2026-04-30: https://virginiamercury.com/2026/04/30/new-state-law-mandates-review-of-dominions-load-forecasting-as-data-centers-raise-concerns/ — note: page returned 403 on fetch; summary is from the search result snippet, treat as directional.)

3. **Interconnection queue is ~70 GW — roughly 3x all-time peak.** Dominion has ~25,000 MW under request for facilities projected to connect through end-2031, plus ~45,000 MW for projects without set connection dates. PJM expects the Dominion zone to have the largest absolute summer-peak increase in PJM 2026–2030 at ~5.4%/yr; 2025 summer peak 23,905 MW (+23% vs 2019), 2025–26 winter peak 25,413 MW (+45% vs 2019–20). (EIA Today in Energy / Daily Energy Insider, 2026; Virginia Business "Dominion prepares for 70,000 MW": https://virginiabusiness.com/dominion-data-center-power-demand-virginia-scc/) — the 70 GW request queue is a *larger, earlier-stage* metric than the 51 GW contracted pipeline; don't conflate.

4. **CVOW offshore wind ~75% complete, first power March 2026, cost now ~$11.5B.** Q1 2026 investor slides put the 2.6 GW project at ~75% complete with first commercial turbine energized (Electrek, 2026-03-25). Cost has risen to ~$11.5B (from $10.7B), including ~$228M tied to a construction suspension and ~$580M of actual/estimated tariff costs; majority of turbines expected in service by end-2026, though some coverage cites a June 2027 target-date as the near-term priority. (Power-Eng 2026 "tops 70% complete"; offshorewind.biz 2025-02; WHRO 2026-02-24: https://www.whro.org/environment/2026-02-24/pause-on-virginia-offshore-wind-farm-cost-dominion-more-than-200m-but-turbines-will-power-up-soon)

5. **$65B 2026–2030 capital plan reaffirmed; DC-driven.** Plan raised ~30% from $50.1B to $64.7B at Q4 2025 earnings (2026-02-23); Dominion Energy Virginia = $54.8B of it, DE South Carolina = $7.6B; ~$2B (~3%) battery storage subject to approval. Q1 2026 affirmed 2026 EPS guidance $3.45–$3.69 and 5–7% long-term growth with a bias to the upper half starting 2028. (Investing.com Q4 2025 slides recap 2026-02-23: https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/dominion-energy-q4-2025-slides-65b-capital-plan-targets-data-center-boom-93CH-4519537; GreentechLead 2026-02.)

## 6. Implications for ratings (NOT pre-deciding numbers)

- **D1 (AI-revenue %) — likely unchanged.** No new revenue-share disclosure since the FY2025 10-K (datacenters = 28% of Virginia Power electricity sales; ~mid-teens-to-20% of consolidated revenue with the volume-vs-dollars ambiguity still flagged). The 70 GW interconnection queue reinforces demand magnitude but is a *pipeline* metric, not current revenue. No change to the evidence base for D1.

- **D2 (return capture / pricing power) — watch, slight negative pressure.** No change to the 9.8% allowed ROE, but the new Virginia load-forecasting-review law and Sen. King's FERC challenge both point the same direction: rising political/regulatory friction around who pays for and who profits from DC growth. This is consistent with the prior "textbook regulated capture, visible political pressure on the allowed return" read — if anything the pressure intensified. Not a mechanical rating trigger, but the D2 thesis (no premium capture) is reinforced.

- **D5 (named commitments / contracted GW) — likely unchanged.** Still no named hyperscaler contracts; the 51 GW pipeline remains anonymous and tariff-priced. The 70 GW request queue is a bigger number but even earlier-stage and still nameless. No new ESA-stage disclosure. Evidence base essentially static.

- **Momentum (M) — check, merger-anchored.** D's price tracks NEE via the 0.8138 fixed ratio, so standalone momentum signals are muted and now carry an added deal-break/rejection tail from the King FERC opposition. The objective 50DMA% metric should be refreshed mechanically (`scripts/momentum_50dma.py`) rather than reasoned from news. Subjective EPS-revisions/relative-strength ratings are largely a function of the merger arb spread now, not fundamentals.

- **Risk R1 (customer concentration) — unchanged.** Regulated, diversified ratepayer base; no single-customer disclosure.
- **Risk R3 (balance sheet) — mild watch.** ~$2.3B new June debt on top of a $65B capex plan; leverage is the pressure point for a capital-heavy regulated utility, but this is planned funding, not distress. Verify against ND/EBITDA at the next objective refresh.
- **Risk R4 (regulatory/political) — negative pressure.** This is the dimension most affected: (a) Sen. King's FERC rejection request (2026-06-29), (b) the new Virginia load-forecasting-review law (2026-04-30), (c) ongoing SCC merger review with watchdog ROE-concession demands. Merger-approval risk and DC-cost-allocation politics are both trending less favorable. R4 warrants a fresh look.
- **Risk R5 (disruption) — unchanged at 5.** Non-Layer-10 physical infrastructure; no AI/structural disruption to the regulated-utility revenue model. Default max stands.
