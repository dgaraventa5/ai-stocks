## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

Allient is a $1.5B small-cap motion roll-up (formerly Allied Motion) with genuinely correct robotics products — Electroflux frameless torque motors, SA Series axial-flux, Megaflux — and a JPMorgan upgrade built on humanoid positioning. But the deep pass was blunt: **zero robotics revenue disclosed and zero "humanoid" mentions in any SEC filing; the entire story is whitepapers and trade shows**, on a stock up 121% in a year. My prior was that this is the weakest-evidenced name in the Layer-11 motion cohort and should be rated accordingly — a narrative long with nothing in the filings to anchor it.

**That prior was half right. The humanoid story is indeed absent from the filings. But there IS a disclosed AI-infrastructure revenue driver, and I had it wrong.**

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

1. **The single most important correction: ALNT does have a disclosed AI-infrastructure revenue driver, and the deep pass missed it.** The FY2025 10-K names *"increased demand in power quality solutions supporting data center infrastructure"* as **the** driver of Industrial (48.4% of revenue) growth and of the backlog build — stated twice, in audited MD&A. That is a real, filed AI-adjacent revenue line, not a whitepaper. It is **not quantified**, which is the honest limit on how far it can be credited.
2. **The humanoid claim remains entirely unfiled.** Zero "humanoid" in the 10-K or 10-Q; zero "robot" of any kind in the Q1 2026 10-Q. The JPMorgan thesis is sell-side. Nothing found this pass changes that.
3. **Insider read is cleaner than I expected.** I anticipated broad post-rally selling after a +121% year. Instead the CEO (1.57M shares) and CFO have sold nothing; the only seller is one group president.
4. **The sell side is now behind the stock, not ahead of it.** Consensus PT $76.80 vs price $87.46 — a 12% *downside* to consensus. That is a momentum/expectations flag, not a bull point.
5. **Price is sitting exactly on its 50DMA** ($87.46 vs $87.28) into an Aug 5 print — no cushion.
6. Confirmed as expected: strong order book (b:b 1.14, backlog $251M), modest margins (32.7% GM, 6.7% operating), ~2.2x net leverage, manufacturing exposure in Mexico and China with explicit tariff language.

## 5. Rating implications

- **D1** should move off a bare 1. There is filed AI-datacenter revenue (power quality solutions), it is the named growth driver of the largest segment, and per the project's convention AI-DC revenue counts toward D1. But it is unquantified and robotics is zero — so band 2 with an explicit contingency: **if the Aug 5 print quantifies data center below 5% of revenue, this goes to 1.**
- **D5 (L11 lens)** stays weak regardless: no named robot program, no contracted robot revenue, no humanoid word in any filing. This is the "narrative-only" end of the cohort and should be rated as such even though D1 improved.
- **D3** must resist the halo from a +121% year. Gross margin 32.7% and operating margin 6.7% are not the signature of pricing power; this is a competent multi-brand component roll-up, not a moat.
- **M3** lands on the default 3: no genuine buys (the Winter trade is a wash) and only a single executive selling — the rubric's band-2 test requires "multiple executives."
- **R1** is a clean 5 on explicit filed language.
- **Rule-9 flag:** the Aug 5 print is the first chance to quantify the data-center line. That single disclosure is what would move D1 up or down a band, so treat every D-rating here as provisional.
