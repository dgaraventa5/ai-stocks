## 1. Pre-research mental model (written BEFORE pulling data)

Applied Materials is the largest process-equipment vendor by revenue, with the broadest portfolio
(deposition, etch, CMP, ion implant, metrology) but — unlike ASML — no single monopoly tool. My
model held that AMAT's AI leverage was real but diluted: it sells into leading-edge logic, DRAM and
advanced packaging, yet also into large trailing-edge and China-driven capacity where growth is
flat-to-down. I believed China was roughly 30%+ of revenue and falling under tightening export
controls, that this was the dominant risk, and that AMAT's growth would trail the AI narrative
because its mix is broader than the AI-exposed nodes. I expected mid-to-high-single-digit growth.

### 2b. FCF conversion red-flag check
TTM FCF $5.34B ÷ TTM net income $8.51B = **62.8% conversion** (TTM revenue $29.02B). Above the 60%
threshold, so the flag does not fire. Directionally the modest conversion is consistent with
management's own disclosure that it has **deliberately increased inventory and build plans** to meet
AI demand — an investment decision, not an accrual concern. **Not flagged**; monitor whether
inventory build continues to outpace revenue after Q3.

### 2c. Expectations red-flag check (rule 14)
`expectations_flag.py AMAT` → **clean**, but this is the tightest call in the rotation: P/S 14.6 =
**96.3rd percentile** of AMAT's own 3-year range, with revenue YoY +10.4% vs 3-year median +5.8%.
Growth is above median, so no flag — but AMAT is trading at essentially the top of its own three-year
multiple range on ~10% revenue growth. The bull argument that must be made explicitly: the >30%
calendar-2026 semi-equipment growth guide and >50% advanced-packaging growth imply the *reported*
10.4% TTM understates the forward rate. If Q3 (Aug 13) does not corroborate that acceleration, the
multiple is exposed.

2. **The HBM intensity argument, quantified:** HBM requires **3–4× more wafer starts per delivered
   bit** than standard DRAM, and stack heights are moving from 12 layers toward 16–20+, increasing
   both wafer starts and process steps. This is the mechanism by which AI demand converts into AMAT
   tool demand — it is a units-and-steps story, not just a capex story.
   — same Zacks source above

3. **New systems launched specifically for 3D memory and packaging** (CMP, ECD, PECVD targeted at
   HBM/3D stacking), announced June 2026.
   — [SahmCapital, 2026-06-26](https://www.sahmcapital.com/news/content/applied-materials-amat-unveils-ai-chip-tools-for-3d-memory-and-packaging-2026-06-26);

## 6. Implications for ratings (flagging only — not pre-deciding)

- **D1 (AI-revenue %)** — revisit. The HBM/advanced-packaging intensity argument is a stronger D1
  case than a naive revenue-mix read. But AMAT still does not break out an AI revenue line; rate on
  disclosed segment evidence, not on the intensity argument alone.
- **D2 (supply-chain position)** — check. AMAT is broad-portfolio rather than monopoly-tool (the
  ASML contrast). The new HBM/3D-packaging systems argue for a stronger bottleneck position in
  packaging specifically than in the portfolio overall.
- **R2 (geography)** — **the dimension most in need of review.** China at 27% and *rising*, a
  realized $253M BIS settlement, ~$600M of flagged FY26 revenue loss, and TrendForce expecting China
  fab spend to fall in 2026. Whatever R2 currently reads, it should be re-derived against these
  specifics rather than carried forward.
- **R4 (regulatory)** — same cluster; the BIS settlement is a concrete adverse regulatory outcome,
  which is the kind of evidence the R4 bands want.
- **M1 (EPS revisions)** — guidance implies ~35% YoY adjusted-EPS growth in Q3; likely supportive.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh:** **not yet due — but will be immediately after Aug 13, 2026.** Queue
  it. If Q3 delivers a >15% beat/miss or >500bps sequential GM move, it becomes priority-1.

**No thesis-break signals.** Export controls are a known, quantified, escalating risk — material to
R2/R4 but not a break.
