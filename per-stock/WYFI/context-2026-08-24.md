**The adjustment (10-Q MD&A, Revenue from cloud services):** *"The decrease in our monthly GPU service
revenue from the termination of our agreement with our Initial Customer was substantially offset by

Backing that out:
- **Cloud services ex-fee ≈ $11.5M vs $16.6M PY → ≈ −31% YoY.**

- Colocation (+$3.0M YoY, MTL-3 fully operational) is the only organically growing line, and it is
  small.

This is a genuine one-time item, not an accrual game — but it is **not recurring revenue and must not
be annualized.** Flagged under rule 3 and the rule-15 "one-time-dominated" principle (that rule is
scoped to EPS YoY, but the same garbage-input logic applies to any growth metric ingesting this print).

### Initial Customer — RESOLVED, and the terms are disclosed (10-Q, Customer Agreements note)
- A **Termination Agreement was executed in Q2 2026.** It **preserved $12.5M of previously invoiced,
  unpaid trade receivables — fully collected as of 6/30/26.**
- Prepayment and service-deposit balances were applied against other receivables; WYFI recognized
  **~$2.2M bad-debt expense** on the unpreserved remainder.
- The Initial Customer owes a **fixed termination fee of $12.3M, recognized as revenue in Q2 2026.**
  **Post-quarter the fee was amended UP to $15.7M, and the $15.7M remains OUTSTANDING as of the
  10-Q date.** (So the cash has not been collected — the revenue is recognized, the receivable is live.)
- GPUs previously allocated to the Initial Customer were **redeployed to other customers.**
- DNA Fund: of the ~$7.3M AR at termination, **$2.2M collected as of the 10-Q date** (unchanged from
  the June briefing — no further recovery in ~2 months).

**The synthesis that matters:** the thesis-critical event happened — NC-1 converted from contract to
cash, which is what the June briefing said the whole bull case required. But the quarter that
announced it is flattered by a one-time fee, the underlying cloud business shrank ~31% YoY while the
anchor rotated, and the buildout is now being funded with a second convertible tranche. The risk
shape changed rather than shrank: from "will the anchor pay?" to "can the buildout be funded to the
point where the new anchors carry it?"

## 5. Implications for ratings (flag only — NOT pre-decided)

- **D1 (AI revenue %)** — unchanged in substance: essentially 100% AI/HPC. No live question.
- **D5 (strength of AI-buildout tie)** — upward pressure with a caveat. The tie is now *contracted and
  billing* (Nscale + investment-grade offtaker) rather than promised, which is a real quality
  upgrade; but ~$933M colo RPO concentrated in one counterparty is the same shape of risk that just
  cost them the Initial Customer.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — the key debate. The old concentration resolved; a new one
  (Nscale) replaced it. Whether that is a net improvement depends on the offtaker's credit, which is
  described only as "investment-grade" and is not named. Do not upgrade R1 on the mere fact that the
  *old* problem is gone.
- **R3 (balance-sheet risk)** — downward pressure (worse). Cash fell $114.4M → $56.1M in six months;
  the $310M 2032 converts stack on the $230M 2031 converts; PP&E nearly doubled. Funded, but tightly,
  and on convertible paper.
- **M (Momentum)** — EPS-revision/relative-strength inputs will read positive (+16.6% on the print);
  the honest counterweight is that the beat contains the termination fee.
- **Rule-13 MW input: REFRESH `00-master/capacity-mw.json`.** NC-1 moved from "delivered 54 gross MW,
  not energized" to "~20 MW IT-load live, 40 MW contracted by end-August," with a further 45 MW gross
  pending a Duke schedule and a new Yadkin County site not yet closed. The EV/MW denominator is stale.
- **Rule-9 objective refresh: DUE — but apply the §2 adjustment.** Feeding the reported +54% into the
  growth inputs without removing the $12.3M fee would repeat the rule-15 garbage-input failure in a
  different column.
