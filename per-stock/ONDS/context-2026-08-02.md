## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

**① The "pending litigation" premise is not supported — correct it.** The Q1 2026 10-Q, Part II Item 1, cross-references Note 16, and Note 16 states in terms: *"There are no such loss contingencies."* There is **no material litigation disclosed as of 2026-05-15.** Rule 3 requires flagging this rather than papering over it: R4 should not be marked down for litigation. If the original note came from a source I did not see, it needs re-verification at the Q2 10-Q.

**② Guidance and backlog are materially better than the roster's numbers.** FY26 guidance went from ≥$390M to **>$525M** (a 25% raise) and pro-forma backlog from $457M to **~$663M**, both on the DZYNE close. Consensus has moved with it: 2026 $525.7M, 2027 $981.4M. This is the strongest revision profile in the Layer-11 defense cohort.

**③ But the dilution is worse than "treadmill" conveys, and it is now quantified.** **305,637,672 potentially dilutive securities** against **569.86M shares outstanding** — a **~54% overhang** — versus 128.3M a year earlier. Ondas raised **$959.1M from stock and warrant sales in Q1 2026 alone**, and issued stock as consideration in at least three more acquisitions since (High Point UAS, DZYNE, World View — each with an Item 3.02 unregistered-sales disclosure). Every incremental dollar of revenue arrives attached to shares.

**④ The cash figure in the roster was wrong, and most of it is now spent.** Cash was **$1.026B** at 2026-03-31, not $1.48B (the larger figure presumably included short-term investments and/or post-quarter raises). Roughly **$876M** of cash-and-stock went to DZYNE in July, plus High Point UAS and World View, plus $35.0M into PDW preferred.

**⑤ The CEO's headline "sales" are not discretionary — this changes the M3 read entirely.** The 2,378,245 shares that left Eric Brock's account on 2026-06-02 at $13.43 were *"sold by the Company to cover potential tax withholding obligations in connection with the vesting of the RSUs"* (footnote F2), and the 475,000 shares on 2025-12-31 were *"sold to cover tax obligations"* on an exchange-agreement issuance (footnote F1). Optics said "CEO dumped $32M"; the filing says company-executed withholding. **But the same footnote reveals a 13,500,000-RSU grant on 2026-02-11** — ~2.4% of the company — which is a dilution and governance fact, not an insider-signal fact, and belongs in R3.

**⑥ Customer concentration is improving.** Top customer fell from 43% (Q1 2025) to **32%** (Q1 2026), and AR concentration from a single customer at 73% of AR to 42%/11%. The roll-up is doing what a roll-up is supposed to do on this axis.

**⑦ Organic growth remains unmeasurable.** Three acquisitions closed after the balance-sheet date; six closed before it. The Q2 10-Q will be the first look at the post-DZYNE entity, and even then the organic line will be obscured.

## 5. Rating implications — proposed 13 subjective ratings

### AI Thesis (D1–D5)

**D1. AI/Robotics Revenue % — 5**
- *Rationale:* Following the deconsolidation of Ondas Networks on 2026-01-16 (the legacy rail/wireless business; ONDS retains ~47.5% and no longer consolidates it), essentially all revenue is autonomous drone systems and counter-UAS — American Robotics/Airobotics platforms, Iron Drone/Raider counter-UAS interceptors, and now DZYNE, World View and High Point UAS under Ondas Sentinel. **Estimated ~95%+** — ONDS does not publish a robotics/non-robotics split, so this is an estimate and is flagged.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 deconsolidation note; 8-Ks 2026-07-06 and 2026-07-24.
- *Confidence:* Medium-High.

**D2. Supply Chain Position — 3**
- *Rationale:* Mid-chain with differentiation. Iron Drone/Raider is a genuinely differentiated kinetic counter-UAS interceptor and counter-UAS is the fastest-moving line in the FY27 budget — but ONDS owns no upstream bottleneck, has assembled its position by acquisition rather than by technical lead, has demonstrated no pricing power, and competes for the same procurements as Anduril, DroneShield, RTX and Leonardo DRS. Substitutes exist and switching costs are procurement-level.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026; acquisition 8-Ks; `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md`.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**D3. Moat Strength — 2**
- *Rationale:* Weak moat, applied ruthlessly per the rubric. The asset base is months old and unintegrated: Ondas Networks deconsolidated in January, six acquisitions closed by May, three more (High Point UAS, DZYNE, World View) in July. Whatever durable advantage exists — American Robotics' FAA BVLOS authorisations, DZYNE's autonomous-aircraft IP — has not been demonstrated to hold share against better-capitalised competitors, and there is no evidence of customers staying through a price increase. Not 1: the counter-UAS interceptor products have real, early, Replicator-adjacent traction and the backlog is contracted.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026; 8-Ks 2026-07-06, 2026-07-24.
- *Confidence:* Medium. *Bias check:* the +273% one-year move is not evidence of a moat; I rated the integration state, not the chart.

**D4. Capacity Expansion — 5**
- *Rationale:* Buildout is funded and underway at extreme scale: **$959.1M of equity/warrant proceeds in Q1 2026 alone**, $1.026B of cash at quarter-end, ~$876M deployed into DZYNE in July plus High Point UAS and World View, a $35.0M PDW preferred investment, and a newly stood-up Ondas Sentinel division — against ~$663M of pro-forma backlog to fill.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 cash-flow statement and balance sheet; 8-Ks 2026-07-06 and 2026-07-24; DZYNE close reporting.
- *Confidence:* High.

**D5. AI-buildout tie — 3 · L11 lens (proposed)**
- *Rationale:* Real but **medium**-strength. Positives: ~$663M pro-forma backlog and >$525M of 2026 guidance aimed squarely at counter-UAS, the fastest-moving line in the FY27 ~$75B drone/autonomy budget, with Replicator-2 relevance. Held to 3 because the tie is **acquired rather than won** — DZYNE alone contributed ~$111M of that backlog — there is **no named program of record** anchoring the position, and organic growth is not separately measurable, so the strength of the tie cannot be verified from ONDS's own execution.
- *Source:* DZYNE close reporting; 10-Q Q1 2026; per the roster's "backlog but roll-up → medium" framing.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

### Risk (R1–R5) — 5 = lowest risk

**R1. Customer Concentration — 3**
- *Rationale:* Three customers at **32%, 20% and 17%** of Q1 2026 revenue — top customer 32%, squarely band 3 ("top customer 25–40%; real dependence but not existential"). Concentration is *improving*: the prior-year quarter was 43%/36%/14%, and AR concentration fell from a single customer at 73% (2025-12-31) to 42%/11% (2026-03-31).
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026, Concentration of Customers note (verbatim in §2).
- *Confidence:* High on the disclosed quarter. **Caveat: the mix will be rewritten by three acquisitions that closed after the balance-sheet date** — re-check at the Q2 10-Q.

**R2. Geographic / Export Risk — 4**
- *Rationale (mandatory named shock + % exposed):* The worst plausible shock is a **regional-conflict escalation or export-licensing restriction affecting the Israel-based counter-UAS engineering and production base** inherited with Airobotics/Iron Drone, which underpins the pre-acquisition counter-UAS product line. Exposed revenue is **not separately disclosed** — this is the honest gap. No China policy leg and no BIS/entity-list exposure. One leg at most.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 (which contains **no geographic revenue or asset-location breakout**).
- *Confidence:* **Low-Medium — this is the weakest-evidenced rating in this briefing.** I could not verify the current asset footprint from the Q1 2026 10-Q this session; the Israel asset leg is carried forward from prior knowledge of the Airobotics acquisition, not from a citation. **Verify against the FY2025 10-K Item 2 / Item 1A before finalising.** If the asset leg is confirmed, the rubric's band-2 "asset leg" language would pull this to 2; if operations have been consolidated into the US, it goes to 5. Do not treat 4 as settled.

**R3. Balance Sheet Risk — 2**
- *Rationale:* The roster's biggest cash pile paired with its worst balance-sheet hygiene — and the hygiene dominates. *Positives:* cash **$1.026B** at 2026-03-31 and essentially **no funded debt** ($243k current + $188k non-current notes payable; $3.41M convertible notes), so there is no maturity wall and no going-concern flag. *Negatives that set the band:* (a) **305,637,672 potentially dilutive securities against 569.86M shares outstanding — a ~54% overhang**, up from 128.3M a year earlier; (b) **$959.1M of stock and warrant sales in Q1 2026 alone**, plus Item 3.02 unregistered issuances in three July acquisitions; (c) total liabilities more than doubled to **$1.359B** from $661.2M, including **$128.1M** of accrued purchase and contingent consideration; (d) ~$876M of the cash committed to DZYNE in July; (e) **adjusted EBITDA −$10.9M** with company-wide adjusted-EBITDA profitability not expected until Q1 2028; (f) a 13.5M-share RSU grant to the CEO on top of all of it. Band 2 ("dilution risk" is the rubric's own band-1/2 language; the absence of leverage and going-concern flags is what keeps it off 1).
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 2026 balance sheet, EPS/dilution note, cash-flow statement; Form 4 footnote F5 (2026-06-02); DZYNE close reporting.
- *Confidence:* High.

**R4. Regulatory / Litigation Risk — 4**
