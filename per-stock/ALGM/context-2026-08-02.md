## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

Allegro is the only US-listed component company with humanoid robotics written into its **10-K**: magnetic and inductive position sensors plus motor drivers, a content ladder of ~$5 in a home robot / ~$55 in a cobot / ~$150 in a humanoid, design wins with large Chinese humanoid OEMs and one North American humanoid company. It also names AI-datacenter cooling. FY2026 (ended 2026-03-27) sales were $890.1M, +22.8%. The catch: robotics revenue is not broken out, management says humanoids only matter around **2030–31**, and the company posted a GAAP net loss in FY2026 so trailing P/E is unusable — the deep pass said use P/S ~8.2x. My prior was that ALGM's AI exposure is a 2030 option, not current revenue, and that D1 therefore belongs in the low bands.

**The July 30 print says otherwise on the AI-revenue question — just not via robotics.**

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

1. **The AI revenue is here now, and it is not robotics — it is the data center.** *"Data center reached a record 17% of total sales"* (Q1 FY27 release) and the 10-Q attributes Taiwan's **+219.3%** surge to data-center demand. Under this project's convention that AI-datacenter revenue counts as AI-infrastructure revenue, ALGM's D1 is a **band-3** name today, not the low band I expected from a 2030–31 humanoid story. This is the single biggest correction in this briefing.
2. **Power ICs, not magnetic sensors, are the growth engine** — +47.4% vs +16.0%. The robotics thesis is built on the magnetic-sensor content ladder; the money right now is in power.
3. **The geography is far more Asia-concentrated than I carried.** Asia is **74.1%** of revenue, the US only **9.7%**. Greater China 25.2% and Taiwan 16.5% are both individually large, and Taiwan is the fast-growing one — the AI-datacenter revenue is physically routed through Taiwan.
4. **The stock is the laggard of the group, badly.** +22.5% over 52 weeks against **SMH +83.0%** — roughly 60 points of underperformance — trading 19% below its 50DMA and 42% below its 52-week high, with 10% of float short. A beat-and-raise print on 2026-07-30 produced **two price-target cuts the next day** (TD Cowen $66→$48, Mizuho $67→$58). That combination is multiple compression, not estimate cuts, but it is a real signal about how the market is valuing the AI-datacenter mix shift.
5. **GAAP profitability has turned.** GAAP operating margin −1.3% → 9.8% in four quarters, GAAP EPS from a loss to $0.08. The "GAAP loss makes P/E unusable" flag from the deep pass is on its way to being stale — but TTM is still distorted, so **use P/S ~8.2x for now and re-check after Q2 FY27.**
6. Confirmed as expected: robotics is not broken out anywhere; humanoid remains a named market with no quantified revenue.

## 5. Rating implications

- **D1 moves up to the 10–25% band on the disclosed 17% data-center mix.** This is exactly the case the project's convention was written for — it is AI-infrastructure revenue even though it is not robotics. Do not let the humanoid framing suppress a disclosed number.
- **D5 must not double-count that.** ALGM reaches hyperscalers **indirectly**, through Taiwan ODMs and power-supply makers; no hyperscaler is named as a customer and the only disclosed concentration is a **distributor** at 12.3%. The rubric's explicit failure mode — "counting 'data center' exposure as hyperscaler exposure" — applies here.
- **R2** now has two clear legs: shipment concentration to Taiwan (16.5%, and the growth engine) plus a Greater China policy overhang (25.2%). That is the AMAT/LRCX/KLAC anchor, not the TSM anchor — there is no fab-in-Taiwan asset leg and no current policy impairment.
- **M2 is a hard 1** and should be rated as such. This is the "great company, poor near-term price action" case the rubric explicitly says not to fight.
- **M3**: aggregate ~20% discretionary selling with zero buys is band 2; the per-person outliers above 30% sit on small bases while the CEO — the largest holder — sold the least.
- **R3** is band 4, not 5: net debt $125.1M, but comfortably under 2x EBITDA with a January 2026 repricing and no maturity wall.
