## 1. Pre-research mental model (written before searching)

Kratos is the "cheap drones and cheap engines" defense company: XQ-58A Valkyrie attritable jet aircraft, a long-running target-drone franchise for the USAF/Navy, small turbojet/turbofan engines for drones and cruise missiles, plus satellite ground systems, microwave electronics and training simulators. My prior was that roughly **55–65% of revenue is unmanned/autonomy-tied**, that the Valkyrie's CCA program-of-record status is the whole equity story, that the balance sheet carries modest leverage, and that the stock had de-rated ~65% on lumpy milestone revenue and a very high forward multiple. I expected Q2 to have already reported.

Two of those priors turned out to be wrong.

## 4. DIFF — pre-research model vs. fresh reality

**① The "~55–65% autonomy revenue" estimate does not survive the segment disclosure. This is the biggest correction in this briefing.**
The Unmanned Systems segment was **$82.6M of $371.0M = 22.3%** of Q1 2026 revenue (10-Q segment note). The drone/cruise-missile turbine engine business sits inside Kratos Government Solutions and is **not separately disclosed**. Even crediting engines generously, autonomy-tied revenue is plausibly **~25–35%**, not 55–65%. And a meaningful part of the engine line serves *cruise missiles and hypersonic testbeds* — munitions, not robotics — so the honest robotics number is at the low end of that. **D1 should be 4, not 5.** This is precisely the "flag estimates as estimates" trap the universe file warned about, and it was the roster's own estimate that was off.

**② The balance sheet is a fortress, not "modest leverage."**
Zero long-term debt (Term Loan A repaid in full 2025-07-02), **$1.46B cash**, $3.41B equity, $200M revolver undrawn — after a **$1.35B net equity raise in February 2026**. This funds the CCA/engine capacity buildout outright. R3 = 5 and D4 = 5 both follow from filed numbers rather than press releases.

**③ Kratos is the subcontractor on its own flagship program.**
On MUX TACAIR, **Northrop Grumman is the sole prime** and owns the autonomy stack (Prism); Kratos supplies the airframe. "Valkyrie is a program of record" is true, but the value capture is airframe-level, and the disclosed dollars so far ($231.5M OTA for the program; $34.8M mission-system integration) are small against $1.75B of FY26 revenue. That caps D5 at 4.

**④ Q2 has not reported (2026-08-04).** Rule 9 makes a refresh mandatory within a week of the print. Watch: (a) whether Q2 revenue lands in the guided $400–410M vs. the $411.7M consensus; (b) whether the guided **negative Q2 operating income (−$6M to −$8M)** materialises; (c) any Unmanned Systems segment inflection, which is the only number that matters for D1.

**⑤ Insider behaviour is bifurcated in a way the headline misses.** The CEO sold 800,000 shares (~$59M) between Dec 2025 and Jan 2026 — but every share was under a 10b5-1 plan, which the rubric explicitly classes as routine. The genuinely discretionary selling is a small tail of outside directors. See §5 M3.

## 5. Rating implications — proposed 13 subjective ratings

### AI Thesis (D1–D5)

**D1. AI/Robotics Revenue % — 4**
- *Rationale:* Unmanned Systems segment = $82.6M / $371.0M = **22.3%** of Q1 2026 revenue; adding the undisclosed drone/cruise-missile turbine-engine line inside KGS gives an **estimated 25–35%** autonomy-tied revenue — band 4 (25–50%), not band 5.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 FY2026 (period ended 2026-03-29), segment revenue disaggregation.
- *Confidence:* Medium — the engine split is not disclosed; the 25–35% is my estimate and is flagged as such.

**D2. Supply Chain Position — 4**
- *Rationale:* Kratos **owns the engine** (Kratos Turbine Technologies small turbojets/turbofans) — a real bottleneck for jet-powered attritable aircraft, of which there are few Western suppliers — and is the only listed pure-ish attritable-jet airframe play. Not 5: it is a supplier into a Northrop-primed program, and Anduril/GA-ASI/Boeing are credible alternatives at the airframe level.
- *Source:* 10-Q segment structure; Northrop/Kratos 2026-01-08 announcement.
- *Confidence:* Medium.

**D3. Moat Strength — 4**
- *Rationale:* Durable in two dimensions — a decades-old, largely sole-source target-drone franchise with USAF/Navy, and small-expendable-turbojet design/production know-how that few US firms hold. The 20+ Valkyrie/F-35 flight demonstrations are a real qualification barrier. Not 5: the product is deliberately *attritable and low-cost*, i.e. designed to be commoditised, and the autonomy layer (the durable part) belongs to Northrop's Prism on the flagship program.
- *Source:* Northrop/Kratos 2026-01-08; USNI 2026-01-12.
- *Confidence:* Medium. *Bias check applied:* rating would be the same with the stock flat — the moat claim rests on the engine and the incumbency, not the price.

**D4. Capacity Expansion — 5**
- *Rationale:* Major buildout, funded and underway: **$1.35B net equity raise Feb 2026**, $1.46B cash, zero LT debt, multi-state facility expansions, engine capacity scale-up — with customer commitments (CCA, target drones, Nomad acquisition) behind it. This is committed capital on the balance sheet, not a press release.
- *Source:* 10-Q Q1 FY2026 cash-flow statement ("Proceeds from the issuance of common stock, net of issuance costs 1,348.6") and balance sheet.
- *Confidence:* High.

**D5. AI-buildout tie — 4 · L11 lens (proposed)**
- *Rationale:* Valkyrie is the airframe on a **USMC CCA program of record** ($231.5M OTA, Jan 2026) plus a second European demand pool via Airbus — a genuinely contracted tie to the FY27 ~$75B drone/autonomy budget. Not 5 because Kratos is the *subcontractor* (Northrop prime, Northrop autonomy), and awarded dollars to date are small relative to a $1.75B revenue base.
- *Source:* Kratos/Northrop 2026-01-08; Airbus Mar 2026; `research/2026-08-02-defense-autonomy-perception.md`.
- *Confidence:* Medium.
