## 1. Pre-research mental model (verbatim, written before any data pull)

> "Seagate is one of two remaining nearline HDD suppliers (with Western Digital), a rational duopoly that stopped fighting for share and started harvesting price. The AI angle is that model training and inference generate enormous volumes of cold and warm data that will not economically live on NAND — $/TB for HDD is still roughly 5-6x better. HAMR (Mozaic) is the technology transition that resets areal density and, more importantly, lets Seagate sell more TB per head/platter, i.e. margin expansion without unit growth. My prior: revenue rebuilding off a cyclical trough toward $2.5-3B/quarter, gross margin recovering into the high 30s/low 40s, HAMR qualification progressing but still a minority of nearline exabytes. The risk is that HDD is a melting ice cube if NAND $/TB ever crosses over."

Practical effect: with the shares far above the exchange price, holders overwhelmingly convert rather than take cash — so this **retires converts into equity dilution rather than cash out the door**. R3-relevant in both directions: less debt, more shares.

**FCF-conversion check (rule 2b / §2b):** FY-0 98% (FCF $3.10B / NI $3.18B), FY-1 56%. Only one year below 60% — **no flag**, and the trend is the right way. One line to watch: **receivables +60% vs revenue +34%**. On a business that has moved to multi-year allocated supply agreements with a handful of hyperscalers, longer/larger receivables are structurally explicable, but this is the metric to re-check next quarter rather than assume away.

**Expectations flag (rule 14 / §2c):** `expectations_flag.py STX` → **clean**, but the numbers deserve to be read rather than filed: **P/S 21.06 = 99.1st percentile of its own 3-year range** — the most extreme multiple position of any name in this rotation — against revenue YoY 33.8% vs a 3-year median of 2.1%. The flag correctly does not fire, because growth is enormously above its own median. But the honest statement of the setup is: *the market is paying a 3-year-record multiple on the assumption that a business whose median growth over three years was 2% has permanently re-rated to 30%+.* That assumption may well be right — supply is allocated into 2028 — but it should be argued explicitly, which is exactly what rule 14 exists to force.

## 6. Implications for ratings (flag only — no ratings decided here)

- **D1 (AI revenue %)** — datacenter at ~90% of exabytes, with the demand explicitly AI-driven. This is a much more direct AI tie than "storage benefits eventually." Revisit.
- **D5 (AI-buildout tie)** — multi-year allocated supply agreements running into 2028 with named hyperscalers is about as contracted as rule-23's parallel scale asks for. Revisit.
- **D3 (moat)** — a two-supplier structure where output is sold out three years forward is pricing power, and the 52.7% margin is the evidence. Revisit.
- **R1 (customer concentration)** — the flip side: ~90% datacenter with a handful of CSPs is *more* concentrated than the historical mixed OEM/channel book. Should move in the opposite direction from D5 on the same facts; do not let the good news carry both.
- **R3 (balance sheet)** — converts retiring 2026-09-08, largely into shares. Net debt improves, share count rises.
- **Quality inputs (rule 9)** — TTM gross/FCF margin will badly understate a business at 52.7% and $1.1B quarterly FCF after 13 quarters of expansion. **This is the exact TTM-vs-MRQ trap CLAUDE.md rule 9 documents.** Note the MRQ values on refresh.
- **Value inputs** — P/S at the 99.1st percentile of its 3-year range. Not a flag, but the single most important thing to argue about on this name.

**Thesis-break signals: none.** The tension here is valuation and concentration, not thesis.
