## Pre-research mental model

Expand Energy is the largest US natural gas producer (~7.5 Bcfe/d), formed October 2024 from Chesapeake + Southwestern, with a dual-basin footprint: Haynesville (Louisiana/Texas, short-haul to Gulf Coast LNG) and Appalachia (Marcellus/Utica). The equity story I carried into this refresh is merger-synergy capture plus deleveraging, converting into large capital returns, with the AI angle strictly second-derivative — gas-fired generation for data centers and LNG exports lift Henry Hub, which lifts EXE's realizations. As of the 2026-06-09 briefing there was still **no signed hyperscaler or data-center supply agreement** anywhere in the record, only "conversations," and even the flagship Delfin LNG SPA was priced at Henry Hub with no premium. My working assumption entering this pass was that Q2 would be much weaker than Q1's Winter-Storm-Fern blowout on a collapsing gas strip, and that the AI/data-center story would remain narrative rather than contracted.

## DIFF vs prior mental model

**Confirmed, and now on stronger evidence:**
- **Still no signed data-center, hyperscaler, or behind-the-meter power agreement.** This is now confirmed three ways: no Item 1.01 in any of the four 8-Ks since the last briefing; the single "data center" reference in the Q2 10-Q is macro MD&A price commentary; and the Twin Eagle press release — EXE's largest strategic announcement of the year — never mentions data centers or AI at all. Prior D1/D5 evidence holds.
- **Price-taker status confirmed, and worse.** The discount to NYMEX widened from $0.12/Mcf in Q1 to **$0.48/Mcf in Q2**.
- **LNG still outranks data centers as the demand driver** in the layer (3.7 Bcf/d LNG feedgas vs ~0.5 Bcf/d power burn) — unchanged from June.

**Changed / new since 2026-06-09:**
- **The commodity regime broke the other way.** Q1's blowout ($5.04 NYMEX, $1.7B quarterly FCF) reversed to $2.90 NYMEX and ~$245M of Q2 FCF. The hedge book flipped from a $0.64/Mcf drag to a $0.48/Mcf benefit — exactly what a >65% floor is for, and a point in favor of the hedging posture I read negatively in June as "the opposite of a bottleneck owner." Both readings are true; the hedge is a price-taker's tool *and* it worked.
- **A $1.25B acquisition that is genuinely strategic, not bolt-on.** Twin Eagle is EXE's first real attempt to move **downstream toward the customer** — 5 Bcf/d marketed, 44 Bcf storage, 2 Bcf/d firm transport, 1,000+ customers, reach to ~90% of the North American market, with a claimed $0.05–0.10/unit breakeven reduction and marketing/commercial FCF target raised 50% to $750M/yr. My June model treated EXE as a pure upstream price-taker with no path off Henry Hub. This is the path management is choosing — unproven, unclosed, and not yet revenue, but it is a real change in strategic direction and it is the mechanism by which a future direct-to-power deal would actually be signed.
- **Deleveraging is essentially complete, and now partially reverses.** Total debt $5.0B → $3.7B, ~0.5x leverage. But Twin Eagle draws on the revolver, so the trough in leverage is Q2 2026.
- **Delfin SPA went from conditional to effective** (all conditions precedent satisfied at 2026-06-30), with ~$2.9B of fixed/determinable commitments. Still Henry-Hub-priced — no premium — and note it is EXE **purchasing** LNG offtake, a volume/optionality position, not a premium-price contract.
- **Management instability is far larger than I had modeled — this is the biggest miss in the prior briefing.** EXE has run on an **interim CEO since February 2026**, with the prior CEO off the board entirely, an HQ move to Houston, a CFO four months into the job, a Controller who resigned in June leaving the CFO as interim principal accounting officer, and a director retiring. The June briefing saw the April 5.02 filings and logged them as routine "officer/director changes" without connecting them to the February CEO departure. A $1.25B acquisition signed under that configuration deserves more weight than "customary."
- **Relative strength has broken down.** −31.6pp vs XLE over six months, near the bottom of the range. In June I had no momentum read in the briefing at all.

**Unchanged and worth restating:** capital returns are real and accelerating (~4% of shares in 1H, new $1B authorization, dividend flat), production guidance was reaffirmed rather than cut, and unit costs are drifting up ~8.5% y/y — a modest but real quality erosion.

## Implications for ratings

*No rating numbers proposed. What follows is which dimensions the new evidence touches and why.*

**D1 (AI Revenue %) — evidence reaffirms, do not move on the Twin Eagle narrative.** Zero disclosed dollars flow from AI- or data-center-specific contracts. Twin Eagle's end markets are described categorically ("power, industrial, LNG"), and its own press release omits data centers entirely. The distinction the task asks me to hold is exactly the live one here: **a marketing platform with utility relationships is a distribution channel, not a contracted data-center revenue stream.** Any D1 uplift on Twin Eagle would be the "counting data-center exposure as AI exposure" failure mode, one level further removed.

**D2 (Supply Chain Position) — genuinely two-sided, and this is the interesting rating this quarter.** Against: the NYMEX discount **widened 4× to $0.48/Mcf**, unit cash costs rose 8.5% y/y, and the Delfin SPA remains Henry-Hub-priced — all pure price-taker evidence, stronger than what drove the June read. In favor: Twin Eagle is a deliberate, funded, $1.25B attempt to capture midstream/marketing margin and reach the customer directly, with a quantified breakeven benefit. The rubric warns against rating Position on past pricing power rather than forward-looking, but it equally warns that announced ≠ committed. The deal is **signed but not closed** (FERC 203(a), HSR, Canada Competition Act pending, outside date 2027-01-24). The honest framing for the rating session: does a signed-but-unclosed integration strategy move Position before it demonstrably changes realizations? My read of the rubric is that it does not yet — but this is the dimension to revisit at close and again once integrated marketing margin shows up in realizations.

**D4 (Capacity Expansion) — mildly informed, probably stable.** Capex guidance reaffirmed at $2.75–2.95B with 11–12 rigs and 205–235 wells; volumes +3.9% y/y; capex explicitly "easing in 2H"; Western Haynesville appraisal is real but small (~$75M, first horizontal well Q4). This is steady-state-to-modest-growth upstream investment. The $1.25B acquisition is **not** productive capacity — folding it into D4 would misread the dimension.

**D5 (Hyperscaler/AI-buildout tie) — reaffirms the floor; strictly narrative.** Nothing contracted, no named counterparty, no volume, no term. Management's own framing routes through utilities and microgrid developers — intermediaries selling to data centers, which is the rubric's "indirect exposure — selling to mid-tier customers who sell to hyperscalers," not a supplier relationship. The Microsoft/Chevron and Meta/Vistra deals surfaced in search are the contrast case: that is what a 5 looks like in this layer, and EXE has no analogue.

**M1 (EPS Revisions) — new evidence, mixed and worth re-examining.** Estimates were being revised **down** into the print (Seeking Alpha preview), then the company beat adjusted EPS by ~19%. The revision trend and the surprise point opposite ways; the gas-strip reset argues the revision direction is the more durable signal. Needs a fresh Yahoo/Zacks revision pull at rating time — I did not obtain one.

**M2 (Relative Strength) — the clearest candidate for change.** −31.6pp vs XLE over six months, price near the bottom of its range. This sits outside the rubric's lagging-by->20% boundary. Per the rubric's own bias check, this is a momentum input, not a quality input — the read should not be softened because the balance sheet improved.

**M3 (Insider Activity) — no new evidence; prior basis stands but is aging.** The only logged transaction is the CFO's 2,000-share open-market buy at $96.43 on 2026-05-08 (~$193k, news-log). I found no Form 4 activity since. Note the shares now trade **below** that purchase price ($94.66), and note that the May buy was by a CFO five weeks into the role. A fresh Form 4 sweep is warranted at rating time, particularly given the executive churn.

**R1 (Customer Concentration) — mildly improving.** Commodity sales are inherently diversified; Twin Eagle adds 1,000+ customers across the US and Canada. No customer >10% is disclosed. Direction is favorable, magnitude small.

**R3 (Balance Sheet) — materially improved, with a partial give-back and a governance overlay.** Total debt $5.0B → $3.7B, net debt $3.1B, ~0.5x leverage, $3.5B revolver availability, $37M gain on note extinguishment, and ~4% of shares retired. That is a strong, clean improvement on the leverage leg. Two offsets to weigh: Twin Eagle is funded partly from the revolver, so Q2 2026 is the leverage trough, not a new baseline; and per rule 23, R3 is the **holistic** balance-sheet/maturities/dilution read rather than the raw ND/EBITDA ratio already scored in Quality — so the collinearity caution applies, and R3 should not simply re-award the leverage improvement that Quality already captures.

**R4 (Regulatory) — new, transaction-specific closing risk.** Twin Eagle requires FERC Section 203(a) approval, HSR expiration, and Canada Competition Act clearance, with an outside date of 2027-01-24. This is ordinary-course for a gas marketing acquisition, but it is a live, dated condition rather than an abstraction.

**R5 (Disruption Risk) — unchanged at the default.** Rule 16 scopes sub-5 R5 to Layer 10 only; EXE is Layer 1 physical infrastructure with no credible AI/structural disruption to the revenue model.

**Rubric-fit gap worth surfacing to Dom (not a rating proposal).** The single largest new risk item this quarter — **an interim CEO for six months, an ongoing CEO search, a four-month-tenured CFO doubling as interim principal accounting officer, a departed Controller, and a $1.25B acquisition signed under that configuration** — has **no clean home in R1–R5**. R1 is customers, R2 geography, R3 balance sheet, R4 regulatory, R5 disruption. Management/execution risk currently falls through the framework. Per the standing preference for framework-vs-name separation, the right handling is probably a name-specific note rather than stretching R3's definition — but it should be a conscious decision at the rating session, not a silent omission.

**Thesis-break signal: NONE.** No customer loss, no contract cancellation, no severe regulatory action, no going-concern issue, and **no guidance cut** — 2026 production (7.4–7.6 Bcfe/d) and capex ($2.75–2.95B) were both reaffirmed. The Q2 earnings decline is a commodity-price reset, which is the expected behavior of a hedged price-taker, not a break in the thesis. The interim-CEO situation and the Twin Eagle closing conditions are **watch items**, not breaks.
