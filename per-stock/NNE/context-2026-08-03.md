## Pre-research mental model

(Written before this pass's searching, from `context-2026-06-10.md`, `thesis.md` — which is still the unedited template — and the one-row `news-log.md`.) NNE is a pre-revenue microreactor developer whose story rests on three concepts — KRONOS (stationary high-temperature gas-cooled MMR, the only one actually in NRC licensing), ZEUS (portable solid-core battery reactor, non-nuclear scale testing only) and ODIN/LOKI (space and other, effectively dormant) — plus subsidiary ambitions in HALEU fuel supply and nuclear-materials transport. I believed the company had ~$569M of cash built entirely from repeated equity raises (IPO, Nov-2024 and May-2025 PIPEs, a $400M Oct-2025 private placement), roughly zero revenue apart from a small logistics business (STS) bought in May 2026, a KRONOS construction permit application accepted by the NRC for a demonstration unit on the University of Illinois campus, and zero binding datacenter power contracts — the AI angle being 100% MOU and narrative (Supermicro, BaRupOn). My working view was that this is among the most speculative names in the watchlist: the entire equity value is an option on a prototype reactor that will not produce electricity until roughly 2029+, funded by a cash pile that dilutes shareholders every time it is topped up.

## Implications for ratings

Framing only — no numbers proposed, per the task. What moved and why:

- **D1 (AI Revenue % of Total).** No change in substance, but the *test* of it is imminent. Revenue remains zero in the last filed period ("we have not generated any material revenue," 10-Q 2026-05-14), and even the incoming STS revenue (~$7.1M/yr of nuclear-materials logistics) is not AI-driven. The fiscal Q3 10-Q, expected ~2026-08-13, will be the first filing to show any revenue at all — worth waiting for before touching this dimension.

- **D2 (Supply Chain Position).** Mildly *strengthened* on process, *weakened* on people. Strengthened: the NRC has actually started its review with a published schedule, and KRONOS now has a contracted USAF SBIR Phase I plus a progressing Fortil fuel-handling design — evidence of an engineering programme rather than slideware. Weakened: the CTO/Head of Reactor Development was terminated with no successor and the CEO is covering the role on an interim basis. For a company whose only asset is technical execution, an unexplained departure of the top technical officer is directly on-point for how defensible the position is.

- **D3 (Moat Strength).** Watch, don't move yet. The regulatory head-start is intact and now further along than in June, but it remains a *procedural* lead (construction permit, not an operating licence) and the proposed NRC Part 57 microreactor framework can compress it for everyone. The Fortil dependency also shows core subsystem engineering is being done by an outside firm — relevant to whether the IP is genuinely in-house.

- **D4 (Capacity Expansion).** Newly informed and arguably the most changed dimension. There is now a hard number on what capacity actually costs: **$300–350M for the first KRONOS unit** against ~$569M of cash. That converts "long runway" into "one unit, then a raise." Any capacity-expansion credit should be tested against funding capacity, not against ambition.

- **D5 (Hyperscaler Exposure / AI Infrastructure Commitment).** No improvement whatsoever. Zero binding datacenter agreements — no 8-K Item 1.01 since the STS acquisition on 2026-05-22, ten-plus weeks ago. Supermicro is still an explicitly non-binding MOU; BaRupOn is still a completed feasibility study with two *unnamed* hyperscalers "evaluating" a *third party's* campus. Under rule 23's parallel-scale framing, the tie to the AI buildout remains entirely uncontracted. If anything the newest genuine contract (AFWERX) points at defense, not datacenters.

- **M1 (EPS Revisions).** Formally low-information — a pre-revenue developer's EPS line is a burn rate, and the six-month loss shrank only because prior-year equity comp was $16.5M higher. Any revision signal here is noise; treat accordingly rather than reading it as fundamental improvement.

- **M2 (Relative Strength vs Sector).** Clearly *deteriorated* and this is the most mechanically forced change of the pass. $17.58 on 2026-08-03 vs a $56.63 high in October 2025; a fresh 52-week low of $14.88 on 2026-07-29; -31.4% in thirty days; only 30% of the last 120 sessions above the 50-day SMA (which also feeds the objective 50DMA % input under rule 11, currently ~30 — a low band). Short interest at ~33% of float compounds the picture, though it cuts both ways.

- **M3 (Insider Activity).** No new Form 4 activity since the 2026-06-03/05 cluster in which the chairman's entity sold net 700,000 shares at $25.70–29.91 under a 10b5-1 plan. Two things to weigh honestly against the project's own principle that selling is ambiguous: the sales are pre-scheduled, *but* the stock is now ~40% below where they printed, and **there has been no insider buying into a 69% drawdown** — the absence of a bid from management is the more informative half.

- **R1 (Customer Concentration).** Technically undefined with no revenue, but note the incoming STS business is a small government/national-lab/commercial logistics book, and the newest KRONOS work is a USAF SBIR. If R1 gets touched, rule 26's government-customer convention (grade diversification *within* the government book rather than flooring it) is the relevant precedent.

- **R3 (Balance Sheet Risk).** Genuinely two-sided and worth an explicit re-argument rather than a carry-forward.
