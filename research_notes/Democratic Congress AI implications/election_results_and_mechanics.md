## Q3. What can a Democratic Congress do without the President?

### Takeaway
With both chambers, Democrats gain **agenda control, subpoena power in every committee, appropriations leverage (including shutdown brinkmanship), the ability to block any Republican bill, and, in the Senate, the power to stall or reject Trump nominees** (FERC, FTC, Commerce/BIS, etc.). They **cannot pass law over a veto, cannot use the Congressional Review Act against Trump rules (the President must sign a CRA resolution), and cannot overcome a GOP filibuster** on their own bills. Most mechanics below are **background knowledge of standing rules, not sourced this session**.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences (procedural, background knowledge unless cited)
| Tool | What Democrats can do from Jan 3, 2027 | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigations/subpoenas** | Every committee chair can issue subpoenas (House rules usually delegate this to chairs). Likely targets: Nvidia/BIS licensing, AI-company dealings with the administration, data-center cost shifting to ratepayers, AI chip smuggling. | Enforcing against the executive branch is slow (contempt referrals go to a DOJ run by the President; civil suits take months to years). Private companies usually comply. Reputational and headline risk is the main channel to stocks. |
| **Appropriations / riders** | Control both chambers' spending bills; can attach riders (e.g., barring funds for specific chip-export licenses, conditioning DOE/FERC funding, defunding AI preemption litigation task forces). Shutdown leverage. | Every appropriations bill still needs Trump's signature and 60 Senate votes, so riders become bargaining chips, not guaranteed outcomes. A lame-duck CR running into early 2027 would hand Democrats this leverage; a full-year bill passed in December would deny it. |
| **Blocking GOP legislation** | Federal preemption of state AI laws, permitting reform on GOP terms and any Trump legislative priority die unless Democrats agree. | Blocking is the strongest power: nothing passes without the Speaker and Majority Leader scheduling it. |
| **Senate confirmations** (Senate flipped) | Committee chairs control hearings; the majority leader controls floor time. Democrats can stall or defeat nominees for FERC, FTC, NRC, Commerce Under Secretary for BIS, SEC, Fed governors, judges. | Trump can rely on acting officials (Vacancies Act time limits apply) and holdover commissioners. Recess appointments are possible only if the Senate actually recesses, which a Democratic Senate controls through pro forma sessions. |
| **Congressional Review Act** | Practically useless offensively: a disapproval resolution must be signed by the President, and Trump would veto disapproval of his own rules. | Conversely, a Democratic Congress removes Republicans' ability to use the CRA against Biden-era or state-friendly rules. |
| **Veto override** | Not available: needs 290 House / 67 Senate. | — |
| **Tax** | W&M (Neal) and Finance (Wyden) can block extensions and hold hearings on tax treatment of data centers/AI. | Most expiring 2017 provisions were dealt with in the 2025 reconciliation law (background knowledge), so 2027 has fewer "must-pass" tax cliffs to bargain over. Not verified this session. |
| **Reconciliation** | A Democratic budget resolution plus reconciliation can't become law without Trump's signature. | — |

- **Bottom line for AI stocks (inference, Medium confidence):** the new Congress is a **headline/oversight and blocking** risk, not a **statutory** risk, through at least January 2029. The two real policy channels are (1) Senate confirmation control over FERC/FTC/BIS personnel and (2) appropriations leverage. Most binding AI/data-center constraints will come from **states and utility commissions** (Q4), not federal law.

## Q4. AI, data centers and electricity prices in the campaign, exit polls, and state results

### Takeaway
**Affordability was the dominant issue, and data centers became one of its sharpest local expressions.** About 90% of voters called the cost of living a major factor. Polling shows broad opposition to local data centers (about three-quarters), although only about a quarter called data centers crucial to their vote. State results matter more for AI infrastructure than Congress does: Democrats flipped governorships in **Texas, Ohio, Georgia, Nevada and Iowa** on platforms hostile to data centers, and won a **3-2 majority on the Georgia PSC** that plans an "affordability docket" on data-center cost allocation. Confidence: **Medium** (secondary aggregators; AP VoteCast primary release not reached).

## Q5. Lame-duck session (Nov-Dec 2026): what AI and energy items are Republicans trying to pass?

### Takeaway
The GOP lame-duck agenda is **shrinking after the losses**. Must-dos are **government funding before December 11** and the **FY2027 NDAA**. **Permitting reform** is Majority Leader Thune's main energy goal (procedural vote scheduled 2026-11-09), but Republican aides doubt it passes before Christmas. **No 2026 report was found of a live push to put AI preemption in the NDAA.** The 2025 attempt stalled and leaders said they were "looking at other places." Confidence: **Medium**.
