## 1. Federal Democratic bills and proposals on data centers (ratepayer protection, moratoria, large-load tariffs, disclosure)

### Takeaway
Nothing data-center-specific from Democrats has become federal law. The live federal vehicles are (a) the bipartisan, House-passed **Ratepayer Protection Act**, which Senate Democrats blocked on Sept 30, 2026 as "toothless" because it only requires states to *consider* making large loads pay; (b) Democratic mandatory alternatives: Heinrich's **GRID Savings Act** (S. 5199, FERC jurisdiction over loads above 150 MW, costs assigned to the customer) and a Schiff bill; (c) the progressive **AI Data Center Moratorium Act** (Sanders/Ocasio-Cortez), which has no path to passage; and (d) ratepayer provisions inside the **bipartisan Senate permitting deal**, which is the most likely vehicle to actually pass.

### Cited Findings
- **[PROPOSED – passed House, blocked in Senate] Ratepayer Protection Act (Republican-led):** passed the House 417–3, including 206 Democrats. It would require state utility regulators to *consider* making large power users such as data centers pay for the grid upgrades needed to serve them. It failed cloture in the Senate 57–43 on **Sept 30, 2026**. Four Democrats voted yes: Hassan, Klobuchar, Ossoff and Warnock — [Valley News Live, 2026-09-30](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/09/30/senate-democrats-block-bill-shield-ratepayers-data-center-costs-57-43-vote/); [Al Jazeera, 2026-09-30](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/us-senate-rejects-bill-targeting-ai-data-centre-electricity-costs); [Newsweek vote list](https://www.newsweek.com/ai-data-center-bill-democrats-voting-republicans-list-12509123); [Utility Dive on House passage](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/house-passes-ratepayer-protection-bill-data-centers/830658/). (High confidence on the vote counts; multiple outlets agree.)
- One summary says the Ratepayer Protection Act uses a **100 MW** large-load threshold. Other reporting conflates this with the GRID Savings Act's 150 MW — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/one-senate-democrats-answer-to-trumps-ratepayer-protection-pledge/); [Substack analysis: "Congress drew the line at 150 megawatts. FERC drew it at 20"](https://todorinchev.substack.com/p/congress-drew-the-line-at-150-megawatts). (Low–Medium confidence on the thresholds.)
- **[RHETORIC] Schumer's objection:** Democrats want it to be *mandatory by law* that data centers cover their own costs, and he called the "consider" standard "toothless." Schumer said on July 14, 2026 that data centers should "pay their fair share" and use more clean energy — [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/politics/democrats-block-data-center-electricity-bill-calling-it-toothless/); [Bloomberg Government, "Schumer Urges More Clean Energy to Fuel 'Important' Data Centers"](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bloomberg-government-news/schumer-urges-more-clean-energy-to-fuel-important-data-centers); [ABC News 4, leaders point fingers](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/senates-top-party-leaders-chuck-schumer-john-thune-point-fingers-after-house-data-center-ai-bill-fails-congress). Note that Schumer's framing calls data centers "important": he wants them to pay their way, not stop being built.
- **[PROPOSED] GRID Savings Act (S. 5199), Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-NM, ENR ranking member), introduced Aug 3, 2026:** amends the Federal Power Act to give FERC jurisdiction over interconnection of large loads (above 150 MW) to transmission. It requires a FERC final rule that applies to all transmitting utilities, assigns interconnection costs to the customer rather than the rate base, and requires a payment guarantee that survives the facility closing. ERCOT is excluded. One account says Sen. Bernie Moreno (R) blocked it on Sept 17 — [NACo](https://www.naco.org/news/senate-energy-and-natural-resources-ranking-member-introduces-legislation-protect-ratepayers); [Los Alamos Daily Post](https://ladailypost.com/heinrich-introduces-legislation-to-ensure-large-users-of-electricity-pay-for-grid-upgrades/); [GCN](https://gcn.com/grid-upgrade-costs-senate-bill-would/21928); [AllCiv bill page S.5199](https://allciv.com/bills/federal/119/5199S119). (Medium confidence. The Moreno-block detail comes from a lower-quality crypto outlet, [cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/heinrich-blocks-data-center-ratepayer-protection-act/).)
- **[PROPOSED] Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) ratepayer bill, unveiled May 18, 2026:** described as legislation "to ensure fair and affordable energy costs for Americans amid data center buildouts" that would require data centers to fund their own power — [Schiff press release, 2026-05-18](https://www.schiff.senate.gov/news/press-releases/news-sen-schiff-unveils-major-legislation-to-ensure-fair-and-affordable-energy-costs-for-americans-amid-data-center-buildouts/); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/schiff-enters-data-center-fray-with-ratepayer-protection-bill/). (Bill name and number not captured; see Gaps.)
- **[PROPOSED] AI Data Center Moratorium Act (Sanders / Ocasio-Cortez, announced March 2026):** an open-ended moratorium on building or upgrading AI data centers above 20 MW. Lifting it would require Congress to pass legislation meeting conditions that include federal review of AI products, proof of no electricity-cost increases for nearby communities, and mandatory union jobs. It has not advanced beyond committee. One source reports about 20 Democratic cosponsors — [Sanders press release](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-ocasio-cortez-announce-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/); [Mother Jones, 2026-03](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/03/bernie-sanders-ai-senate-bill-data-center-construction-moratorium-aoc-alexandria-ocasio-cortez/); [MLQ.ai, post-midterm](https://mlq.ai/news/democrats-data-center-moratorium-push-gains-momentum-after-midterms-but-congress-remains-a-long-shot/).
- **[RHETORIC] Republican crossover on the moratorium:** after the midterms, Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO) called for a nationwide moratorium on new data center construction until Congress guarantees that electricity demand won't raise bills. Reports call him the first GOP senator to back a pause — [The National Desk](https://thenationaldesk.com/news/connect-to-congress/hawley-backs-data-center-moratorium-as-washington-weighs-new-restrictions-artificial-intelligence-midterm-elections-electricity-water-usage); [Benzinga, Nov 2026](https://benzinga.com/news/politics/26/11/63172338/first-gop-senator-joins-democrats-in-demanding-nationwide-data-center-moratorium-until-congress-passes-a-bill-of-rights-protecting-americans-from-rising-power-bills).
- **[RHETORIC] Campaign pledges:** Heatmap reports that "almost every single Senate Democrat who won promised to make data centers pay for their full cost of electricity service." Oversight of the administration's handling of the data-center buildout, and of moves to bring FERC and the NRC under White House control, is expected — [Heatmap, "Democrats Retake Congress"](https://heatmap.news/politics/democrats-retake-the-house).

### Inferences
- The center of gravity among Democrats is **"make them pay," not "stop them."** 206 House Democrats voted for even the weak version, and Senate Democrats blocked it for being too weak, not too strong. A moratorium is a progressive-flank position with no path to a 60-vote Senate or a presidential signature. (Medium–High confidence.)
- The most likely federal outcome in 2027–28 is a **mandatory cost-causation standard** (large loads pay for their own interconnection and network upgrades) attached to a must-pass or bipartisan vehicle, most likely permitting or appropriations. That raises a developer's all-in cost per MW. It does not cut power demand, and utility capex grows if anything, because it is customer-funded rather than rate-based. Hawley's crossover suggests a populist-right coalition could form around tougher terms. (Medium confidence.)
- Under a GRID-Savings-style regime, **regulated utilities and grid-equipment vendors are largely insulated**: the upgrades still get built, only the payer changes. The marginal risk falls on **developers and hyperscalers' cost of capacity** and on speculative queue positions, which collateral and exit-fee rules squeeze out.

- Could not confirm the Schiff bill's name, number or mechanics, or the GRID Savings Act's cosponsor count.

- Behind-the-meter generation: found no Democratic federal bill specifically restricting it. FERC's co-location proceeding (Q3) is the operative venue.

## 2. How central were electricity prices and data centers to the 2026 midterms, and what did Democrats promise?

### Takeaway
Very central. Electricity bills, with data centers as the named culprit, were a top-tier "affordability" message. Democrats outspent Republicans on data-center ads more than 3:1, and Democratic gubernatorial winners in data-center hot spots (TX, OH, GA) ran on making data centers "pay their fair share," conditional moratoria and rate freezes. How decisive the issue was is contested.

## 3. Federal energy levers: FERC, DOE, permitting, transmission, nuclear, IRA credits, gas

### Takeaway
Most federal levers stay with the **Trump administration through January 2029**: FERC has a 3–2 Republican-chaired majority, DOE controls emergency orders, and every bill needs the President's signature. A Democratic Congress gains oversight, appropriations leverage, Senate confirmation control of future FERC and NRC nominees, and veto-proof leverage only where the issue is bipartisan. Restoring the IRA wind and solar credits is Democrats' top stated clean-energy priority but is **very unlikely to become law before 2029** unless it is traded in a must-pass deal. A bipartisan **permitting deal with ratepayer provisions** is the one major energy bill with momentum: the Senate cleared a procedural vote 73–25 in the lame duck.

### Cited Findings
**FERC large-load interconnection (DOE-directed rulemaking RM26-4)**
- DOE asked FERC to act on large-load interconnection by **April 30, 2026**. FERC missed that date and, on **June 18, 2026**, issued tailored **show-cause orders to the six RTOs** instead of a generic final rule. Each RTO had 60 days to justify its existing tariffs or file large-load provisions. The orders preliminarily define a large load as ≥50 MW at one site above 69 kV (DOE had proposed 20 MW). Docket RM26-4-000 remains open — [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/ferc_acts_advance_data_center_large_load_integration_six_rto_regions_0626.html); [HyperSinc](https://hypersinc.com/article/ferc-misses-doe-deadline-on-data-center-grid-access-rule-1777568499276); [Kirkland (background on the DOE ANOPR)](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/11/potential-game-changing-rulemaking-for-data-centers); [PJM-hosted FERC order dated 2026-06-18, EL26-67-000](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/orders/2026/20260618-el26-67-000.pdf). (Medium confidence. The 50 MW vs 20 MW threshold is reported inconsistently across sources.)
- FERC granted abeyance motions from PJM and certain transmission owners on **Aug 14, 2026**, delaying show-cause responses. A rehearing order in the PJM proceeding is dated Sept 21, 2026 — [PJM-hosted FERC order 2026-09-21, EL26-67-001](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/orders/2026/20260921-el26-67-001.pdf). (Medium confidence; drawn from the search digest of these PDFs.)

**FERC co-location (PJM)**
- **[ENACTED – regulatory]** In **December 2025**, FERC ordered PJM to create new transmission services for co-located loads such as data centers and to set clearer co-location rules. Chairman Swett and Commissioners See and LaCerte formed the majority; Rosner and Chang concurred separately — [FERC news release](https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-directs-nations-largest-grid-operator-create-new-rules-embrace-innovation-and); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ferc-orders-pjm-largest-u-s-grid-operator-to-revise-tariff-to-permit-new-transmission-services-for-co-located-loads-such-as-data-centers/); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2025/december/ferc-issues-order-providing-guidance-for-co-locating-power-plants-with-data-centers-within-pjm). A PJM compliance filing was accepted in part, effective Oct 12, 2026 — [FERC order 2026-10-09, ER26-3515](https://www.pjm.com/pjmfiles/directory/etariff/FercOrders/9207/20261009-er26-3515-000.pdf).
- **FERC composition:** 2026 orders list Laura Swett (Chairman), David Rosner, Lindsay See, Judy Chang and David LaCerte — [FERC order PDF, 2026-04, ER26-1088](https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/ER26-1088-000.pdf). - Heatmap reports Democrats will use oversight on the administration's move to bring FERC and the NRC under direct White House control — [Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/politics/democrats-retake-the-house).

**Permitting reform and transmission**
- **[PROPOSED]** The House passed the **SPEED Act (H.R. 4776)** 221–196 on **Dec 18, 2025** (NEPA reform) — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/house-passes-speed-act-a-first-step-toward-broader-permitting-reform/); [BHFS](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/bipartisan-permitting-deal-passes-house-senate-up-next-with-speed-bumps-ahead/).

### Inferences
- **FERC is the most consequential federal venue, and Democrats do not control it.** The large-load and co-location framework will be written by a Republican-chaired FERC, which has favored "speed plus consumer protection" through show-cause orders rather than a national rule. A Democratic Senate can (a) refuse confirmation of future nominees as terms expire, potentially leaving seats vacant, (b) use hearings and letters, and (c) legislate jurisdiction (GRID Savings Act) only with a presidential signature. (Medium confidence.)
- **IRA restoration:** effectively zero probability of enactment before Jan 2029 absent a bargain (Trump veto, and 54–56 Senate seats is below 60). Even reconciliation needs the President's signature. Practical consequence: wind and solar still face the OBBBA end-2027 cliff. That keeps the near-term marginal data-center MW tilted to **gas, existing nuclear uprates and restarts, and storage**, which supports gas-turbine OEMs and IPPs with dispatchable fleets regardless of Democratic rhetoric. (Medium confidence.)
- **The permitting deal is the swing variable.** If it passes in the lame duck (Republican Senate) with data-center-pays transmission provisions, it de-risks transmission buildout (positive for grid equipment and transmission-heavy utilities) while formalizing cost-causation on hyperscalers. If it slips to 2027, the Democratic Senate's leverage rises and the terms may tighten. (Medium confidence.)
- DOE emergency orders (FPA 202(c), keeping fossil plants open) stay fully with the administration. Congress can only object or attach appropriations riders.

## 4. State-level actions most likely to matter (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
States are where Democratic power over data centers is real and immediate. Key changes: **Georgia PSC flipped to a 3–2 Democratic majority** (takes office January 2027) that is targeting Georgia Power's roughly 10 GW gas buildout; **Texas governor-elect Hinojosa** pledges a moratorium on new approvals on top of existing ERCOT and TCEQ pauses and SB6; **Ohio governor-elect Acton** wants conditional limits, though AEP Ohio's take-or-pay tariff already does much of that; **Virginia (Spanberger)** has an enacted data-center consumption tax and a proposed six-year residential rate freeze; **New Jersey (Sherrill)** froze rate hikes and signed a law for a separate data-center rate class. PJM capacity prices are pinned at caps through 2028/29. Local opposition blocked or delayed about 120 projects worth about $198B in H1 2026.

### Inferences
- **The highest-impact near-term risks are state-specific:** (1) **Georgia Power and Southern Co.:** a Democratic PSC can slow or shrink incremental gas certifications (the ~6 GW pending), tighten large-load cost recovery and stress-test load forecasts. That is negative for the incremental gas-turbine order flow tied to Georgia but does not undo the approved ~10 GW. (2) **Texas:** a gubernatorial pause on new approvals is the single biggest new constraint on the largest large-load queue in the US. It is executive-driven and reversible, and legislative change needs the Legislature. (3) **Virginia and Dominion:** a rate freeze as a merger condition compresses utility earnings growth on the residential side while shifting costs to data centers. (Medium confidence.)
- **The "large-load tariff" model is already the industry norm** (AEP Ohio take-or-pay, NJ 50 MW class, SB6, Georgia contracts; EEI tracks dozens). Democratic wins mainly **ratchet up minimum-take terms, collateral and cost allocation** rather than introducing a new instrument. This filters out speculative load. It probably helps utilities with credible forecasts and hurts speculative developers. (Medium confidence.)
- PJM caps paired with shortfalls show the binding constraint is **supply**. Democratic governors (Shapiro, Moore, Murphy's successor Sherrill, Spanberger) are pushing PJM reform and price relief, which squeezes **IPP capacity revenue upside** (CEG, VST, TLN and others) even as energy prices stay firm. (Medium confidence.)

## 5. Where Democratic interests align with the buildout: bipartisan zones

### Takeaway
Democrats are **not anti-buildout in the aggregate**. Leadership (Schumer) calls data centers "important" and wants them to pay their way and use clean power. The bipartisan zones most likely to produce law or action are: **mandatory ratepayer protection and cost-causation** (417–3 House vote; Hawley crossover), **transmission and permitting** (a 73–25 procedural vote with Heinrich and Whitehouse as co-authors), **nuclear** (ADVANCE Act lineage, Democratic governors embracing it, conditioned on NRC independence), **clean firm power** (geothermal, nuclear, fusion named by Schumer), possibly a **tech-neutral battery production credit**, and **union construction labor**, which progressives write into moratorium-lift conditions and Hinojosa wants as a requirement.

### Inferences
- **Federal vs state split (summary for the report writer):**

| Lever | Who controls through Jan 2029 | Democratic leverage | Likely direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large-load interconnection rules | FERC (3–2 R) | Oversight; confirmations; GRID Savings Act (needs signature) | Show-cause approach continues; cost-causation tightens modestly |
| Co-location / behind-the-meter | FERC | Same | PJM framework already ordered (Dec 2025) |
| IRA credit restoration | Congress + President | Can pass House and Senate (reconciliation) but faces a veto | Very unlikely before 2029 |
| Permitting / transmission | Congress + President | Bipartisan; Democrats split | Plausible enactment (lame duck or 2027) with data-center-pays terms |
| Nuclear support | Congress + NRC | Bipartisan, conditioned on NRC independence | Continued support; incremental |
| Moratorium | Congress | Progressive bill, no path | Not enacted federally |
| Retail rates, large-load tariffs, IRPs, gas certificates | State PUCs and governors | Direct (GA PSC; TX, OH, VA, NJ governors) | **Material, immediate** |
| Local zoning and referendums | Counties and municipalities | Indirect | Continuing high block/delay rate |

- **Net read for the supply chain (Medium confidence):** the Democratic sweep shifts *who pays* and *how fast approvals happen* more than *whether* power gets built. Relative winners: transmission and grid equipment (permitting deal, data-center-funded upgrades), nuclear (bipartisan), storage. Relative pressure: speculative data-center developers and site contractors in TX, OH and GA, incremental utility gas build in Georgia, IPP capacity-price upside in PJM, and residential rate-base growth at utilities facing rate freezes (VA, NJ). Gas turbines face *state-level* (GA PSC) headwinds, but federal policy cannot reverse the post-OBBBA tilt toward dispatchable gas before 2029.
