## Q0 (context). What did the 2026 midterms actually produce?

### Takeaway
The election outcome is bigger than "a Democratic House": multiple outlets project Democrats won **both** chambers on Nov 3, 2026, with Trump still president. That means unified Democratic control of committees and subpoena power from Jan 3, 2027, but legislation still faces a veto (and the Senate filibuster). ## Q1. Which specific AI bills have Democrats (or bipartisan coalitions with Democratic leads) introduced in the 119th Congress?

### Takeaway
None of the Democratic-led or bipartisan AI-specific bills below has been enacted; the furthest-advanced is the Hawley–Blumenthal **GUARD Act** (chatbots/minors), which cleared Senate Judiciary unanimously on 2026-04-30. The most market-relevant bipartisan bills pair Hawley (R) with a Democrat (Blumenthal, Durbin, Warner), which is exactly the profile that could survive a Trump veto calculation.

## Q2. Democratic position on federal preemption of state AI laws, and what changes with Democratic control

### Takeaway
Preemption has failed twice in Congress, both times with bipartisan opposition (the Senate stripped the reconciliation-bill moratorium 99–1 in July 2025), and Trump's December 2025 executive order cannot preempt state law on its own. With Democrats controlling both chambers in 2027, statutory preemption of state AI laws looks very unlikely; the fight shifts to the courts (DOJ AI Litigation Task Force vs. states) and to funding conditions. One caveat: Jeffries' AI commission co-chair Ted Lieu has said the goal is a *federal standard*, so a Democratic-written federal floor (with weaker or no preemption) is possible.

### Cited Findings
- The Senate voted 99–1 to strip a 10-year moratorium on state AI-law enforcement from the reconciliation bill (H.R. 1); a Cruz compromise (5 years with carve-outs) also failed. Opposition included state legislators in all 50 states, 40 state AGs and 17 Republican governors. — [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/?p=15667); [CAP report](https://www.americanprogress.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/11/UPDATED.PDF-2025.11.25-AIMoratoriumReport.pdf)
- Congress has twice rejected moratorium proposals; no federal preemption statute has been enacted; state laws remain enforceable — [Washington Legal Foundation, 2026-06-22](https://www.wlf.org/2026/06/22/wlf-legal-pulse/compromise-necessary-but-not-sufficient-for-ai-preemption/). (The user's premise of an NDAA preemption attempt was not confirmed by sources found this session — see Gaps.)
- Trump's Dec 11, 2025 EO directs the AG to create an "AI Litigation Task Force" within 30 days to challenge state AI laws (incl. on Commerce Clause grounds), threatens certain broadband funding, names the Colorado AI Act, and carves out child-safety laws — [Axios](https://axios.com/2025/12/11/trump-signs-executive-order-state-ai-laws); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/new-executive-order-targets-state-ai-laws-states-show-no-signs-of-backing-down); [McGuireWoods Consulting](https://mwcllc.com/2026/01/20/executive-order-targets-state-ai-regulation-through-federal-preemption/)
- Legal experts and Gov. DeSantis said an EO cannot preempt state law without Congress — [NPR via WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/national-politics/2025-12-11/trump-is-trying-to-preempt-state-ai-laws-via-an-executive-order-it-may-not-be-legal); [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/trump-executive-order-seeks-to-rein-in-state-ai-laws-drawing-pushback-from-states-lawmakers)
- Democrats called the EO a gift to tech billionaires; Klobuchar said it targets red- and blue-state laws on deepfakes, scams and exploitation; Colorado AG Phil Weiser (D) had said he would sue — [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/trump-executive-order-seeks-to-rein-in-state-ai-laws-drawing-pushback-from-states-lawmakers); [MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2025/12/19/how-trumps-ai-executive-order-will-impact-states)
- White House released a non-binding National Policy Framework for AI on 2026-03-20 urging Congress to replace the state patchwork; Blackburn released a draft "TRUMP AMERICA AI Act" in March; after a July meeting with Trump, Cruz said "he doesn't know that the votes are there" — [WLF](https://www.wlf.org/2026/06/22/wlf-legal-pulse/compromise-necessary-but-not-sufficient-for-ai-preemption/); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/congress-pursues-ai-legislation-what-employers-should-know-about-competing-proposals-advancing)
- A **FRONTIER Act** (late July 2026, rework of a "Great American AI Act") would preempt new state obligations on frontier developers in three subject areas — via [Regulatory Review](https://www.theregreview.org/2026/10/07/knapp-the-hidden-costs-of-ai-preemption/) / search summary (Low-Medium: sponsor and status not confirmed)
- House GOP preemption bill: **H.R. 5388, American Artificial Intelligence Leadership and Uniformity Act** — [congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5388/text) (sponsor/status not checked)
- Lieu told Axios the Democratic AI commission's focus is getting "a federal AI standard across the finish line" — [Axios, 2026-01-08](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/08/democrats-jeffries-ai-trump)

### Inferences
- **Statutory preemption in 2027–28: Low probability (<15%, my estimate).** It failed under unified GOP control; Democratic committee chairs will not advance it. Industry's only path would be a grand bargain (federal safety/kids standard + limited preemption), which the Lieu/Gottheimer wing might entertain but the Markey/Sanders wing and Democratic AGs would fight.
- **Net effect for AI companies:** state law (California SB 53, Colorado AI Act, NY RAISE Act etc.) remains the binding regulatory layer; the EO's litigation and broadband-funding levers become the battleground, plus likely Democratic oversight of the DOJ task force.
- Democratic appropriations power could be used to defund or constrain the AI Litigation Task Force or the broadband-funding conditions (speculative; no source found stating this plan).

## Q3. Antitrust and market-power agenda; AI "circular deals" and bubble/systemic-risk concerns

### Takeaway
The concrete Democratic actions so far are letters, not bills: Warren (often with Blumenthal and Wyden) has targeted (1) "acqui-hire" deals by Nvidia, Meta and Google, notably Nvidia–Groq, which reportedly drew a formal DOJ probe in Sept 2026, and (2) OpenAI's ~$1.4T spending commitments, "circular" financing and any federal "backstop." With Democrats chairing Senate Banking and Judiciary in 2027, these letters can become subpoenas and hearings.

### Cited Findings
- Warren, Wyden and Blumenthal asked FTC/DOJ (Feb 2026) to scrutinize AI talent deals by Nvidia, Meta and Google worth >$36.7B combined, including Nvidia's $20B Groq deal — [CNBC, 2026-02-04](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/04/sen-warren-others-urge-ftc-doj-to-scrutinize-tech-acquihire-deals.html)
- Warren and Blumenthal wrote to Jensen Huang (Mar 2026) saying the Groq deal appeared to evade antitrust review — [Bloomberg, 2026-03-20](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-20/nvidia-s-20-billion-groq-deal-queried-by-warren-blumenthal)

- Warner's July 2026 framework includes an "AI infrastructure, competition and safety" pillar — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/sen-warner-releases-comprehensive-ai-legislative-agenda)

### Inferences
- **Most likely 2027 actions (Medium-High confidence):** Senate Banking (Warren is the senior Democrat there and the presumptive chair, an inference not confirmed by sources this session) hearings and document demands on OpenAI's financing, vendor financing (Nvidia–OpenAI, OpenAI–Oracle/AMD), and off-balance-sheet/SPV data-center debt; Judiciary antitrust subcommittee work on acqui-hires and Microsoft/OpenAI, Google and Nvidia market power. ## Q4. Statements from likely leaders since mid-2025; what Democrats say they will investigate first

### Takeaway
Post-election rhetoric is aggressive but so far rhetorical: Markey promised a flood of subpoenas, Casar wants AI CEOs under oath, and the presumptive Oversight chair Robert Garcia told Republicans to "lawyer up." ## Q6. Precedent: the 2019–2020 Democratic House and Big Tech

### Takeaway
The House Judiciary antitrust subcommittee's 16-month investigation (CEO testimony July 2020; 450-page report Oct 6, 2020) found monopoly power at Amazon, Apple, Facebook and Google and recommended structural separations, but **produced no enacted law**. Its main legacy was intellectual support for later DOJ/FTC/state suits. Markets shrugged: the only data point found shows the stocks mostly **rose** the next day (Amazon +3%+; Facebook the exception).

### Cited Findings
- Majority staff report released Oct 6, 2020 after a 16-month investigation; found the four firms enjoy monopoly power; recommended they not both control and compete in adjacent markets, plus structural separations — [House Judiciary Democrats](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/judiciary-antitrust-subcommittee-investigation-reveals-digital-economy-highly-concentrated-impacted-by-monopoly-power); [Insurance Journal/AP](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2020/10/07/585632.htm)
- Called the most dramatic competition-law overhaul proposal in decades; Jim Jordan called it "radical"; Ken Buck and some Republicans issued a partial companion report; analysts expected it to fail in the GOP Senate — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2020/10/07/house-panel-big-tech-antitrust-panel/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/house-panel-calls-for-sweeping-antitrust-reforms-for-big-tech); [Skadden on Buck report](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2021/01/2021-insights/buck_report_companion_report.pdf)
- Market reaction: "investors were largely unmoved… all of the companies' stocks except Facebook's rising in Wednesday trading, Amazon up over three per cent" — [The Logic](https://thelogic.co/briefing/democratic-wave-could-lead-to-sweeping-big-tech-regulation-analysts/) (single source)
- Companies (Google, Facebook, Amazon) publicly rebutted the report — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/120817/google-facebook-amazon-twitter-slam-us-house-antitrust-report); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/big-tech-responds-house-antitrust-report/)
- Recommendations were not enacted as legislation; findings were later cited by enforcers — per search summary of [Rutgers RICSI](https://www.business.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/documents/ricsi/quasi-october-2020-house-antitrust-report-big-tech.pdf) (Medium)

### Inferences
- **Base rate:** a Democratic House investigation under a hostile Senate/president generates headlines and document disclosures but negligible direct earnings impact; stock reactions were muted. 2027 differs in two ways that raise risk modestly: Democrats now hold *both* chambers (Senate subpoenas too), and several AI bills have Republican co-leads (Hawley, Grassley), unlike 2020 antitrust where Republicans split.
- Investigations' longer-tail effect (feeding enforcement cases 2020–2024) suggests the real risk channel is discovery of internal documents that later fuel litigation (copyright suits, child-safety torts, state AG actions).
