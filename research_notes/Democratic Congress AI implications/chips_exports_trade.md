## Q1. How did Democrats react to the 2025–2026 chip-export decisions, and what happened to the bills (GAIN AI, Chip Security Act, NDAA)?

### Takeaway
Democrats have opposed every major loosening: the H20 and MI308 15% revenue share, the H200 approval with its 25% Section 232 levy, and the Gulf (UAE/Saudi) deals. The leaders are Warren, Schumer, Meeks, Wyden and Neal, plus the intelligence and foreign-affairs Democrats. The hawkish bills, however, have repeatedly failed to become law: the GAIN AI Act passed the Senate inside the FY2026 NDAA and was dropped in conference, and the FY2027 NDAA reportedly drops GAIN AI, the Chip Security Act and the AI OVERWATCH Act as well. **No new statutory export-control constraint on Nvidia or AMD China sales is ENACTED as of 2026-11-14.**

### Inferences
- The likeliest vehicle for binding chip-export legislation in the 119th→120th Congress transition is the **FY2028 NDAA (calendar 2027)**. A Democratic Senate that already passed GAIN AI 77–20 under Republican control, plus a Democratic House that controls conference, makes GAIN-style or Chip-Security-style text more likely to survive conference than in 2025–26. It still needs the President's signature, and NDAAs are rarely vetoed over one provision, so the NDAA is the main route around a veto. (Medium-confidence inference)
- The **Section 232 "export tax" theory** is the sharpest Democratic weapon. If litigated or written into law, it would threaten the 25% H200 levy and with it the legal basis for the current "pay to export" model. Losing the levy would not by itself ban sales; it would push the decision back to BIS licensing without a revenue share.

## Q2. Is export-control hawkishness bipartisan, and what can Congress actually force versus what stays with the executive (BIS)?

### Takeaway
Yes. China-chip hawkishness is clearly bipartisan, and the Republican Select Committee chair Moolenaar is among the loudest critics of the H200 decision. Democrats taking over will intensify oversight and add the Section 232/tariff-legality angle. Statute (ECRA) still gives Commerce/BIS licensing discretion, though, so Congress can force outcomes only by passing law over or with the President: NDAA riders, appropriations limits, or a GAIN/Chip-Security-type mandate. Oversight (subpoenas, hearings, IG referrals, records demands) is what a Democratic majority can do unilaterally.

### Inferences
- **What Congress can force:**
  - Statutory mandates such as GAIN-style priority rules or chip location verification, via the NDAA.
  - Appropriations riders barring Commerce from using funds to license certain chips or to collect a revenue share.
  - Subpoenas, hearings and IG referrals, and possibly defunding or blocking the 232 levy mechanism.
- **What stays executive:** BIS license approvals and denials, entity listings, the content of performance-threshold rules, and Section 232 proclamations. A President can veto standalone bills, and Democrats lack two-thirds in both chambers. (Medium)
- **Investor read:** Restrictive China export policy is the bipartisan *default*; the Trump administration's loosening is the outlier. A Democratic Congress raises the odds that China upside for Nvidia and AMD (H200, MI308, MI325) gets clawed back or frozen. The scandal around Nvidia's former CEO (Q5) makes Republican defense of loosening politically harder. (Medium)

## Q3. Tariffs: Section 232 semiconductor tariffs, the IEEPA Supreme Court ruling, Democratic positions, and whether a Democratic House could constrain them

### Takeaway
**The IEEPA tariffs are dead.** In *Learning Resources v. Trump* (2026-02-20, 6–3) the Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. The Section 122 replacement tariffs reportedly expired 2026-07-24. **Section 232 tariffs, including the narrow 25% advanced-chip tariff, were untouched.** Democratic leaders promise early votes to cut tariffs, but their first targets are Canada, Section 122 and 338, and the forced-labor Section 301 duties, not Section 232. They lack veto-proof margins, so semiconductor 232 tariffs are more likely to face oversight and the "export tax" challenge than repeal.

### Cited Findings
- **ENACTED (ruling):** On 2026-02-20 the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* that IEEPA gives no tariff authority, citing Congress's constitutional taxing power. The ruling does not affect Section 232 or 301 tariffs, and refunds were left to the lower courts. — [Skadden, Feb 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/02/the-supreme-court-ends-ieepa-tariffs); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-supreme-court-issues-international-emergency-economic-powers-act-tariff-decision); [Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/blog/supreme-court-trump-tariffs-ruling/) (High)
- **Section 122 replacement:** a temporary global tariff took effect about 2026-02-24 and expired 2026-07-24. — [ABA, Fall 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/) (Medium: one source)
- **Section 232 semiconductors (ENACTED):**
  - Proclamation 11002, 25% on certain advanced computing chips and derivatives, effective 2026-01-15; scope limited to specific HTS subheadings and performance bands; a review of data-center chips was due by 2026-07-01. — [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0209-us-section-232-proclamation-imposes-25-percent-tariff-on-certain-semiconductors); [Sandler Travis](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-232-investigation-semiconductors); [Covington Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/02/a-month-in-semiconductor-policy-section-232-measures-bis-rule-and-taiwan-deal-signal-strategic-push/) (High)

### Inferences
- **Direct tariff exposure for the named stocks:** For Nvidia and AMD, the 25% chip 232 tariff mainly works as the toll on China-bound H200 and MI-series chips, not a broad import cost. Democratic action against it would hit the *China-export channel*, not domestic AI data-center demand. A broader semiconductor 232 (wider HTS coverage) has not been imposed, so a Democratic Congress mostly lowers the tail risk of escalation through oversight and pressure rather than cutting current costs. (Medium)
- Tariff-repeal votes are likely to pass the House but be vetoed. The real constraint comes from NDAA and appropriations riders and from must-pass leverage. (Medium)

## Q4. CHIPS Act: grants converted to equity (Intel stake), renegotiations, and Democratic oversight plans

### Takeaway
The administration turned CHIPS grants into equity: a 9.9% non-voting Intel stake, then stakes in other companies, reportedly 41 in total. It also explored similar terms with Micron, Samsung and TSMC. Democrats criticize this as "state capitalism," but I found **no specific Democratic bill or hearing** aimed at it, and Democrats are not united against equity stakes. A rider would govern future awards and leave signed stakes in place.
