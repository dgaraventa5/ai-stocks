## INCLUDE

### NOVT — Novanta ($5.1B, USD/USD, $56M/day) — strongest name in the screen
The only company here with **audited, disclosed robotics revenue**. FY2025 10-K disaggregation: **Robotics & Automation $318.6M of $980.6M total = 32.5%**, up from $273.5M (FY23) → $288.3M (FY24). Segment gross margin 47.8%.

It owns two scarce franchises: **Celera Motion** (frameless motors, optical + inductive encoders, servo drives — Omni+, IncOder CORE, Everest Safe) and **ATI Industrial Automation** (acquired 2021, $172M upfront) — the merchant leader in **six-axis force/torque sensors**, which McKinsey names as one of the two highest-risk humanoid component clusters, supplied by "a limited number of vendors, including ATI Industrial Automation and OnRobot." OnRobot is private, so **ATI is the listed way to own that bottleneck**.

2026: $50M/3-yr warehouse-automation design win with "the world's largest e-commerce player" deploying from 2026, crescendo in '27–'28; management sizes physical AI (warehouse + humanoid) as a **~$1B incremental TAM by 2030** against a $1.0B revenue base; GenAI-related applications ~15% of revenue growing ~20%.

**Red flags:** 100x trailing / ~34–38x forward P/E; organic growth only 3% in Q1 2026 (reported 10% is M&A-assisted) — bookings +35% segment / +50% R&A have **not yet converted to revenue**; medical is 53% of end-market mix; no named humanoid customer; management explicitly says humanoid use cases "need to get proven still." **Reports Aug 5.**

### TKR — Timken ($9.6B, $105M/day)
**The only scaled US-listed owner of both strain-wave (Cone Drive) and cycloidal (Spinea) gearing** — the two humanoid joint architectures — plus Rollon linear/7th-axis, CGI precision gearing, and thin-section bearings. The SEC search independently confirms it: Timken is the sole operating company using "cycloidal" in any 2026 filing.

May 2026 Investor Day has a **dedicated humanoid slide** claiming participation across **25–30% of a humanoid's bill of materials**, "designs and prototypes with several U.S. companies," and **"Top 2 — Strain Wave Drives, North America."** A robotics customer example shows 50x lifetime-value expansion — the highest of any vertical in the deck. Second leg is genuine: bearings/couplings for data centers and semis, plus 50Hz→60Hz turbine conversion to unlock stranded power.

Q1 2026: revenue $1.23B (+8%); **Industrial Motion $425.1M (+12%), a record**; Engineered Bearings $806.2M (+6%) but margin fell to 19.7% from 20.9%. FY2025 segments: Engineered Bearings $3,018.1M / Industrial Motion $1,563.7M (34.1%) / total $4,581.8M.

**Red flags:** robotics ~3–6% (estimate — Timken discloses no figure); **the 25–30% BOM number is content opportunity, not revenue**; management's own 2028 plan is only ~4% revenue CAGR, so a humanoid ramp is *above* guidance; 66% is cyclical bearings; China "still a little bit soft"; stock +89% over 1yr. Cheapest of the include set at ~2.1x sales / ~22.7x forward. **Reports Aug 4.**

### RRX — Regal Rexnord ($13.7B, $200M/day) — but the thesis is not humanoids
The only motion name where "humanoid" appears in an SEC-filed document. From the **DEF 14A filed 2026-03-18**, verbatim: *"humanoid robot solutions, which can control motion in a majority of a robot's joints."* It integrates Kollmorgen frameless servos, Thomson linear motion, micro gearing and brakes/clutches — a full joint stack few US suppliers can match. JPMorgan (Tomohiko Sano): >$30M annual humanoid-related business already secured within a ~$200M pipeline spanning humanoid, cobot and surgical robotics.

**But be honest about the numbers.** CEO Louis Pinkham, Q1 2026 call: *"Humanoid in the quarter, we only saw a little bit over $1 million of orders… last year, we saw $40 million of orders."* Management did not clarify whether the $40M was one-time. Humanoid is ~0.5% of $6.0B revenue.

**The real 2026 AI angle is data-center power**: $180M switchgear in 2026 → ~$900M projected 2027 including ePOD, at 20%+ EBITDA margins. Kerrisdale's model: $130M (2025) → $238M (2026) → $970M (2027). Q1 AMC orders +34% (+28% ex-data-center); aero/defense +76%, medical +53%.

**Correction to flag:** the Kerrisdale report is a **LONG thesis** (June 30, 2026), arguing ~11.5x 2027 EBITDA and 100%+ upside (an earlier read of it as a short was wrong).

**Red flags:** net debt/EBITDA **3.31x**; **$12.3M net insider selling over 90 days including CEO and CFO**; PES segment −10.3% organic; humanoid orders collapsed YoY. Its ePOD/switchgear business **overlaps ETN, VRT and POWL already on the watchlist** — check the same exposure isn't doubled. **Reports Aug 5.** (The IPS leadership change checked out as routine planned succession, not a red flag.)

### ROK — Rockwell Automation ($53.4B) — include, but valuation-gated
Only name in its batch where robotics is a resourced product line: OTTO/Clearpath AMRs (~1–2% of revenue, est.), NVIDIA Isaac integration, Kinetix servo drives/motors. Q2 FY2026 sales $2,239M (+12%). **But the 2026 inflection is AI-datacenter and semiconductor construction, not robots** — data center "more than doubling" though management sizes it as only "low single digits" of sales. At ~35x forward, within 4% of its high. The Hannover humanoid was a partner's demo at the AWS booth, not a Rockwell product. **Reports Aug 4.**

## Structural conclusions

1. **There is no US/EU-listed pure-play harmonic/strain-wave gear maker.** The space is Japanese (Harmonic Drive 6324.T — its OTC line HSYDF trades ~$140–200k/day, unusable in size) and Chinese (Leaderdrive, now 60% SKF). **All Western listed exposure sits inside Timken (Cone Drive + Spinea).**
2. **No US/EU-listed roller-screw or ballscrew pure play either.** Ewellix → Schaeffler, Thomson → RRX, Rollon → TKR, Exlar → CW, Haydon Kerk → AME. Don't hunt for one; make sure RRX/TKR coverage prices the content.
3. **The best encoder assets are private** — Heidenhain, Baumer, Balluff, Faulhaber, maxon. Listed routes are Novanta, Ralliant, Melexis and Renishaw.
4. **Actuators are 30–50% of humanoid BOM** (20–40 per robot, $40–60k BOM per Morgan Stanley via secondary sources — source quality mixed, treat as directional).
5. **Adjacent finds outside this bucket, both with audited 10-K humanoid language:** **ALGM** Allegro MicroSystems ($7.7B) — magnetic position/angle sensors and motor drivers, names humanoid robots *and* AI-datacenter cooling; **MP** MP Materials ($7.4B) — NdPr magnets, names "humanoid robots and other physical AI."
